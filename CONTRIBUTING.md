# Contributing to Unpeeragogy

All contributions are welcome. This is not an academic project — it's a **structured empirical feedback layer** for examining Peeragogy in practice.

The best way to contribute is to **share what happened when you tried Peeragogy in a real group**. Not what you think about the theory — what happened.

## 📖 Research Protocol

Before contributing, you may want to read the full research protocol at:

**[/protocol/](https://unpeeragogy.pyragogy.org/protocol/)**

It explains:
- How evidence is collected and classified
- What the project can and cannot claim
- How interpretations are revised
- How bias is acknowledged and mitigated
- The role of counter-evidence and negative cases

## 💬 How to contribute evidence

### Share Your Story (for everyone)

If you tried something described in the Handbook — or something that looks like a Peeragogy pattern — and something happened (anything), open a discussion using the **📖 Share Your Story** template:

[https://github.com/pyragogy/UnPeeragogy/discussions/new?category=field-reports](https://github.com/pyragogy/UnPeeragogy/discussions/new?category=field-reports)

The form asks:
1. What group? What context?
2. What were you trying to do?
3. What did you actually do?
4. What happened?
5. What surprised you?
6. What do you think it meant? (Separate from the facts — both are valuable)
7. What might we be getting wrong?

You don't need to know about tension_index, failure vectors, or any of the framework's vocabulary. Just tell the story. We'll figure out the rest.

### Structural Analysis (for people inside the framework)

If you're already familiar with the corpus and want to propose a specific revision, use the **🔍 Structural Analysis** template. This requires:
- A clear position (Confirms / Extends / Contradicts / Question)
- Provenance — where your analysis comes from (specific incidents, observations)
- Optional counter-evidence — what would count against your proposal

**Final editorial decisions rest with the project.** Contributions are evaluated on evidentiary grounds, not by authority or popularity. This prevents the framework from being captured by any single perspective — including the project's own.

## 🔄 How contributions are processed

1. **Logging** — Your field report is recorded in the corpus with evidence status "Observed"
2. **Context matching** — It's linked to the relevant pattern(s)
3. **Initial interpretation** — The project examines the incident and proposes a provisional reading
4. **Tension assessment** — If the incident diverges from what the pattern predicts, the tension_index may be adjusted (always with documented rationale)
5. **Possible revision** — If your report provides counter-evidence to a previous interpretation, the affected entry is reviewed and may be updated. The old interpretation remains in the commit history.

Every interpretation retains a **provenance trail** — it's linked back to the specific incident(s) that generated it.

## 📋 What we're looking for

- **Incidents** — specific things that happened (not general opinions)
- **Context** — group size, purpose, duration, commitment level, tools used
- **Surprises** — what didn't go as expected
- **Counter-evidence** — observations that challenge what we currently think
- **Negative cases** — times when a pattern worked well (equally valuable)

## ❌ What this isn't

UnPeeragogy is not:
- A definitive evaluation of Peeragogy's validity
- A statistical study of Peeragogy outcomes
- A replacement for the Handbook
- A platform for debating theory in the abstract

It's a **structured forum for practitioner experience to feed back into theoretical understanding**. If you've done Peeragogy — not read about it, not discussed it — your experience matters here.
