"Open Research Questions." "Research method and goals." "Segmenting and grouping."

A page about how to research peeragogy. The methodology. The open questions.

**Why it matters:** Researching peeragogy is a meta-activity: you use peeragogy to study peeragogy. A recursive loop that produces interesting texts and few results.

The theory is rich. The methodology is rigorous. The hypotheses are interesting. But the page reads as a research agenda — a list of things to investigate. Most of which haven't been investigated. The research questions are open. They stay open.

**What to do instead:** A research agenda is useful if someone pursues it. If it just sits in the Handbook, it's a list of good intentions. Researching peeragogy should be the group's activity, not a section in a book. If the group isn't researching, the research section is aspirational. And aspirations aren't findings.

> **Perturbator:** *"Open research questions. Open. Like a door that no one walks through. Researching peeragogy is a fascinating topic. It's also a way to avoid practicing it. While you research how peeragogy works, you're not doing peeragogy. You're doing meta-peeragogy. There's a difference."*
