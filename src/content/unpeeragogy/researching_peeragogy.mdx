### The Assumption

Researching peeragogy is a meta-activity that produces few results, with research questions remaining open and unaddressed, positioned as avoiding actual practice.

### Operational Reality

The pattern dismisses research as avoidance. The evidence suggests the opposite: the people documenting how things work are not avoiding the work — they are doing the work that makes the work possible.

GitLab's stewardship documentation is research — explicit documentation of governance boundaries that reduces friction. LWN.net's kernel summaries are research — independent third-party documentation that creates shared awareness. IETF's working group models are research — documented escalation paths that make peer governance work at scale. Debian's DPL bits newsletter is research — community-produced documentation that becomes governance infrastructure.

The meta trap is real when research becomes an end in itself. But dismissing all documentation as avoidance misses the distinction between research that enables action and research that replaces it.

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if the people documenting how things work aren't avoiding the work — they're doing the work that makes the work possible — and research that enables action is different from research that replaces it?"*

### Empirical Evidence

**Resilience Cases (Confirming):**
- [GitLab Stewardship Handbook](https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/) — Explicit documentation of governance boundaries between corporate sponsor and community reduces friction. Governance documentation is research that enables action.
- [LWN.net Kernel Development](https://lwn.net/Kernel/) — Independent third-party summaries of peer production activity create shared awareness and distributed governance capacity. Research as infrastructure documentation enables distributed governance.

**Fracture Cases (Complicating):**
- [IETF Rough Consensus](https://www.ietf.org/about/) — IETF working group rough consensus governance fails without documented escalation paths. Research into governance mechanisms is essential to peer production at scale.
- [Debian DPL Bits Newsletter](https://lists.debian.org/debian-devel-announce/) — Community-produced documentation becomes governance infrastructure. What looks like meta-activity is often the infrastructure that makes practice possible.

### Operational Heuristics

**Positive Boundary:** Research activities are productive when they create shared awareness infrastructure — explicit documentation of governance boundaries and independent summaries enable distributed governance.

**Negative Boundary:** Do not dismiss all research and documentation as meta-activity avoidance — peer governance fails without documented escalation paths; research is the infrastructure that makes practice possible.

### Epistemic Synthesis

The pattern dismisses research as avoidance. The evidence suggests the opposite: GitLab's stewardship documentation is research that enables action. LWN.net's kernel summaries are research that creates shared awareness. IETF's escalation paths are research that makes peer governance work. Debian's bits newsletter is research that becomes governance infrastructure. The meta trap is real only when research replaces action, not when it enables it.
