## Summary

Documentation creates the infrastructure that makes peer work possible. Theory says research is meta-avoidance. Practice shows GitLab's governance docs and LWN's kernel summaries enable distributed coordination at scale.

- **Fenomeno:** Regular leadership summaries in peer-governed projects create agenda-setting power — what gets summarized is what gets attention.
- **Fonte:** [https://lists.debian.org/debian-devel-announce/](https://lists.debian.org/debian-devel-announce/)

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> Research activities are productive when they create shared awareness infrastructure. GitLab's stewardship documentation and LWN.net's kernel summaries show that explicit documentation of governance boundaries and third-party synthesis of distributed activity enable peer governance to function at scale.

### Negative Rule

> Do not dismiss all research and documentation as meta-activity avoidance. The IETF rough consensus model shows that peer governance fails without documented escalation paths, and Debian DPL newsletters demonstrate that regular synthesis creates necessary agenda-setting functions.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern claims documentation substitutes for action — classic meta-trap thinking. Partial verification, but conditional on distinguishing infrastructure from avoidance. GitLab's stewardship boundaries and LWN's synthesis prove shared awareness enables governance, while IETF rough consensus and Debian newsletters show peer systems fail without escalation paths and regular synthesis. The mechanism missing: when documentation becomes the work versus when it replaces the work. Sometimes the people writing about how things work are the only ones making things work.
