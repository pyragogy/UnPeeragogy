"Workscape: a learning environment designed for peer production." "A space where learning happens through collaboration."

**Why it matters:** A workscape is a designed environment. It's the stage where peer learning happens. The problem is that stages don't determine the play. You can build the most beautiful workscape and have it stay empty if there are no actors.

The theory spends energy on designing the environment. The practice says: the environment is secondary. The group is primary. A workscape can facilitate or hinder. It can't create. And the Handbook treats workscape as a solution. It's not. It's a container. And the container doesn't fill itself.

**What to do instead:** Design the workscape around the group's actual behavior. Not around theory. Not around ideals. Watch how the group works. Then build the environment to support that work. If you build the environment before you understand the group, you'll have an empty stage. Beautiful. And useless.

> **Perturbator:** *"You designed a beautiful workscape. Spaces for collaboration, tools for co-creation, rhythms for co-learning. The group still doesn't work. Because the workscape is a stage. A stage doesn't give the actors lines. It gives them a place to perform. If there's no performance, the stage is decoration. Peeragogy has a lot of stage design. Not enough actors who know their lines."*
