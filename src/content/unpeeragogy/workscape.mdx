## Summary

Workscapes have no boundaries because they have no structure. Theory says peer learning happens everywhere — practice shows it happens nowhere without explicit roles. The workspace becomes a map of nothing when nobody knows who decides what.

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern works when combined with explicit role definition and governance boundaries. P2PU learning circles with defined facilitators and GitLab's documented stewardship model show that structured peer collaboration in shared workspaces produces sustainable outcomes over years of operation.

### Negative Rule

> Do not apply this pattern when roles remain completely fluid and no governance structure exists. P2P University's governance challenges show that peer learning communities without hierarchy or external validation frameworks inevitably face credentialing and quality assurance crises that fragment the community.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern claims peer learning ecosystems emerge naturally in boundary-less workspaces. Partial verification — but only when governance exists. P2PU learning circles work because facilitators have defined roles, GitLab's stewardship model works because boundaries are documented. P2P University's governance challenges and the credentialing gaps show what happens without structure. The real mechanism isn't emergence — it's explicit coordination preventing the diffusion of responsibility. A workspace without decisions is just expensive procrastination.
