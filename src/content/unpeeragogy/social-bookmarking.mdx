## Summary

Social bookmarking died because curation without governance becomes noise. Theory promises collective intelligence — practice delivers spam and abandoned tags. The pattern works only when someone decides what stays and what goes.

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern works when explicit governance boundaries and decision-making processes are established before peer participation begins. GitLab's stewardship handbook demonstrates that documented authority structures enable smooth collaboration by clarifying who decides what in contested situations.

### Negative Rule

> Do not apply this pattern at scale without formal governance structures. Mondragon's worker cooperative governance shows that even cooperative organizations require representative democracy and formal councils to coordinate effectively beyond small group sizes.

## Sintesi Obliqua

Peer curation claims to self-organize into useful knowledge structures. Partial verification — it works when explicit boundaries exist. Mondragon's cooperative councils and GitLab's stewardship handbook both show that documented authority prevents coordination breakdown, while flat bookmarking platforms and unmoderated wikis demonstrate how good intentions create unusable archives. The missing piece isn't technical infrastructure but the willingness to exclude. Curation means saying no.
