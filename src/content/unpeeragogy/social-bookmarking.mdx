"Using social bookmarking to create a shared library of resources."

Social bookmarking was a technology from the 2000s. Everyone shared links. It seemed revolutionary.

**Why it matters:** Social bookmarking promised collective curation. A shared library of resources. It delivered a pile of links. No structure. No quality control. No way to distinguish good resources from noise. The technology collapsed under its own weight.

The Handbook mentions social bookmarking as a method. It's still in the text. The technology it describes (Delicious) no longer exists. The pattern persists in the book long after the practice died. The Handbook includes patterns tied to extinct technologies. This is the risk of writing about tools: they age faster than the theory.

**What to do instead:** When you write about technology, date it. "In 2010, social bookmarking was..." Not "social bookmarking is..." If you want timeless advice, write about principles, not platforms. Principles don't die. Platforms do.

> **Perturbator:** *"Social bookmarking. The Handbook says it's useful. It probably was. In 2010. The platform is gone. The practice is gone. But the pattern is still in the Handbook. A ghost pattern. Technology moves fast. Handbooks don't. The Handbook is full of ghosts. Tools that no one uses. Platforms that have disappeared. Update or remove. Leaving ghosts doesn't help anyone."*
