"Cooperating is one of the hardest things a group can do." "Conflict isn't a bug — it's the engine."

The Handbook says cooperation requires shared goals, but the real fuel is friction. Two people who agree on everything don't cooperate. They mirror. Cooperation begins where agreement ends.

**Why it matters:** Groups that avoid conflict never learn to cooperate. They stay in the polite phase, where everyone nods and nothing moves. Conflict isn't a failure of cooperation. It's the moment cooperation justifies its cost. Without friction, you don't need a group. You can agree with yourself.

The theory treats cooperation as harmony. Practice knows it's logistics. Someone carries the agenda. Someone calls out when trust is being weaponized. Someone says "this consensus is fake." Those moments are the only real output of cooperation. Everything else is coffee.

**What to do instead:** Welcome conflict early. The first disagreement is the group's first real test. If the group survives it, it can work. If it smoothes it over, it has postponed a necessary confrontation. Conflict delayed is conflict amplified.

> **Perturbator:** *"You think cooperation means getting along. It doesn't. Cooperation means getting things done despite not getting along. The group that smiles through every disagreement isn't cooperating. It's postponing. Real cooperation starts when someone says 'I disagree' and the group doesn't collapse. That's the first cooperative act. Everything before that is just being nice."*
