A list of references. Texts cited in the Handbook. "An Open Future."

**Why it matters:** A bibliography is the opposite of a resource. It's a list of claims. "These are the books that inspired us." But inspiration isn't a guide. It's a trail. And trails don't tell you which path to take. They tell you which others have taken.

The bibliography is important for scholars. It's nearly useless for practitioners. Practice needs instructions, not references. Patterns, not citations. Knowing which books the authors read doesn't help a group that can't get a decision made. The bibliography is a sign of academic culture. Academic culture values sources over solutions.

**What to do instead:** If you want to help practitioners, add annotations. Not just "read this." Also "read this if you have X problem." A bibliography without context is a list of titles. A list of titles helps no one who doesn't already know what to look for.

> **Perturbator:** *"The bibliography is impressive. Long. Academic. Important. It also doesn't help anyone solve a real problem. A practitioner doesn't need to know what inspired the Handbook. They need to know what to do on Tuesday. The bibliography answers: nothing."*
