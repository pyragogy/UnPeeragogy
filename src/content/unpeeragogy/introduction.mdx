"The Introduction to the Peeragogy Handbook." Where the journey begins.

**Why it matters:** The introduction is the Handbook's thesis statement. "Here's what peeragogy is, why it matters, and how the Handbook is organized." It sets the frame.

The problem is the introduction is too long. It explains the history, the method, the contributors. It prepares the reader for a long journey. But most readers don't need preparation. They need the patterns. The introduction could be two pages. It's ten.

**What to do instead:** An introduction should make the reader want to continue. Not provide a complete background. Background can come later. An introduction is a hook, not a foundation. The Handbook's introduction is a foundation. Most readers stop before reaching the patterns because the foundation is already exhaustive. They feel like they've already read the book.

> **Perturbator:** *"The introduction is a complete summary of the Handbook. It explains everything. Including the patterns you haven't read yet. If the introduction explains the patterns, why read the patterns? The introduction is the Handbook's biggest enemy. It satisfies curiosity before the content begins."*
