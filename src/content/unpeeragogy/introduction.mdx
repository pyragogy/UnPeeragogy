### The Assumption

The introduction serves as the entry point to peeragogy, providing foundational concepts and orienting readers to the collaborative learning approach. It sets expectations for what follows — a journey of collaborative discovery.

### Operational Reality

The Peeragogy Handbook opens with a confession of imperfection, then immediately breaks its own promise. "We are not going to pretend this book is perfect" — but the next 300 pages read like an expert instruction manual, complete with patterns, principles, and prescribed methods. Howard Rheingold writes the foreword. Academic contributors dominate the contributor list. The structure follows textbook convention: theory first, examples second, reflection last.

Not peer learning. Not collaborative discovery. Not the messy, uncertain work of figuring things out together.

The governance evidence tells a different story — one the handbook does not address. When Mozilla restructured in 2020, community governance councils found their authority suddenly circumscribed by corporate decisions made in closed rooms. The elected representatives had transparent records and defined processes, but no power when it mattered. Peer governance works until it meets institutional reality.

GitLab shows that peer learning works when governance boundaries are explicit and protected from external override. KDE demonstrates that transparent, bounded decision-making sustains peer production. Mozilla's Servo layoffs show that peer communities without explicit boundaries are vulnerable to external override.

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if the introduction promises peer learning but delivers a lecture — and introductions had to earn their promises through visible struggle rather than confident declarations?"*

### Empirical Evidence

**Resilience Cases (Confirming):**
- [GitLab Stewardship Handbook](https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/) — Explicit documentation of governance boundaries between corporate sponsor and community reduces friction in open-core projects. Peer learning works when governance boundaries are explicit and protected from external override.
- [KDE Community Council](https://community.kde.org/Community_Council) — Elected peer governance councils with defined authority and transparent records provide stable long-term community governance. Transparent, bounded decision-making sustains peer production.

**Fracture Cases (Complicating):**
- [Mozilla Servo Layoffs (The Register)](https://www.theregister.com/2020/08/11/mozilla_servo_future/) — Community-governed projects housed within larger organizations are vulnerable to restructuring decisions made outside the community's governance scope. Peer communities without explicit boundaries are vulnerable to external override.

### Operational Heuristics

**Positive Boundary:** Peer learning works when governance boundaries are documented explicitly and protected from external override — transparent, delimited decision-making structures sustain peer production.

**Negative Boundary:** Do not assume peer learning can generate sustainable governance organically — peer communities without explicit governance boundaries are vulnerable to external decisions; the most honest introduction begins by confessing what it cannot protect.

### Epistemic Synthesis

The opening gestures of peer learning must differ from those of traditional education — not because the content differs, but because the relationship with authority must be established differently from the first encounter. The handbook's confession of imperfection reads as humility but functions as expertise: "we know enough to know what we don't know." This preserves exactly the teacher-student dynamic that peer learning claims to dissolve. The tension between promise and delivery in introductions — to handbooks and communities alike — might be where actual learning begins.
