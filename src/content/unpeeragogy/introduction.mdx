## Summary

Introductions work when they document governance boundaries, not learning philosophy. The Peeragogy Handbook promises peer collaboration but opens with a lecture — theory says participation, practice delivers instruction. GitLab's stewardship handbook shows what explicit governance documentation actually looks like.

## Evidenze Grounded

### 🔹 Confirming (2)

**Caso 1 — GitLab stewardship handbook**
- **Fenomeno:** Explicit documentation of governance boundaries between corporate sponsor and community reduces friction in open-core projects.
- **Fonte:** [https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/](https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/)

**Caso 2 — KDE Community Council governance**
- **Fenomeno:** Elected peer governance councils with defined authority and transparent records provide stable long-term community governance.
- **Fonte:** [https://community.kde.org/Community_Council](https://community.kde.org/Community_Council)

### 🔸 Complicating (2)

**Caso 1 — Mozilla governance / Servo team layoffs**
- **Fenomeno:** Community-governed projects housed within larger organizations are vulnerable to restructuring decisions made outside the community's governance scope.
- **Fonte:** [https://www.theregister.com/2020/08/11/mozilla_servo_future/](https://www.theregister.com/2020/08/11/mozilla_servo_future/)

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> Peer learning works when governance boundaries are explicitly documented and protected from external override. GitLab's stewardship handbook and KDE's Community Council demonstrate that transparent, bounded peer governance can operate stably within larger institutional contexts.

### Negative Rule

> Do not assume peer learning can bootstrap sustainable governance organically. Mozilla's Servo layoffs (2020) show that peer communities without explicit governance boundaries are vulnerable to external restructuring decisions that can eliminate the community entirely.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern claims peer learning bootstraps sustainable governance through collaborative engagement. Partial verification — it works when governance boundaries are explicitly documented and protected from external override. GitLab's stewardship handbook and KDE's Community Council demonstrate stable peer governance within institutional contexts, while Mozilla's Servo layoffs show how communities without explicit boundaries get eliminated by corporate restructuring decisions. The real mechanism isn't collaborative learning — it's institutional protection of peer decision-making authority. Theory promises emergence. Practice requires boundaries.
