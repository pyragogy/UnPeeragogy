The Peeragogy Handbook opens with a confession: "we will not pretend this book is perfect." Then it spends 300 pages explaining why collaboration is beautiful, just, necessary.

What it doesn't address: what happens when collaboration fails — not as an exception, but as the normal state of a group.

**What the theory doesn't account for**
The theory describes peeragogy as "a flexible framework of techniques for peer learning." Practice, in many contexts, looks different: three people who don't get along, a shared document where only one person writes, and someone who disappears after two weeks with "sorry, I had a problem."

The theory may not have answers for this — because it assumes that everyone wants to collaborate. In reality, collaboration is effortful, costly, and sometimes not worth it. The theory tends to foreground "flexibility" and "scalability" rather than the cost of participation.

> **Perturbator:** *"The handbook tells you how to organize a group of peers. It doesn't tell you that the problem isn't organizing it — it's keeping it together after the third meeting. The theory is beautiful until you have to send the follow-up email to the person who hasn't read the document yet. That email isn't in the patterns. And yet it's the only thing that matters."*

**What the theory foregrounds**
The Introduction invokes Howard Rheingold — the wisdom of crowds, the power of horizontal learning. It quotes a beautiful line: "we live where no one knows the answer and the struggle is to understand the question."

But in practice, the struggle may not be understanding the question. The struggle, sometimes, is that after three weeks half the group hasn't opened the document. That those who did don't know what to write. That no one wants to admit they don't know what to do.

The theory prepares us to talk about struggle, not to navigate it.

**What remains**
The handbook isn't useless. It may be incomplete in a particular way: it gives tools to imagine perfect collaboration, but fewer tools to survive real collaboration. Like a swimming manual that describes the perfect stroke but doesn't mention what happens when water fills your lungs.

Read it. Then look at what remains once the theory meets practice.
