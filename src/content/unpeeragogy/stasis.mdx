"Stasis is a pattern in which the group seems to fall into a state of equilibrium." "There is no will to do things that might move the project in a new direction."

Stasis is the most dangerous pattern. The group stops moving. Everything is stable. Nothing is alive.

**Why it matters:** Stasis feels like peace. It's not. It's death. A group in stasis doesn't produce, doesn't change, doesn't attract new members. It exists. That's all.

The theory says stasis is an equilibrium. Practice says stasis is a failure of energy. The group has no reason to move. It doesn't have a problem to solve. It doesn't have a goal that excites anyone. It has inertia. Comfort. The absence of friction. And without friction, there's no growth. Growth requires friction. Friction requires discomfort. Discomfort requires choosing.

**What to do instead:** Stasis breaks when something external hits the group. A new member who asks uncomfortable questions. A deadline that can't be ignored. A conflict that can't be smoothed over. If the group can't generate internal movement, it needs external stimulus. The alternative is dissolution. And sometimes dissolution is the right choice. Not all groups deserve to survive.

> **Perturbator:** *"Your group is stable. No conflicts. No decisions to make. No problems. It's also producing nothing. Stasis feels like peace. It's not. It's atrophy. A group in stasis is a group that doesn't need to exist. If the group disappeared tomorrow, would anyone notice? If the answer is 'no,' the group is already dead. It just hasn't dissolved yet."*
