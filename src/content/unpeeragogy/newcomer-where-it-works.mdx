## Summary

Welcoming culture breaks without structure. Theory promises that nice meetings create contributors — practice shows they need clear paths with measurable milestones. The difference is binding authority when consensus fails.

## Evidenze Grounded

### 🔹 Confirming (2)

**Caso 1 — Wikipedia Good Article process**
- **Fenomeno:** Peer assessment with transparent criteria and independent reviewers creates sustainable quality tiers in open communities.
- **Fonte:** [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Good_articles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Good_articles)

**Caso 2 — OpenStreetMap Data Working Group resolution**
- **Fenomeno:** Formal dispute resolution bodies with binding authority and published reasoning resolve peer production conflicts that informal consensus cannot.
- **Fonte:** [https://wiki.osmfoundation.org/wiki/Data_Working_Group](https://wiki.osmfoundation.org/wiki/Data_Working_Group)

### 🔸 Complicating (2)

**Caso 1 — IETF working group rough consensus**
- **Fenomeno:** Rough consensus governance only works when backed by hierarchical escalation paths; pure consensus without appeals fails.
- **Fonte:** [https://www.ietf.org/about/](https://www.ietf.org/about/)

**Caso 2 — WebAssembly Community Group governance**
- **Fenomeno:** Choosing lighter governance models for standards development trades authority for speed, creating different failure modes for peer participation.
- **Fonte:** [https://www.w3.org/community/webassembly/](https://www.w3.org/community/webassembly/)

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern works when welcoming culture is combined with transparent assessment criteria and binding authority structures. Wikipedia's Good Article process shows that peer assessment with clear criteria creates sustainable quality tiers, while OpenStreetMap's Data Working Group demonstrates that formal dispute resolution enables long-term contributor retention.

### Negative Rule

> Do not rely solely on welcoming culture for newcomer integration. The IETF evidence shows that consensus-based approaches fail without hierarchical escalation, and Charlotte's experience demonstrates that cultural warmth cannot prevent bystander effects or contribution inequality.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern claims welcoming culture integrates newcomers into peer production communities. Partial verification — it works when paired with transparent assessment criteria and formal escalation mechanisms. Wikipedia's Good Article process and OpenStreetMap's Data Working Group both demonstrate that clear pathways with binding authority create sustainable contributor retention, while IETF's rough consensus approach and Charlotte's welcoming meetings show that informal culture alone produces dropout, not integration. What's missing is the threshold mechanism — when does welcoming culture tip from retention into exclusion through niceness overhead. Structure does the work that culture promises.
