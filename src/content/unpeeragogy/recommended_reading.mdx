## Summary

Reading lists become gatekeeping mechanisms. Theory says knowledge sharing requires preparation — practice shows it kills participation. The moment you assign homework, you've created hierarchy.

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern works when reading emerges from practice and maintains optional status. Wikipedia's Good Article process shows that peer assessment with transparent criteria creates sustainable quality without prerequisite knowledge barriers.

### Negative Rule

> Do not use recommended reading as a community entry requirement. The Peeragogy Handbook incidents show that reading prerequisites create status hierarchies and decision paralysis, transforming peer relationships into teacher-student dynamics.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern claims reading prerequisites enable informed collaboration. Partial verification — when reading stays optional and emerges from practice, not before it. GitLab's stewardship handbook and Wikipedia's Good Article process work because they document boundaries and criteria without blocking entry. Drupal's governance transitions show how even well-intentioned knowledge requirements create decision paralysis and fragment communities. The real mechanism isn't preparation — it's the difference between reading as resource and reading as debt. Quietly, if necessary.
