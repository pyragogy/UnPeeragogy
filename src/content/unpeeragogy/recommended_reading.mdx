### The Assumption

Curated reading lists bootstrap peer learning communities by creating shared intellectual resources and common ground for collaborative knowledge building.

### Operational Reality

Nothing kills peer learning faster than a syllabus. The moment you assign homework, you have created teachers and students.

The handbook incidents show that recommended reading creates guilt, decision paralysis, and preference for action over preparation. Not because reading is bad. Because reading lists establish a hierarchy of knowledge that contradicts the egalitarian premise of peer learning. Someone chose these texts. Someone decided they matter more than other texts. Someone defined what "informed participation" looks like.

GitLab's stewardship handbook works because it documents operational boundaries — not required reading. Wikipedia's Good Article process works through reviewable work, not reading prerequisites. Drupal's governance shows that when reading becomes an entry requirement, it selects for people with time to read rather than people with something to contribute.

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if nothing kills peer learning faster than a syllabus — and the moment you assign homework, you've created teachers and students?"*

### Empirical Evidence

**Resilience Cases (Confirming):**
- [GitLab Stewardship Handbook](https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/) — Explicit documentation of governance boundaries between corporate sponsor and community reduces friction in open-core projects. Operational documentation outperforms curated reading for community building.
- [Wikipedia Good Article Process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Good_articles) — Peer assessment with transparent criteria and independent reviewers creates sustainable quality tiers. Peer learning based on reviewable work outperforms reading-based communities.

**Fracture Cases (Complicating):**
- [Drupal Governance](https://www.drupal.org/governance) — Intentional governance transition from BDFL to community model required years of structural work. Reading-based entry requirements select for time availability, not contribution potential.

### Operational Heuristics

**Positive Boundary:** Reading lists work when reading emerges from practice and remains optional — peer assessment with transparent criteria creates sustainable quality without requiring reading prerequisites.

**Negative Boundary:** Do not use recommended reading as an entry requirement to the community — reading prerequisites create status hierarchies and decision paralysis, transforming peer relationships into teacher-student relationships.

### Epistemic Synthesis

Nothing kills peer learning faster than a syllabus. Recommended reading establishes a hierarchy of knowledge that contradicts the egalitarian premise of peer learning. Someone chose these texts. Someone decided they matter more than others. GitLab works because it documents operational boundaries — not required reading. Wikipedia works through reviewable work, not reading prerequisites.
