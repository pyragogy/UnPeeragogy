"Here are titles that the editors of the Handbook found useful." A list of books and articles.

**Why it matters:** Recommended reading is a list that makes people feel inadequate. "If only I had read these, I would be a better peeragogist." No. Reading the list doesn't make you a peeragogist. Practicing does.

The problem with recommended reading is that it creates guilt. "I should read this." But most people won't. And they'll feel bad about it. Meanwhile, they could be practicing without having read anything. Practice doesn't require reading. It requires doing.

**What to do instead:** If you recommend a reading, say: "read this if you have X problem." Contextualize. Not "read this because it's important." "Read this if your group can't make decisions." That's useful. A list without context is noise.

> **Perturbator:** *"Recommended reading is a list of books that the editors found useful. How many have you read? None? Good. The list exists to make you feel inadequate. Don't play that game. Practice doesn't require a bibliography. It requires action. Read after you act. Not before."*
