## Summary

Governance paralysis has two opposite causes. Theory says add process when authority is unclear — practice shows this only works below 50 people. Above that threshold, equal decision-makers create deadlock, not democracy.

## Evidenze Grounded

### 🔹 Confirming (2)

**Caso 1 — GitLab stewardship handbook**
- **Fenomeno:** Explicit documentation of governance boundaries between corporate sponsor and community reduces friction in open-core projects.
- **Fonte:** [https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/](https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/)

**Caso 2 — OpenStreetMap Data Working Group resolution**
- **Fenomeno:** Formal dispute resolution bodies with binding authority and published reasoning resolve peer production conflicts that informal consensus cannot.
- **Fonte:** [https://wiki.osmfoundation.org/wiki/Data_Working_Group](https://wiki.osmfoundation.org/wiki/Data_Working_Group)

### 🔸 Complicating (2)

**Caso 1 — ConstitutionDAO dissolution**
- **Fenomeno:** DAOs face severe governance scalability problems — coordinating thousands of token holders with equal voting rights produces paralysis, not efficiency.
- **Fonte:** [https://www.wired.com/story/constitutiondao-ethereum/](https://www.wired.com/story/constitutiondao-ethereum/)

**Caso 2 — Linux kernel Reviewed-by tag system**
- **Fenomeno:** Formal peer assessment tags create accountability but also reveal review scarcity — the bottleneck shifts from writing code to getting it reviewed.
- **Fonte:** [https://docs.kernel.org/process/submitting-patches.html](https://docs.kernel.org/process/submitting-patches.html)

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern works when stuckness comes from unclear authority boundaries and the group has fewer than ~50 active participants. GitLab's stewardship handbook and OpenStreetMap's Data Working Group show that explicit governance boundaries resolve coordination paralysis in moderately-sized groups.

### Negative Rule

> Do not apply standard 'add more process' solutions when stuckness comes from too many equal decision-makers. ConstitutionDAO's dissolution shows that formal voting with thousands of equal participants creates paralysis, not resolution.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern claims formal boundaries cure coordination paralysis. Partial verification — but only when the group stays small and the problem is under-governance, not over-governance. GitLab's stewardship handbook and OpenStreetMap's dispute resolution prove that explicit authority works when you have maybe dozens of active contributors. ConstitutionDAO's collapse with thousands of equal voters proves the opposite: too many decision-makers kills decisions entirely. The real mechanism isn't about weak ties or strong ties. It's about whether you're stuck because nobody can decide, or because everybody gets to.
