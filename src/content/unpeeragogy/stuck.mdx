"The group gets stuck. The bottleneck is not an idea but the means to move forward."

**Why it matters:** Getting stuck is the state that precedes quitting. Not always. Sometimes stuck is followed by a breakthrough. More often, stuck is the prelude to dissolution. The group stays stuck until no one cares, and then it dissolves without deciding to.

The Handbook says the bottleneck isn't an idea but the means to move forward. Interesting distinction. It means the group has ideas but not the infrastructure, energy, or coordination to execute. So it stays stuck. Stuck not because it doesn't know what to do. Stuck because it can't do it.

**What to do instead:** When stuck, identify the bottleneck. Is it energy? People? Coordination? Money? Most of the time, it's coordination. The group doesn't know who does what. When stuck, assign. Not decide. Assign. Someone takes responsibility. The stuck dissolves not when everyone agrees but when someone acts.

> **Perturbator:** *"The group is stuck. You know what to do. You don't know how to do it. That's the difference between theory and practice. Theory says 'do X.' Practice says 'X is clear. The path to X is blocked.' The bottleneck isn't knowledge. It's execution. And execution is the hardest part of peeragogy. Because execution requires someone to take responsibility. And no one wants it."*
