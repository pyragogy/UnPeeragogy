### Il Dogma

Cooperation emerges when work is modular (allowing independent progress), when there's shared vision despite tactical disagreements, and when asymmetry in contributions is accepted rather than resented. The theory assumes these conditions produce sustainable collaboration without explicit governance overhead.

### La Realtà

The Kubernetes project ships releases every three months. Over 3,000 contributors across dozens of companies somehow coordinate changes to a codebase that runs half the internet's workloads. No central authority decides what gets merged — yet the system holds together. Not through consensus meetings. Not through democratic voting. Not through benevolent dictatorship.

Through boundaries that everyone can see.

Each Special Interest Group owns specific modules. The steering committee handles conflicts between SIGs, not technical decisions within them. Corporate sponsors fund development but cannot override community governance. When Red Hat or Google wants a feature, they contribute code through the same review process as weekend hobbyists. The asymmetry is visible — some contributors write more code, others review more pull requests, others focus on documentation. But the governance boundaries prevent any single actor from capturing the whole system.

This is cooperation that has survived corporate acquisitions, leadership changes, and explosive growth from dozens to thousands of contributors.

### Perturbatore

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if modularity gets you started, but governance keeps you alive — and the pattern isn't about eliminating hierarchy, but about making power visible enough to resist?"*

### Evidenze di Tenuta

**about.gitlab.com** — Explicit documentation of governance boundaries between corporate sponsor and community reduces friction in open-core projects. GitLab shows that modularity without boundaries is just fragmentation.

**blender.org** — Foundation governance with a board, CTO, and module owners can sustain complex peer production for decades. Blender demonstrates that explicit governance — not just modular work — enables long-term cooperation.

### Evidenze di Faglia

**moodle.org** — Successful educational open-source projects require explicit governance transitions from founder-led to community-led models. Moodle shows that the assumption "modularity enables cooperation" misses the governance transition problem entirely.

**drupal.org** — Intentional governance transition from BDFL to community model required years of structural work, not organic emergence. Drupal confirms that even modular projects hit coordination crises when informal structures meet scaling pressure.

### Euristiche Operative

**Regola Positiva:** Applica questo pattern solo quando combinato con documentazione esplicita di governance dall'inizio. GitLab e Blender dimostrano che confini chiari e processi decisionali definiti rendono sostenibile la cooperazione modulare.

**Regola Negativa:** Non applicare questo pattern a progetti in scaling senza pianificare transizioni di governance. Moodle e Drupal mostrano che anche progetti modulari di successo incontrano crisi di coordinazione quando le strutture informali incontrano la pressione della scala.

Kubernetes e Blender funzionano perché le loro strutture di governance — non solo la modularità del lavoro — prevengono la cattura da parte di attori singoli. Moodle e Drupal mostrano che le crisi di cooperazione non sono causate da mancanza di modularità ma da governance invisibile che non scala. La modularità ti fa partire. La governance ti tiene vivo.
