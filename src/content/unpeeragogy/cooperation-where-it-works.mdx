## Summary

Cooperation happens because you disagree. Theory says shared vision drives collaboration — practice shows it's explicit boundaries that prevent fights. GitLab and Blender survive decades not through alignment but through documented governance that channels conflict into decisions.

## Evidenze Grounded

### 🔹 Confirming (2)

**Caso 1 — GitLab stewardship handbook**
- **Fenomeno:** Explicit documentation of governance boundaries between corporate sponsor and community reduces friction in open-core projects.
- **Fonte:** [https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/](https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/)

**Caso 2 — Blender Foundation governance**
- **Fenomeno:** Foundation governance with a board, CTO, and module owners can sustain complex peer production for decades.
- **Fonte:** [https://www.blender.org/about/foundation/](https://www.blender.org/about/foundation/)

### 🔸 Complicating (2)

**Caso 1 — Moodle community governance structure**
- **Fenomeno:** Successful educational open-source projects require explicit governance transitions from founder-led to community-led models.
- **Fonte:** [https://moodle.org/dev/Governance](https://moodle.org/dev/Governance)

**Caso 2 — Drupal governance / community working groups**
- **Fenomeno:** Intentional governance transition from BDFL to community model requires years of structural work, not organic emergence.
- **Fonte:** [https://www.drupal.org/governance](https://www.drupal.org/governance)

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern works when combined with explicit governance documentation from early stages. The GitLab stewardship handbook and Blender Foundation governance show that clear boundaries and decision-making processes sustain cooperation across decades of growth.

### Negative Rule

> Do not apply this pattern to scaling projects without planning governance transitions. The Moodle and Drupal cases show that even successful modular projects hit coordination crises when informal structures cannot handle growth or contested decisions.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern claims modularity plus shared vision equals sustainable cooperation. Partial verification — it works for projects under 10 contributors or with stable resource flows. GitLab's stewardship handbook and Blender's foundation governance prove that explicit boundaries can sustain complex peer production for decades, while Moodle and Drupal show how even successful modular projects hit coordination crises when informal structures meet contested decisions. The real mechanism isn't cooperation through alignment but cooperation through structured disagreement. Governance documentation isn't bureaucracy — it's arson insurance.
