## Summary

Shared governance without boundaries is just a committee that never adjourns. Theory says distributed authority creates collective intelligence — practice shows it amplifies conflict when decision domains overlap. The mechanism works only when you document who controls what before the fighting starts.

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern works when governance boundaries are explicitly documented and stakeholder domains are clearly separated. GitLab's stewardship handbook shows that transparent processes within defined boundaries reduce friction and enable effective collective decision-making.

### Negative Rule

> Do not apply this pattern when decision domains are undefined or when all stakeholders expect input on all decisions. Wikipedia's 2021 ArbCom case shows that shared governance without boundaries amplifies conflict rather than resolving it through collective intelligence.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern claims that explicit governance boundaries enable effective collective decision-making through distributed authority. Partial verification — but only when stakeholder domains are pre-defined and enforced. GitLab's stewardship handbook demonstrates that transparent processes within bounded domains do reduce friction, while Wikipedia's 2021 ArbCom case shows how undefined decision territories turn shared governance into protracted dispute. What's missing is the enforcement mechanism — documentation alone doesn't prevent domain creep. Boundaries work when they have teeth.
