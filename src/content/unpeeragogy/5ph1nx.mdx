### Claim

Author: David Preston

### Reality

Preston documents his 5PH1NX experiment — students creating collaborative riddles across media platforms — as if the acronym itself weren't already performing the problem. The handbook presents this as assessment innovation, but anyone who has watched students game rubrics knows better. Not theoretical engagement. Not authentic collaboration. Not learning that transfers beyond the assignment's artificial boundaries.

The Wikipedia ArbCom case exposes what happens when governance boundaries blur into endless process. Committee members spent months debating content moderation policies while actual editors abandoned contested articles. The community fractured along ideological lines — some demanding stricter oversight, others defending editorial autonomy. The result wasn't resolution but exhaustion.

Governance without limits becomes governance without function.

> What if the riddle isn't meant to be solved — but to reveal who stops trying first?

### Synthesis

These cases suggest something uncomfortable about collaborative structures. The 5PH1NX project may work precisely because it's bounded by semester timelines and grade requirements — artificial constraints that prevent the endless drift of consensus-seeking. Wikipedia's crisis points to the opposite problem: when everything is negotiable, nothing gets decided.

The tension isn't between structure and freedom, but between useful friction and paralysis. Preston's students produce because they must. Wikipedia's committees deliberate because they can. What remains to be tested is whether peer learning requires the pressure of external limits — or whether it can generate its own.
