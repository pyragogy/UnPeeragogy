### The Assumption

Peer-based assessment can work when it is gamified and embedded in collaborative discovery. The 5PH1NX experiment — students discovering anomalies in a course blog and collaborating across media to solve assessment puzzles — supposedly demonstrates that decentralising evaluative authority produces deeper learning than traditional hierarchical assessment.

### Operational Reality

The 5PH1NX story is well-documented: students found a riddle embedded in a blog post, collaborated across classes, and created multimedia responses. It is a genuine educational experiment and a useful case study in engagement design.

But the letter does more work than the spirit. The acronym — 5tudent Peer Heuristic for 1Nformation Xchange — performs the claim of methodological innovation while the actual innovation remains thin: a one-week puzzle embedded in a course blog, within a school system where the teacher still holds grades and the institution still enforces attendance. The authority never left. It was just temporarily hidden behind a riddle.

What the handbook does not examine: whether the students would have engaged as deeply if the puzzle led not to a grade but to nothing at all. Peer-based assessment that operates inside an institutional grading container tests compliance, not self-directed evaluation.

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if the riddle isn't meant to be solved — but to reveal who stops trying first?"*

### Empirical Evidence

**Resilience Cases (Confirming):**
- [GitLab Stewardship Handbook](https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/) — Explicit documentation of governance boundaries between corporate sponsor and community reduces friction. When evaluation authority is transparent, peer participation aligns with clear decision rights.

**Fracture Cases (Complicating):**
- [Wikipedia Arbitration Committee (2021)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arbitration_Committee_(Wikimedia)) — Shared governance without boundaries amplifies conflict. The ArbCom case shows that decentralised authority without explicit scope definition produces exhaustion, not resilient collective intelligence.

### Operational Heuristics

**Positive Boundary:** Use gamified peer assessment when the institutional authority container is transparent about its boundaries — participants need to know when the "game" ends and the grade begins.

**Negative Boundary:** Do not rely on gamified peer assessment as evidence that decentralised authority replaces hierarchy — the 5PH1NX experiment operated within a grading container whose authority was never actually distributed.

### Epistemic Synthesis

The 5PH1NX experiment is a genuine educational innovation that demonstrates engagement design, but it does not demonstrate the claim it is used to support: that decentralising evaluative authority through collaborative puzzles produces resilient peer assessment. The authority was hidden, not distributed. The evidence does not answer whether peer assessment can function outside an institutional grading container. It identifies this as an open question.
