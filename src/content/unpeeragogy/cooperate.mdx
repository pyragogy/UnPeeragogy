### The Assumption

Cooperation is fundamentally transactional, built on acknowledged dependence rather than shared ideals. Conflict reveals true desires; cooperation shows willingness to compromise. Cooperation emerges from mutual need rather than altruistic motivation — a pragmatic alignment of interests.

### Operational Reality

Most cooperation fails before it starts. Not because people cannot agree on goals — they can. Not because the vision is not clear — it usually is. Because nobody wants to admit they need the other person more than the other person needs them.

Open-source projects collapse when corporate sponsors try to maintain the fiction of equal partnership. The company needs the community's legitimacy. The community needs the company's infrastructure. Both sides know this. Neither will say it out loud. The result: months of diplomatic language around "shared governance" while real decisions happen in channels the community cannot see.

Honest dependence cuts through this. When Automattic explicitly documented that they controlled WordPress.com while the community controlled WordPress.org, the boundaries became workable. The friction stayed — but it became useful friction. GitLab's stewardship handbook demonstrates that clear authority boundaries enable cooperation that does not require pretending power does not exist. Drupal shows that acknowledged dependence without structured decision-making leads to years of paralysis — knowing you need each other is not enough.

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if acknowledging dependence is the easy part — and building the machinery to act on it is where most cooperation dies?"*

### Empirical Evidence

**Resilience Cases (Confirming):**
- [GitLab Stewardship Handbook](https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/) — Explicit documentation of governance boundaries between corporate sponsor and community reduces friction in open-core projects. Clear authority boundaries enable cooperation that does not require pretending power does not exist.

**Fracture Cases (Complicating):**
- [Drupal Governance](https://www.drupal.org/governance) — Intentional governance transition from BDFL to community model required years of structural work, not organic emergence. Acknowledged dependence without structured decision-making leads to years of paralysis — knowing you need each other is not enough.

### Operational Heuristics

**Positive Boundary:** Apply cooperation patterns when governance boundaries are documented explicitly and enforced — clear authority boundaries between sponsor and community enable cooperation without pretense.

**Negative Boundary:** Do not rely on cooperation when governance infrastructure is absent or informal — acknowledged dependence without structured decision-making leads to paralysis, not functioning cooperation.

### Epistemic Synthesis

The evidence suggests cooperation functions more as a recognition system than a harmony system. Communities that survive recognize their asymmetries early — who has what leverage, who needs what, where the real decision points live. The machinery to act on recognized dependence — governance structures, conflict resolution protocols, ways to change rules when power shifts — remains experimental. The distinction is between cooperation as performance and cooperation as practice.
