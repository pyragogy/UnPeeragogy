### Claim

The node presents cooperation as fundamentally transactional, built on acknowledged dependence rather than shared ideals. It states that 'conflict reveals true desires, while cooperation shows willingness to compromise' and frames cooperation as emerging from mutual need rather than altruistic motiv

### Reality

Most cooperation fails before it starts. Not because people can't agree on goals — they can. Not because the vision isn't clear — it usually is. Because nobody wants to admit they need the other person more than the other person needs them.

I've watched open-source projects collapse when corporate sponsors tried to maintain the fiction of equal partnership. The company needed the community's legitimacy. The community needed the company's infrastructure. Both sides knew this. Neither would say it out loud. The result? Months of diplomatic language around "shared governance" while the real decisions happened in Slack channels the community couldn't see.

Honest dependence cuts through this. When Automattic explicitly documented that they controlled WordPress.com while the community controlled WordPress.org, the boundaries became workable. The friction stayed — but it became useful friction.

> What if cooperation isn't about eliminating power imbalances but making them visible enough to work with?

### Synthesis

These cases suggest that cooperation functions as a recognition system, not a harmony system. The communities that survive acknowledge their asymmetries early — who has what leverage, who needs what from whom, where the real decision points live.

What remains untested is whether this kind of explicit dependence can scale beyond small groups. The machinery for acting on acknowledged need — the actual governance structures, the conflict resolution protocols, the ways to change the rules when power shifts — this stays experimental.

The distinction may be between cooperation as performance and cooperation as practice. One performs equality while practicing hierarchy. The other admits the hierarchy and builds systems that can hold it.
