## Summary

Cooperation happens because you disagree. Theory says acknowledge dependence — practice demands governance machinery first. Without documented boundaries, mutual need becomes mutual paralysis.

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern works when explicit governance boundaries are documented and enforced. GitLab's stewardship handbook demonstrates that clear authority boundaries between corporate sponsors and community contributors enable sustainable transactional cooperation in open-core models.

### Negative Rule

> Do not apply this pattern when governance infrastructure is absent or informal. The Drupal transition (2000s-2010s) shows that acknowledged dependence without structured decision-making processes leads to years of governance crisis, not functional cooperation.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern claims acknowledged dependence enables cooperation. Partial verification — but only when explicit governance exists first. GitLab's stewardship handbook proves clear authority boundaries work, while Drupal's decade-long transition shows that recognized interdependence without decision-making structure creates governance crisis, not function. The real mechanism isn't acknowledgment. It's the unglamorous work of building conflict resolution before you need it.
