The "cooperate" page has no content. It's empty — like the Cooperation section it belongs to. The Handbook admits: "this section is rather sparse, with most case studies moved elsewhere."

Cooperating is so hard that the Handbook has nothing to say.

**Why it matters:** You cooperate when you can't do it alone. Cooperation isn't an act of generosity. It's an acknowledgment of dependence. "I need you because my goal is unreachable without you." This is the only solid foundation for cooperation. Everything else — affinity, friendship, shared vision — is superstructure that collapses when dependence is gone.

The theory presents cooperation as an ideal. Practice knows it's a transaction. You do this, I do that. If one doesn't deliver, the other stops. There's nothing magical. It's logistics.

**What to do instead:** Don't start from harmony. Start from conflict. Conflict reveals what everyone actually wants. Cooperation reveals what everyone is willing to give up. The honest question isn't "how do we cooperate better?" It's "what do you need that you can't get alone?" Answer that, and cooperation has a reason to exist.

> **Perturbator:** *"You cooperate because you have no choice. Not out of goodness. If you can do it alone, do it alone. It's faster. Real cooperation begins when you admit that alone you get nowhere. That admission isn't an ideal. It's a surrender. And surrender is the only honest basis for working together."*
