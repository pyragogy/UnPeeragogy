### Il Dogma

Cooperation is fundamentally transactional, built on acknowledged dependence rather than shared ideals. Conflict reveals true desires; cooperation shows willingness to compromise. The theory frames cooperation as emerging from mutual need rather than altruistic motivation — a pragmatic alignment of interests.

### La Realtà

Most cooperation fails before it starts. Not because people can't agree on goals — they can. Not because the vision isn't clear — it usually is. Because nobody wants to admit they need the other person more than the other person needs them.

I've watched open-source projects collapse when corporate sponsors tried to maintain the fiction of equal partnership. The company needed the community's legitimacy. The community needed the company's infrastructure. Both sides knew this. Neither would say it out loud. The result? Months of diplomatic language around "shared governance" while the real decisions happened in Slack channels the community couldn't see.

Honest dependence cuts through this. When Automattic explicitly documented that they controlled WordPress.com while the community controlled WordPress.org, the boundaries became workable. The friction stayed — but it became useful friction.

### Perturbatore

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if acknowledging dependence is the easy part — and building the machinery to act on it is where most cooperation dies?"*

### Evidenze di Tenuta

**about.gitlab.com** — Explicit documentation of governance boundaries between corporate sponsor and community reduces friction in open-core projects. GitLab demonstrates that clear authority boundaries enable cooperation that doesn't require pretending power doesn't exist.

### Evidenze di Faglia

**drupal.org** — Intentional governance transition from BDFL to community model required years of structural work, not organic emergence. Drupal shows that acknowledged dependence without structured decision-making leads to years of paralysis — knowing you need each other isn't enough.

### Euristiche Operative

**Regola Positiva:** Applica questo pattern quando i confini di governance sono documentati esplicitamente e enforceati. GitLab dimostra che confini di autorità chiari tra sponsor e comunità permettono cooperazione senza finzione.

**Regola Negativa:** Non applicare questo pattern quando l'infrastruttura di governance è assente o informale. La transizione Drupal mostra che la dipendenza riconosciuta senza processi decisionali strutturati porta ad anni di paralisi — non a cooperazione funzionante.

**Stato:** PROVVISORIO

L'evidenza suggerisce che la cooperazione funzioni più come un sistema di riconoscimento che come un sistema di armonia. Le comunità che sopravvivono riconoscono le loro asimmetrie presto — chi ha quale leva, chi ha bisogno di cosa, dove vivono i veri punti decisionali. La macchina per agire sulla dipendenza riconosciuta — strutture di governance, protocolli di risoluzione conflitti, modi per cambiare le regole quando il potere si sposta — questo resta sperimentale. La distinzione è tra cooperazione come performance e cooperazione come pratica.
