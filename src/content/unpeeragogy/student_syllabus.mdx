"A possible syllabus for a student course on peeragogy." A reading list and schedule.

**Why it matters:** A syllabus is a promise of structure. It says: here's what we'll learn, in what order, and how you'll be evaluated. The student syllabus in the Handbook is a syllabus for a course that may or may not have been taught.

A syllabus without students is a reading list. The Handbook has a syllabus. It doesn't have student feedback. It doesn't have course evaluations. It doesn't have learning outcomes data. It's a plan, not a curriculum.

**What to do instead:** If you create a syllabus, include feedback from those who followed it. A syllabus is a living document. It changes with each cohort. The Handbook's syllabus is frozen. It represents version one of a course. It should represent version four, with lessons learned. Instead, it's a fossil.

> **Perturbator:** *"The syllabus assumes a teacher, a classroom, and a semester. Peeragogy questions all three. A peeragogical syllabus is an oxymoron. It says 'here's a plan for something that rejects planning.' The contradiction is fundamental. You can't plan emergence. You can't schedule freedom."*
