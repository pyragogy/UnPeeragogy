## Summary

Someone still decides the curriculum. The student syllabus shifts who holds the pen, not who holds authority. It works when governance boundaries are explicit — otherwise you get knowledge hoarding disguised as peer learning.

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern works when governance boundaries are explicitly documented and structural transitions are intentionally designed. GitLab's stewardship handbook shows that clear documentation of decision-making authority enables successful collaborative learning, while Drupal's multi-year governance evolution demonstrates that peer models succeed through engineering, not accident.

### Negative Rule

> Do not apply this pattern when governance boundaries are unclear or feedback mechanisms are absent. The vault incidents show that undefined authority leads to knowledge-hoarding and decision-stagnation, while Drupal's experience proves that governance transitions cannot be left to organic emergence.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern claims peer co-creation eliminates hierarchical control over learning content. Partial verification — but only when structural scaffolding precedes the gift. GitLab's stewardship handbook and Drupal's multi-year governance transition show that successful peer models require engineering, not emergence, while vault incidents reveal how undefined authority creates knowledge silos. The real mechanism isn't collaborative syllabus design. It's who gets to say no.
