"Once you have a group, you need to co-create the direction." "Collect ideas for what the group should do."

Polling: collecting ideas from the group to decide direction.

**Why it matters:** Polling is the most democratic way to decide nothing. Everyone gives their opinion. No one decides. The result is a list of preferences with no priority. A list is not a direction.

The problem with polling is that it distributes responsibility. If everyone contributed an idea, everyone is responsible. Which means no one is responsible. The direction doesn't emerge from the poll. It emerges from whoever champions an idea and pushes it through. But the champion isn't chosen by the poll. The poll gave the illusion that everyone decides. In reality, someone decides. The poll just hid it.

**What to do instead:** If you want to decide a direction, don't poll. Discuss. Argue. Choose. Polling is appropriate when there are clear options and clear criteria. In the early phase of a group, neither exists. Polling is premature. You're not ready to vote. You're ready to talk.

> **Perturbator:** *"You polled the group. Everyone gave an idea. Now you have a list of 12 ideas. Which one do you pursue? The answer is: none. Because polling gave you a list, not a decision. A decision requires someone to say 'this one.' Polling avoids decisions. It distributes them so no one has to take responsibility."*
