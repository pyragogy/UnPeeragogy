### Il Dogma

Polling for ideas leads to decision-stagnation, distributes responsibility to avoid accountability, and real direction emerges from champions rather than polls.

### La Realtà

A community garden project spent three months polling members about what to plant. Google Forms, ranked choice, discussion threads — every participatory tool in the book. The results were clear: tomatoes, basil, zucchini. Nobody planted anything. The poll had distributed responsibility so evenly that no one felt authorized to buy seeds.

Then one member showed up with tomato starts and planted them without asking. The garden produced tomatoes that year.

The pattern correctly diagnoses the problem: polling without authority produces paralysis. But it misses the counter-case. OpenStreetMap's Data Working Group uses formal voting with binding authority. Wikipedia's Good Article process uses structured peer review with published criteria. These aren't polls — they're decision systems with consequences. The difference isn't the mechanism. The difference is whether someone enforces the result.

### Perturbatore

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if sometimes the crowd actually knows what it wants — it just needs someone to count the votes and enforce the result?"*

### Evidenze di Tenuta

**wiki.osmfoundation.org** — Formal dispute resolution bodies with binding authority and published reasoning resolve peer production conflicts. OpenStreetMap shows that polling with binding authority produces accountable decisions.

**en.wikipedia.org** — Peer assessment with transparent criteria and independent reviewers creates sustainable quality tiers. Wikipedia demonstrates that structured peer review with enforcement mechanisms produces better outcomes than informal champion emergence.

### Evidenze di Faglia

**w3.org** — Choosing lighter governance models trades authority for speed, creating different failure modes. WebAssembly shows that polling without binding mechanisms produces the exact paralysis the pattern diagnoses.

**blog.makerdao.com** — DAO governance mechanisms designed for normal operations can fail catastrophically under stress. MakerDAO's Black Thursday demonstrates that polling without emergency override produces systemic failure.

### Euristiche Operative

**Regola Positiva:** I sondaggi funzionano quando combinati con autorità vincolante e criteri trasparenti. OpenStreetMap e Wikipedia mostrano che i gruppi peer possono prendere decisioni accountable quando hanno strutture decisionali chiare.

**Regola Negativa:** Non applicare questo pattern quando strutture di governance formali con autorità vincolante esistono già. OpenStreetMap mostra che il problema non è il polling — è l'assenza di enforcement.

### Sintesi & Tracciabilità

**Riferimento:** audit-v2 (delta tensione: 1.3 → 1.1138, −0.1862)

**Stato:** PROVVISORIO

Il pattern identifica correttamente la paralisi da polling senza autorità. Ma il contro-caso è istruttivo: OpenStreetMap vota con autorità vincolante, Wikipedia valuta con criteri pubblicati. La differenza non è il meccanismo — è se qualcuno enforce il risultato. Un sondaggio senza conseguenze è un termometro. Non un volante.
