### The Assumption

Polling for ideas leads to decision-stagnation, distributes responsibility to avoid accountability, and real direction emerges from champions rather than polls.

### Operational Reality

A community garden project spent three months polling members about what to plant. Google Forms, ranked choice, discussion threads — every participatory tool available. The results were clear: tomatoes, basil, zucchini. Nobody planted anything. The poll had distributed responsibility so evenly that no one felt authorized to buy seeds.

Then one member showed up with tomato starts and planted them without asking. The garden produced tomatoes that season.

The pattern correctly diagnoses the problem: polling without authority produces paralysis. But it misses the counter-case. OpenStreetMap's Data Working Group uses formal voting with binding authority. Wikipedia's Good Article process uses structured peer review with published criteria. These are not polls — they are decision systems with consequences. The difference is not the mechanism. It is whether someone enforces the result.

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if sometimes the crowd actually knows what it wants — it just needs someone to count the votes and enforce the result?"*

### Empirical Evidence

**Resilience Cases (Confirming):**
- [OpenStreetMap Data Working Group](https://wiki.osmfoundation.org/wiki/Data_Working_Group) — Formal dispute resolution bodies with binding authority and published reasoning resolve peer production conflicts. Polling with binding authority produces accountable decisions.
- [Wikipedia Good Article Process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Good_articles) — Peer assessment with transparent criteria and independent reviewers creates sustainable quality tiers. Structured peer review with enforcement mechanisms produces better outcomes than informal champion emergence.

**Fracture Cases (Complicating):**
- [WebAssembly Community Group](https://www.w3.org/community/webassembly/) — Choosing lighter governance models for standards development trades authority for speed, creating different failure modes. Polling without binding mechanisms produces the exact paralysis the pattern diagnoses.
- [MakerDAO Black Thursday](https://blog.makerdao.com/makerdao-community-emergency-response-to-black-thursday/) — DAO governance mechanisms designed for normal operations can fail catastrophically under stress. Polling without emergency override produces systemic failure.

### Operational Heuristics

**Positive Boundary:** Polling works when combined with binding authority and transparent criteria — structured peer review with enforcement mechanisms produces better outcomes than informal champion emergence.

**Negative Boundary:** Do not apply this pattern when formal governance structures with binding authority already exist — the problem is not polling itself, but the absence of enforcement.

### Epistemic Synthesis

The pattern correctly identifies the problem: polling without authority produces paralysis. But the counter-case is instructive. OpenStreetMap votes with binding authority. Wikipedia evaluates with published criteria. The difference is not the mechanism — it is whether someone enforces the result. A poll without consequences is a thermometer, not a steering wheel.
