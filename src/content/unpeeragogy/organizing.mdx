### Il Dogma

Organizing in peer learning contexts requires balancing structure with flexibility, where organizers provide scaffolding without imposing rigid hierarchies that stifle peer agency.

### La Realtà

A community garden project in Portland spent eight months designing its decision-making process. They wanted flat governance, rotating leadership, consensus on every plot assignment. The design was beautiful. The process was fair. The garden produced exactly nothing in its first season because every planting decision required three meetings.

Not because the organizers lacked commitment. Not because the community resisted structure. Because flat governance optimizes for fairness at the cost of speed. When the first frost date passed while they were still debating tomato varieties, the garden had already voted — with its results.

The groups that organize effectively don't choose between structure and flexibility. They layer them. The P2PU learning circles that worked had defined facilitator roles and institutional partnerships — explicit scaffolding that looked hierarchical but enabled peer learning precisely because the hierarchy was known and bounded.

### Perturbatore

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if organizing without ownership is just herding cats with a philosophy degree — and the real question isn't how flat your governance is, but how fast it can make a decision when the first frost is coming?"*

### Evidenze di Tenuta

**about.gitlab.com** — Explicit documentation of governance boundaries between corporate sponsor and community reduces friction in open-core projects. GitLab shows that documented boundaries between peer coordination and institutional authority increase sustainability.

**p2pu.org** — Peer learning models with defined facilitator roles and institutional partnerships are more sustainable than purely peer-driven learning. P2PU demonstrates that explicit scaffolding enables peer agency rather than constraining it.

### Evidenze di Faglia

**theverge.com** — Coordinated community protest can temporarily disrupt platform operations but has limited lasting impact when the platform holds ownership rights. Reddit's API protest (2023) demonstrates that peer organizing cannot override platform ownership — structure without ownership is theater.

**researchgate.net** — cMOOC vs xMOOC pedagogical governance comparison shows peer coordination has known scalability limits that different pedagogical choices cannot fully resolve. cMOOCs demonstrate that peer organizing has structural ceilings that informal methods cannot surpass.

### Euristiche Operative

**Regola Positiva:** L'organizzazione funziona quando i ruoli di facilitazione sono definiti esplicitamente e le partnership istituzionali forniscono autorità legittima. P2PU e GitLab mostrano che confini di governance documentati tra coordinamento peer e autorità istituzionale aumentano la sostenibilità.

**Regola Negativa:** Non applicare questo pattern quando la proprietà o l'autorità decisionale finale non è chiara. La protesta API di Reddit mostra che l'azione peer coordinata non può superare i diritti di proprietà della piattaforma, e la governance cMOOC mostra che il coordinamento peer ha limiti di scalabilità noti.

**Stato:** PROVVISORIO

L'organizzazione peer efficace non sceglie tra struttura e flessibilità — le sovrappone. I learning circle P2PU che funzionano hanno ruoli di facilitazione definiti e partnership istituzionali, scaffolding esplicito che sembra gerarchico ma permette il peer learning proprio perché la gerarchia è conosciuta e delimitata. La tensione non è tra controllo e libertà, ma tra chiarezza e rendicontabilità. L'organizzazione è un atto politico perché decide chi paga per i costi di coordinamento.
