"Organizing a Learning Context: designing the environment so that learning can happen."

**Why it matters:** Organizing is never neutral. Every choice — platform, schedule, roles — is a decision about who has power. The theory presents organizing as design. Practice knows it's politics.

The problem with organizing is that it's invisible to those who didn't do it. The organizers see the structure as logical. Participants see it as arbitrary. "Why do we use this platform?" "Why this schedule?" "Why these roles?" The answers make sense to the organizers. They seem random to everyone else.

**What to do instead:** Organizing should be transparent and revisable. The group should know the current structure and be able to propose changes. If the structure is invisible, it becomes power. If it's visible, it's infrastructure. The Handbook's organizing section is heavy on design. Light on politics.

> **Perturbator:** *"You organized the context. You chose the platform, the schedule, the roles. Did you ask the group? No. You designed. They used. That's not organizing a learning context. That's building a cage and calling it a classroom. The difference between organizing and controlling is who does it. If the organizer is the group, it's organizing. If it's someone else, it's governance."*
