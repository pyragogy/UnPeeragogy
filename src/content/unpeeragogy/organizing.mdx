## Summary

Learning contexts need someone who can say no. Theory promises peer coordination — practice requires documented authority boundaries. Without explicit governance, informal hierarchies fill the vacuum.

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern works when facilitator roles are explicitly defined and institutional partnerships provide legitimate authority. P2PU learning circles and GitLab's stewardship model show that documented governance boundaries between peer coordination and institutional authority create sustainable organizing structures.

### Negative Rule

> Do not apply this pattern when ownership or final decision authority is unclear. The Reddit API protest shows that coordinated peer action cannot override platform ownership rights, and cMOOC governance shows peer coordination has known scalability limits without institutional backing.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern claims organizing is inherently political because learning contexts require power distribution decisions. Partial verification — it works when authority is documented and partnerships provide legitimacy. P2PU learning circles and GitLab's stewardship handbook show explicit facilitator roles create sustainability, but Reddit's API protest and cMOOC governance failures reveal peer coordination cannot override ownership rights or resolve structural deadlocks. The real mechanism isn't political awareness — it's the friction between peer ideals and institutional reality. Someone always holds the keys.
