## Summary

Collaborative media projects die in the planning phase. Theory promises structured discussions will emerge naturally — practice shows they need explicit production roles. Without someone owning the recording schedule, enthusiasm becomes entropy.

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern works when explicit governance boundaries separate planning from execution roles. GitLab's stewardship handbook demonstrates that clear boundaries between community input and production decisions enable sustained collaborative output in open projects.

### Negative Rule

> Do not apply this pattern when production roles remain undefined or when participants assume consensus will emerge naturally. The Peeragogy Handbook's unrealized podcast plans show that collaborative enthusiasm without explicit governance leads to decision-stagnation and zero output.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern claims structured discussions boost project coherence when people have interesting things to say. Partial verification — GitLab's stewardship handbook shows explicit governance boundaries can separate community input from production decisions in open projects. But Wikipedia's deletion debates reveal how consistent participation creates de facto hierarchies, while ArbCom demonstrates that consensus processes produce their own power structures. The mechanism isn't about having interesting conversations. Someone has to press record.
