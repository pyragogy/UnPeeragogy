### The Assumption

Podcasting is a medium for peer learning where the process of creating the podcast can be as educational as the outcome. Podcasts enable distributed knowledge sharing and can capture emergent insights from collaborative conversations.

### Operational Reality

The Peeragogy Handbook planned 27 podcast episodes. Most were never recorded. The pattern repeats across every collaborative project: podcast episodes proposed, outlined, scheduled, and quietly abandoned. Not because the topics were not interesting. Not because the hosts were not committed. Because planning a podcast is a collaborative act. Recording it is a production act. And production requires someone with authority to say "we are recording now."

GitLab's stewardship handbook documents boundaries that separate community input from production decisions. The handbook's unrealized podcast plans show the cost of not having that separation: everyone agreed the podcast should exist, nobody had the authority to make it exist. Wikipedia's deletion debates show that consensus-based planning without production authority produces paralysis. The ArbCom demonstrates that even transparent communities need decision-making bodies that can say "this happens now."

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if the most educational podcast is the one that actually gets recorded — and planning without execution is just meeting to plan meetings about meetings?"*

### Empirical Evidence

**Resilience Cases (Confirming):**
- [GitLab Stewardship Handbook](https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/) — Explicit documentation of governance boundaries between corporate sponsor and community reduces friction in open-core projects. Clear boundaries between community input and production decisions enable execution.

**Fracture Cases (Complicating):**
- [Wikipedia Deletion Debates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Articles_for_deletion) — Consensus processes produce de facto hierarchies through participation inequality. Consensus-based planning without production authority produces paralysis.
- [Wikipedia Arbitration Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arbitration_Committee_(Wikimedia)) — Permanent records of community conflicts that consensus failed to resolve. Even transparent communities need decision-making bodies that can say "this happens now."

### Operational Heuristics

**Positive Boundary:** Podcast production works when explicit governance boundaries separate planning from production roles — clear boundaries between community input and production decisions enable execution.

**Negative Boundary:** Do not apply this pattern when production roles remain undefined or when participants assume consensus will emerge naturally — the handbook's unrealized podcast plans show that collaborative production without decision-making authority produces no output.

### Epistemic Synthesis

Planning a podcast is a collaborative act. Recording it is a production act. Production requires someone with authority to say "we are recording now." The handbook's unrealized podcast plans show the cost of not having that separation: everyone agreed the podcast should exist, nobody had the authority to make it exist.
