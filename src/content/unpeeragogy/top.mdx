"Top Ten: patterns that stand out from the rest." A list of the most useful patterns.

**Why it matters:** A "top ten" list is a selection. It says "these are the most useful." But the selection reveals the selector's bias. The top ten tells you what the editors found valuable. Not what's universally valuable.

The problem with top lists is that they're treated as definitive. Users focus on the top ten and ignore the rest. The list becomes a filter that hides the diversity of patterns. A pattern that isn't in the top ten is perceived as less important. Maybe it's just more specific.

**What to do instead:** A top ten should come with a warning: "these are the most general patterns. Specific problems require specific patterns. Don't limit yourself to the top ten." The Handbook doesn't give this warning. The top ten is presented as "the best." In reality, it's "the most general."

> **Perturbator:** *"Top ten patterns. A list of the best. According to whom? The editors. Their experience. Their context. Does that match yours? Probably not entirely. A top ten is a shortcut. Shortcuts are useful when you're in a hurry. But they're also omissions. Omissions that might cost you."*

**Reference:** Peeragogy Handbook, "Top Ten Patterns" (Peeragogy in Practice).
