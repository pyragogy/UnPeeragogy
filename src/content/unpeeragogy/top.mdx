## Summary

Canonical lists become governance documents. Theory says they organize knowledge — practice shows they organize power. The friction happens when 'best practices' become enforcement mechanisms.

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern works when selection criteria are made explicit and the list includes clear disclaimers about its scope. GitLab's stewardship handbook (ongoing) shows that transparent governance boundaries prevent the friction that emerges when authority is implicit or contested.

### Negative Rule

> Do not apply this pattern when community members have diverse expertise levels or contested views on priorities. The Node.js/io.js merger (2015) and Wikipedia ArbCom disputes (2021) show that canonical lists become political battlegrounds when stakeholders have different visions for what constitutes 'top' knowledge.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern claims that explicit curation prevents community fragmentation. Partial verification — it works when boundaries are clear and disclaimers are honest. GitLab's stewardship handbook shows transparent governance can defuse authority contests, but Node.js/io.js merger and Wikipedia ArbCom disputes prove that canonical status becomes a political target when stakeholders have competing visions. The real mechanism isn't about organizing information. It's about who gets to decide what counts as legitimate knowledge — quietly, if necessary.
