"Antipatterns: patterns that look good but lead to negative outcomes." "Recognizing antipatterns is the first step to avoiding them."

An antipattern is a practice that seems reasonable but creates damage. "Too many facilitators." "Trying to force participation." "Consensus as a weapon."

**Why it matters:** Antipatterns are the most honest part of the Handbook. They admit that peeragogy can fail. That what looks like a solution can be a trap. But listing antipatterns isn't enough. Because knowing an antipattern doesn't prevent it. It requires detection in real time. And detection is harder than recognition.

The gap between "I know this antipattern" and "I see it happening now" is wide. In the moment, the antipattern feels right. It feels like the right thing to do. That's why it's an antipattern. It looks like a solution until it's not. The Handbook lists them. Practice must learn to spot them in the wild. That's a different skill.

**What to do instead:** Study antipatterns by examining your group's failures, not the Handbook's list. The list gives you categories. Your failures give you data. The most useful antipatterns are the ones your group actually falls into. Not the ones someone else documented.

> **Perturbator:** *"Antipatterns are the mistakes someone else made. They're comfortable. You read them and think 'we would never do that.' Then you do it three months later. Antipatterns are easy to recognize in theory. In practice, they feel like the right move. Until they're not. And by then it's too late."*
