## Summary

Naming dysfunction doesn't fix it. Theory says antipatterns guide prevention — practice shows they just redistribute failure modes. The real work happens after recognition.

## Evidenze Grounded

### 🔹 Confirming (2)

**Caso 1 — GitLab stewardship handbook**
- **Fenomeno:** Explicit documentation of governance boundaries between corporate sponsor and community reduces friction in open-core projects.
- **Fonte:** [https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/](https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/)

**Caso 2 — OpenStreetMap Data Working Group resolution**
- **Fenomeno:** Formal dispute resolution bodies with binding authority and published reasoning resolve peer production conflicts that informal consensus cannot.
- **Fonte:** [https://wiki.osmfoundation.org/wiki/Data_Working_Group](https://wiki.osmfoundation.org/wiki/Data_Working_Group)

### 🔸 Complicating (2)

**Caso 1 — WebAssembly Community Group governance**
- **Fenomeno:** Choosing lighter governance models for standards development trades authority for speed, creating different failure modes for peer participation.
- **Fonte:** [https://www.w3.org/community/webassembly/](https://www.w3.org/community/webassembly/)

**Caso 2 — Wikipedia deletion debates and notability**
- **Fenomeno:** Wikipedia's consensus processes produce de facto hierarchies through participation inequality — those who show up consistently gain disproportionate influence.
- **Fonte:** [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Articles_for_deletion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Articles_for_deletion)

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern works when combined with explicit governance trade-off analysis. GitLab stewardship handbook and OpenStreetMap Data Working Group show that documenting boundaries and formal dispute resolution prevent specific antipatterns while acknowledging the new failure modes they create.

### Negative Rule

> Do not apply antipattern recognition as a silver bullet for governance dysfunction. WebAssembly Community Group shows that lighter governance trades formal authority problems for speed-versus-consensus tensions, while Wikipedia deletion debates show consensus processes create participation inequality hierarchies.

## Sintesi Obliqua

Antipattern documentation promises governance improvement through systematic failure recognition. Partial verification — GitLab's stewardship boundaries and OpenStreetMap's dispute resolution do prevent specific dysfunctions while creating new ones. WebAssembly's lighter governance trades formal authority problems for consensus-speed tensions, while Wikipedia's deletion debates show how process clarity can amplify rather than resolve conflict. The pattern assumes dysfunction is eliminable rather than redistributable. Quietly, the mirror shows you what you already are.
