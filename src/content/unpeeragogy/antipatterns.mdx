### Il Dogma

Antipatterns are recognizable dysfunctional patterns that lead to negative outcomes despite appearing reasonable at first glance. The handbook catalogs specific failure modes — consensus-paralysis, authority-attrition, free-rider problems — with the implicit claim that recognition enables prevention.

### La Realtà

The Peeragogy Handbook lists six antipatterns like items in a field guide to dysfunction. Stuck. Magical Thinking. Stasis. Each gets its own page, its own definition, its own neat boundaries. The assumption runs deeper than the categories — that naming the problem halfway solves it.

But Wikipedia's consensus processes expose the gap between theory and practice. The project formally operates on peer equality, yet participation inequality creates de facto hierarchies. Those who show up consistently gain disproportionate influence — not through declared authority but through sustained presence. The antipattern isn't in the handbook. It emerges from the very solution the handbook would prescribe.

Not documentation. Not governance boundaries. Not formal dispute resolution. The friction lives in the space between what we say we're doing and what actually happens when humans gather around shared work.

### Perturbatore

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if knowing the name of your dysfunction doesn't cure it — it just makes you sound smarter while you repeat it?"*

### Evidenze di Tenuta

**about.gitlab.com** — Explicit documentation of governance boundaries between corporate sponsor and community reduces friction in open-core projects. GitLab shows that naming potential dysfunction and designing boundaries in advance prevents the most common antipatterns.

**wiki.osmfoundation.org** — Formal dispute resolution bodies with binding authority and published reasoning resolve peer production conflicts that informal consensus cannot. OpenStreetMap demonstrates that some antipatterns require formal mechanisms, not just awareness.

### Evidenze di Faglia

**w3.org** — Choosing lighter governance models for standards development trades authority for speed, creating different failure modes. WebAssembly Community Group shows that lighter governance prevents one antipattern (bureaucracy) while creating another (speed versus consensus trade-offs).

**en.wikipedia.org** — Wikipedia's consensus processes produce de facto hierarchies through participation inequality — those who show up consistently gain disproportionate influence. The antipattern is not a bug but an emergent property of how peer production scales.

### Euristiche Operative

**Regola Positiva:** Applica l'analisi degli antipattern solo quando combinata con un esplicito trade-off di governance. GitLab e OpenStreetMap dimostrano che documentare i confini e progettare meccanismi di risoluzione previene le disfunzioni più comuni.

**Regola Negativa:** Non usare il riconoscimento degli antipattern come soluzione magica per disfunzioni di governance. Wikipedia e WebAssembly dimostrano che gli antipattern sono proprietà emergenti inevitabili delle scelte di governance — conoscerne il nome non li previene.

### Sintesi & Tracciabilità

**Riferimento:** audit-v2 (delta tensione: 1.5 → 1.52, +0.02)
**Evidenze esaminate:** 4 (2 confermanti, 2 complicanti)
**Stato:** PROVVISORIO

L'assunzione centrale — che riconoscere gli antipattern ne prevenga la ricorrenza — è strutturalmente contraddetta dall'evidenza. Gli antipattern non sono bug da fixare ma proprietà emergenti inevitabili delle scelte di governance. Ogni scelta di governance previene alcuni antipattern e ne crea altri. Il pattern teorico è utile se riletto come mappa dei trade-off, non come diagnosi.
