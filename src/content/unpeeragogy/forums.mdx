"Choosing the right platform for forum conversations is important." "Forums can be a powerful tool for co-learning."

The forum is peeragogy's virtual square. Where discussions should flourish.

**Why it matters:** A forum is a space. Not a community. Creating a forum doesn't mean creating a community. It means creating a place where a community might meet. If the community isn't there, the forum is an empty square. An empty square is sadder than no square at all.

The problem with forums is that they require maintenance. Moderation, stimulation, responses, reboots. A forum without a moderator dies. A forum with a moderator survives as long as the moderator has energy. Then it dies too. The theory says: "forums are a powerful tool." True. But they're also a powerful time sink. They require more attention than most groups are willing to give.

**What to do instead:** A forum works if there's a core of people posting regularly. If there isn't, it doesn't work. No matter how beautiful the platform. The platform doesn't create conversation. Conversation creates the platform. First people, then the tool. Always.

> **Perturbator:** *"Your forum has 200 members and 3 posts in the last month. That's not a forum. It's a cemetery of discussions. The problem isn't the platform. It's that people don't feel like writing. Or have nothing to say. A forum doesn't animate itself. It needs questions, provocations, conflicts. Without those, the forum is a blank page waiting for someone to fill it. No one will."*
