## Summary

Forums die from governance, not neglect. Theory promises asynchronous co-learning — practice delivers moderation theater over infrastructure you don't control. The real work happens in documenting who decides what before anyone invests time.

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> Forums achieve sustainable community engagement when governance boundaries are explicitly documented before community investment deepens. GitLab's stewardship handbook demonstrates that clear documentation of corporate-community boundaries reduces friction and enables long-term collaboration.

### Negative Rule

> Do not assume moderation and maintenance create sustainable forums when ownership rights are concentrated. The Reddit API protest (2023) shows that community investment without governance rights produces temporary disruption but no lasting change when platforms control infrastructure.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern claims forums enable distributed collaboration through structured conversation. Partial verification — but only when ownership boundaries are explicit upfront. GitLab's stewardship handbook proves corporate-community friction drops when decision rights are documented, while Mastodon's federated governance creates externalities where admin choices affect users with zero voting power, and Reddit's API blackout showed that community investment without governance rights produces noise, not change. The mechanism missing: forums aren't communication tools. They're miniature constitutions.
