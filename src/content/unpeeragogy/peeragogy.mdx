## Summary

Peer learning breaks down when nobody can tell competence from enthusiasm. Apache runs on formal consensus — GitLab documents corporate boundaries. The difference is structure before the work starts.

## Evidenze Grounded

### 🔹 Confirming (2)

**Caso 1 — Apache HTTP Server consensus-based governance**
- **Fenomeno:** Structured consensus with formal voting rules and meritocratic advancement can sustain peer production for decades.
- **Fonte:** [https://www.apache.org/foundation/how-it-works.html](https://www.apache.org/foundation/how-it-works.html)

**Caso 2 — GitLab stewardship handbook**
- **Fenomeno:** Explicit documentation of governance boundaries between corporate sponsor and community reduces friction in open-core projects.
- **Fonte:** [https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/](https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/)

**Caso 2 — cMOOC vs xMOOC pedagogical governance**
- **Fenomeno:** The divide in open education between peer-governed and institution-governed models shows that peer governance has known scalability limits.
- **Fonte:** [https://www.researchgate.net/publication/259999056_Connectivist_MOOCs_and_xMOOCs](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/259999056_Connectivist_MOOCs_and_xMOOCs)

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern works when formal consensus mechanisms and merit-based advancement are established before peer activities begin. Apache HTTP Server's 25+ year consensus-based governance and GitLab's explicit stewardship boundaries show that structured peer production can sustain itself across decades.

### Negative Rule

> Do not apply peeragogy without explicit governance structures when credentialing or quality assurance matters. P2P University governance challenges and the cMOOC vs xMOOC divide show that peer learning communities without formal validation mechanisms inevitably face legitimacy crises.

## Sintesi Obliqua

Peeragogy claims self-organization produces better learning and more resilient work. Partial verification — but only when governance precedes activity. Apache's 25-year consensus model and GitLab's stewardship handbook work because they define authority before disputes arise. P2P University's credentialing struggles and the cMOOC fragmentation show what happens without formal validation mechanisms. The pattern describes the success cases but ignores the selection pressure. Peer production survives when it can afford to lose the people who need hierarchy.
