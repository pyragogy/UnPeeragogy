### Il Dogma

Peeragogy is a flexible, evolving framework for peer learning and peer production. We define it as a collection of techniques for collaborative learning and collaborative work. It is a toolkit for self-organizing educational activities.

### La Realtà

The Apache HTTP Server has run under consensus-based governance for 25+ years. Not because its contributors read the Peeragogy Handbook. Because they built formal consensus mechanisms before they needed them. The Apache Software Foundation's governance model — meritocracy, voting, foundation as legal umbrella — predates most of the peeragogy literature.

Peeragogy describes what Apache does. Apache didn't need the description.

The gap between peeragogy as description and peeragogy as instruction is where the tension lives. The framework explains peer learning beautifully — Patrons, Peers, Catalysts, timing, energy, roles. But groups trying to *do* peer learning rarely fail because they don't understand their roles. They fail because they can't agree on who decides when the roles conflict.

### Perturbatore

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if self-organizing sounds great until someone has to decide who actually knows what they're talking about — and peeragogy is the best explanation of peer learning for people who already know how to do it?"*

### Evidenze di Tenuta

**apache.org** — Apache HTTP Server's 25+ year consensus-based governance demonstrates that structured peer production with formal consensus mechanisms and merit-based advancement succeeds at scale. Apache shows that the pattern works when governance is built before it's needed.

**about.gitlab.com** — Explicit documentation of governance boundaries between corporate sponsor and community reduces friction in open-core projects. GitLab demonstrates that stewardship boundaries protect peer autonomy more effectively than pure self-organization.

### Evidenze di Faglia

**p2pu.org** — Peer learning communities inevitably face quality assurance crises that require hierarchy or external validation. P2PU's governance challenges show that peeragogy without formal validation mechanisms cannot sustain credentialing.

**researchgate.net** — cMOOC vs xMOOC pedagogical governance comparison shows peer coordination has known scalability limits. The cMOOC divide demonstrates that peer learning without formal governance structures has structural ceilings.

### Euristiche Operative

**Regola Positiva:** Peeragogy funziona quando meccanismi di consenso formali e advancement merit-based sono stabiliti prima che le attività peer inizino. Apache e GitLab mostrano che la produzione peer strutturata con confini di governance espliciti e advancement trasparente scala con successo.

**Regola Negativa:** Non applicare peeragogy senza strutture di governance esplicite quando la credentialing o l'assicurazione di qualità sono rilevanti. P2PU e cMOOC mostrano che le comunità peer learning senza meccanismi di validazione formali affrontano crisi di legittimità e ceiling strutturali.

### Sintesi & Tracciabilità

**Riferimento:** audit-v2 (delta tensione: 1.0 → 1.0337, +0.0338)
**Evidenze esaminate:** 4 (2 confermanti, 2 complicanti)
**Stato:** PROVVISORIO

Peeragogy descrive ciò che Apache fa da 25 anni. Apache non aveva bisogno della descrizione. La tensione tra peeragogy come descrizione e peeragogy come istruzione è dove vive la frizione utile. Il framework spiega il peer learning splendidamente — Patroni, Pari, Catalizzatori, timing, energia, ruoli. Ma i gruppi che cercano di *fare* peer learning raramente falliscono perché non capiscono i loro ruoli. Falliscono perché non riescono a concordare su chi decide quando i ruoli sono in conflitto. Peeragogy è la migliore spiegazione del peer learning per persone che sanno già come farlo.
