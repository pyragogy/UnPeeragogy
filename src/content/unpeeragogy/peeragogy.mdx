"Peeragogy is a collection of techniques for peer learning and peer knowledge production." "It provides a set of patterns for guiding one's own learning and for organizing interactions."

The Handbook's first page. It defines peeragogy. It starts with "why another theory?" It asks: "why peeragogy and not pedagogy?"

**Why it matters:** The Handbook spends the first pages explaining why a new word exists. Not what it does. Why it exists. The etymology. The need. The academic gap. By the time it gets to practice, the reader has already absorbed a dozen caveats.

The need to explain itself is peeragogy's original sin. It proves its existence instead of demonstrating its usefulness. A practice that works doesn't explain itself. It's used. Peeragogy explains itself at length. Because it hasn't been widely adopted. And it hasn't been widely adopted because it's hard to explain. A circular trap.

**What to do instead:** The best introduction to peeragogy is an example of a group that works. Not a definition. Definitions are for skeptics. Examples are for practitioners. The Handbook starts with a definition. It should start with a story. A story of a group that used peeragogy and produced something. The definition after the story. Always.

> **Perturbator:** *"'Why peeragogy and not pedagogy?' Because 'peer' sounds nicer than 'teacher'? No, the real question is: 'does it work?' If it works, the name doesn't matter. If it doesn't work, a better name won't save it. Peeragogy spends too much time on the name. Not enough on the results."*

**Reference:** Peeragogy Handbook, "Peeragogy" Introduction.
