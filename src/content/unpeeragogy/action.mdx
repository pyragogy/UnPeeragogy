## Summary

Action without governance is just motion. Theory says individual initiative drives change. Practice shows that sustainable action requires explicit frameworks — roles, policies, transparent decision-making. Without institutional scaffolding, good intentions become abandoned task lists.

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern works when action is embedded within explicit governance frameworks with defined roles and transparent policies. GitLab stewardship handbook and Debian stable release process show that documented institutional structures enable sustained action and reliable outcomes over multi-year timescales.

### Negative Rule

> Do not apply this pattern by simply encouraging individual initiative without institutional scaffolding. The peeragogy action page itself shows that individual action without governance structure produces abandoned task lists and planning-to-procrastination cycles.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern claims that documented governance enables sustained peer action over time. Partial verification — when boundaries are explicit. GitLab's stewardship handbook and Debian's release process prove that transparent institutional structures work, but Stack Exchange's moderator strike and peeragogy's own planning-to-procrastination cycle show how quickly action collapses when governance assumptions break. The real mechanism isn't individual motivation or even collective energy. It's the boring work of defining who decides what counts as done.
