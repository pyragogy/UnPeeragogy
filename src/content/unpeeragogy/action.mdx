The "Peeragogy in Action" page is a list of links. "New Content: Very High-Level Outline (WIP)." "Technology Stack." "Old Introduction."

A page about action — whose content is titles of things to do. Not actions. Reminders.

**Why it matters:** Planning action is the most effective way not to act. As long as you're planning, you're productive. As long as you're updating the to-do list, you're organized. As long as you're discussing the tech stack, you're strategic. None of this is action. It's preparation for action. And infinite preparation is sophisticated procrastination.

The Handbook's action page is a perfect example: it lists things to do that were written years ago. The "Very High-Level Outline of v4" is still WIP. The "Technology Stack" has probably changed. Real action happened elsewhere — in the people who wrote, edited, published. But the page doesn't tell those stories. It tells the story of meetings where it was decided to do things. Not the same thing.

**What to do instead:** Action doesn't get planned. It gets started. Planning only serves to postpone starting. The Handbook has many pages on how to act. Very few on how action actually happened. Maybe because real action is boring: write, edit, publish. There's nothing to plan. There's only doing.

> **Perturbator:** *"Look at your to-do list. How many items are older than a month? If the answer is 'many,' you don't have an action list. You have a regret list. Real action doesn't go on a list. It gets done. And when it's done, the list becomes irrelevant."*
