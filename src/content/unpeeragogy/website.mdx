## Summary

Websites become deserts when they mistake information architecture for interaction design. Theory says peer governance scales through open access — practice shows it fragments without explicit authority boundaries. The operational difference: who decides when collaboration has failed.

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern works when governance boundaries are explicitly documented and institutional partnerships provide scaffolding for peer participation. P2PU learning circles and GitLab's stewardship handbook show that hybrid models with clear authority boundaries sustain peer collaboration over multiple years.

### Negative Rule

> Do not assume pure peer governance scales without institutional scaffolding. The cMOOC vs xMOOC divide shows that peer governance hits credentialing and quality assurance crises that fragment communities when no authority structure exists to resolve disputes.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern claims that distributed websites enable multi-directional peer interaction through public domain licensing and decentralized hosting. Partial verification — but only when institutional scaffolding exists. P2PU learning circles work because facilitator roles are defined and library partnerships provide credentialing pathways, while GitLab's stewardship handbook explicitly documents where corporate authority overrides community consensus. But P2P University's governance crises and the cMOOC fragmentation show that peer networks hit quality assurance walls that no amount of openness resolves. What's missing: the mechanism that decides when peer process has reached its limits. The desert isn't the static site — it's the silence when nobody knows who gets to say no.
