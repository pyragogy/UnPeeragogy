### The Assumption

Websites are static documents that fail to demonstrate peeragogy principles, functioning as repositories rather than communities.

### Operational Reality

Static websites do not demonstrate peeragogy. Neither do websites that pretend hierarchy does not exist.

P2PU's learning circles show that peer learning sustainability comes from defined facilitator roles and institutional partnerships — not from website features. GitLab's stewardship handbook documents governance boundaries that make the website a boundary object, not a community container. P2P University's governance challenges and the cMOOC divide show that peer governance without institutional scaffolding hits credentialing crises regardless of website design.

The pattern assumes the website is the problem. It is not. A static site with clear governance works better than a dynamic site with confused authority. The medium does not determine peer learning — governance does.

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if static websites don't demonstrate peeragogy — and neither do websites that pretend hierarchy doesn't exist — because the medium doesn't determine peer learning, governance does?"*

### Empirical Evidence

**Resilience Cases (Confirming):**
- [P2PU Learning Circles](https://www.p2pu.org/en/learning-circles/) — Peer learning with defined facilitator roles and institutional partnerships is more sustainable than purely peer-driven learning. Governance determines website effectiveness, not the reverse.
- [GitLab Stewardship Handbook](https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/) — Explicit documentation of governance boundaries reduces friction. Governance boundaries make the website a boundary object, not a community container.

**Fracture Cases (Complicating):**
- [P2PU About / Governance Challenges](https://www.p2pu.org/en/about/) — Peer governance without institutional scaffolding hits credentialing crises regardless of website design.
- [cMOOC vs xMOOC Pedagogical Governance](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/259999056_Connectivist_MOOCs_and_xMOOCs) — Peer governance without formal validation has structural ceilings that website design cannot resolve.

### Operational Heuristics

**Positive Boundary:** The website works when governance boundaries are documented explicitly and institutional partnerships provide scaffolding for peer participation — governance, not platform, determines effectiveness.

**Negative Boundary:** Do not assume pure peer governance scales without institutional scaffolding — peer governance without formal validation mechanisms hits structural ceilings that no website design can resolve.

### Epistemic Synthesis

The pattern assumes the website is the problem. It is not. A static site with clear governance works better than a dynamic site with confused authority. P2PU and GitLab show that governance determines website effectiveness — not the reverse. The medium does not determine peer learning. Governance does.
