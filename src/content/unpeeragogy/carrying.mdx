"This pattern helps participants recognize and communicate their stress." "Given enough eyeballs, all bugs are shallow."

The Carrying Capacity pattern is about resilience. Recognizing your limits. Communicating them. Not burning out.

**Why it matters:** Recognizing your limits is the first step toward quitting. Not cynicism. Physiology. The person who says "I can't do this" is usually not asking for help. They're looking for an exit. And the group, which doesn't want pressure, gives it to them. "Sure, take a break." The break becomes permanent.

The theory says: "communicate your stress." But communication doesn't resolve stress. It shifts it. The person communicating stress is asking the group to carry some of it. But the group already has its own stress. It has no extra carrying capacity. A group's resilience is inversely proportional to the number of people declaring fragility. The more people declare fragility, the less resilient the group becomes. Fragility is contagious.

**What to do instead:** Before you tell someone "take a break," ask: "what would need to change so you don't need a break?" Sometimes the honest answer is: "this project is too big for us." There's no pattern that fixes an under-resourced group. Recognizing limits also means knowing when to stop.

> **Perturbator:** *"Is your group resilient, or just tolerant? Resilience shows when someone is under pressure and the group supports them. Tolerance shows when someone says 'I can't do this' and the group says 'ok, stop.' Tolerance is kind. Resilience is expensive. Most groups choose kindness. Then they dissolve."*
