## Summary

Carrying someone means you both sink together. Theory says peer support distributes load — practice shows it just redistributes failure. The real question isn't who carries whom, but whether the structure can handle anyone failing at all.

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern works when formal dispute resolution bodies with binding authority exist as backstops to informal support. The OpenStreetMap Data Working Group shows that published reasoning and binding decisions resolve conflicts that peer consensus cannot handle, making temporary carrying sustainable.

### Negative Rule

> Do not apply this pattern when fragility affects core decision-making or becomes chronic without formal escalation mechanisms. The Linux kernel Code of Conflict shows that peer communities cannot resolve fundamental governance disputes through informal carrying alone — external pressure becomes necessary.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern claims formal dispute resolution makes temporary carrying sustainable by providing escalation when peer consensus breaks down. Partial verification — but only when authority can actually be redistributed. OpenStreetMap's Data Working Group proves that published reasoning resolves what informal support cannot, while Linux's Code of Conflict shows external pressure forces changes that internal deliberation never would. The mechanism missing: how to distinguish between temporary fragility worth carrying and structural weakness that carrying only masks. Quietly carrying someone to the exit.
