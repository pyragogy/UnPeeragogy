### The Assumption

Peer groups can absorb and redistribute the load when some members become fragile, maintaining collective function through mutual support. Recognizing and communicating limits is the first step toward sustainable peer production.

### Operational Reality

The pattern of administrative burnout in large peer production projects is well-documented. Wikipedia editors who spend years mediating disputes, cleaning vandalism, and diplomatically managing personalities eventually face a structural trap: they are not carrying the project's intellectual load but its emotional overhead — the friction that accumulates when thousands of humans try to coordinate without hierarchy.

Every peer production system discovers this eventually. Someone ends up carrying what everyone else will not.

OpenStreetMap's Data Working Group shows that formal dispute resolution bodies with binding authority resolve what informal consensus cannot: someone must carry the authority to make binding decisions. The Linux kernel's Code of Conflict shows that external pressure forces governance changes that peer communities cannot produce through internal deliberation alone. WebAssembly's lighter governance prevents one kind of carrying burden (bureaucracy) while creating another (consensus fatigue).

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if the people who talk most about distributed leadership are the ones least willing to distribute the actual work?"*

### Empirical Evidence

**Resilience Cases (Confirming):**
- [OpenStreetMap Data Working Group](https://wiki.osmfoundation.org/wiki/Data_Working_Group) — Formal dispute resolution bodies with binding authority resolve peer production conflicts that informal consensus cannot. Someone must carry the authority to make binding decisions.

**Fracture Cases (Complicating):**
- [Linux Kernel Code of Conflict](https://docs.kernel.org/process/code-of-conflict.html) — External pressure forces governance changes that peer communities cannot produce through internal deliberation alone. Chronic carrying problems require external escalation mechanisms.
- [WebAssembly Community Group](https://www.w3.org/community/webassembly/) — Lighter governance prevents bureaucratic carrying burden but creates consensus fatigue as a different failure mode.

### Operational Heuristics

**Positive Boundary:** Establish formal dispute resolution bodies as backstop for informal support — published reasoning and binding decisions reduce the emotional load on those who mediate.

**Negative Boundary:** Do not rely on informal mutual support when fragility affects the decision core or becomes chronic — peer communities cannot resolve chronic carrying problems through informal mechanisms alone.

### Epistemic Synthesis

The assumption that groups can absorb and redistribute load through mutual support is contradicted by the evidence. OpenStreetMap shows that formal authority is required to resolve what informal consensus cannot. The Linux kernel shows that external pressure is necessary when internal mechanisms fail. Carrying someone sounds noble until you are the one being carried toward the exit.
