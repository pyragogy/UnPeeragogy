## Summary

Moderation burns out volunteers faster than communities can replace them. Theory says shared responsibility distributes the load — practice shows it just diffuses accountability. The real mechanism isn't participation balance but authority backup.

## Evidenze Grounded

### 🔹 Confirming (2)

**Caso 1 — OpenStreetMap Data Working Group resolution**
- **Fenomeno:** Formal dispute resolution bodies with binding authority and published reasoning resolve peer production conflicts that informal consensus cannot.
- **Fonte:** [https://wiki.osmfoundation.org/wiki/Data_Working_Group](https://wiki.osmfoundation.org/wiki/Data_Working_Group)

**Caso 2 — Wikipedia Good Article process**
- **Fenomeno:** Peer assessment with transparent criteria and independent reviewers creates sustainable quality tiers in open communities.
- **Fonte:** [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Good_articles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Good_articles)

### 🔸 Complicating (2)

**Caso 1 — Rust moderation team resignation**
- **Fenomeno:** Governance crises in peer communities can erupt suddenly when delegated authority is perceived as unsupported by central leadership.
- **Fonte:** [https://blog.rust-lang.org/2021/05/17/Moderation-team-resignation.html](https://blog.rust-lang.org/2021/05/17/Moderation-team-resignation.html)

**Caso 2 — Fediverse / Mastodon moderation governance**
- **Fenomeno:** Decentralized peer governance without shared authority produces inconsistent enforcement and can be exploited by bad actors playing instances against each other.
- **Fonte:** [https://docs.joinmastodon.org/admin/moderation/](https://docs.joinmastodon.org/admin/moderation/)

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern works when formal dispute resolution bodies have binding authority and published reasoning. The OpenStreetMap Data Working Group shows that transparent criteria with independent reviewers resolve conflicts that informal consensus cannot handle, creating sustainable quality control over 15+ years.

### Negative Rule

> Do not apply this pattern when authority delegation lacks formal backing from community leadership. The Rust moderation team resignation (2021) shows that moderators will abandon their roles when they perceive their authority as unsupported, creating sudden governance crises.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern claims that stepping back creates space for others to engage, balancing participation through voluntary restraint. Partial verification — but only when formal structures exist to catch what voluntary moderation misses. OpenStreetMap's Data Working Group and Wikipedia's Good Article process work because they have binding authority and published reasoning, not because moderators practice self-restraint. Rust's moderation team collapse shows what happens when community backing evaporates. The missing piece isn't moderation technique but legitimacy infrastructure. Someone still has to take out the trash.
