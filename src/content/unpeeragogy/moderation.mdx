"Moderation is the method of maintaining the conversation in a productive direction." "Being the moderator is a great way to improve the quality of discussions."

The theory presents moderation as a service role. You moderate to help the group.

**Why it matters:** Moderation is the most unpopular role in any group. You interrupt, redirect, filter. You say "this is off-topic" to someone who's enthusiastic. You say "this discussion is closed" to someone who wants to continue. You make enemies. Every time. Moderation is the willingness to be disliked for the good of the group. Not everyone has it.

The problem with peeragogical groups is that no one wants to moderate. Because moderating means taking responsibility for the conversation. And in a peer group, responsibility is distributed. Which means no one takes it. Discussions drift. Time is wasted. The group feels chaotic. Not because the method doesn't work. Because no one is willing to interrupt.

**What to do instead:** Moderation works if it's rotated. If everyone takes a turn. If it's seen as a service, not a power. But rotation requires training. And training requires time. Without training, moderation stays with the few who are willing to be disliked. And they burn out. Then the group no longer has moderation. Then it dissolves.

> **Perturbator:** *"Being the moderator means getting interrupted, ignored, and challenged. It's not a power trip. It's a sacrifice. If no one in your group wants to moderate, the group doesn't value the conversation enough to protect it. And if the conversation isn't protected, it's noise. Not dialogue."*
