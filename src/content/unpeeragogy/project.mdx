"Peeragogy is also referred to as Paragogy." "The Peeragogy Project was established by a group of students and faculty."

**Why it matters:** A project is a container. It holds people, ideas, energy. But a container doesn't create the content. It holds it. The Peeragogy Project was a container for the Handbook. The Handbook was produced. The container did its job.

The problem with projects is that they can become the activity. "We are a project." "We meet as a project." The container becomes the content. And the project survives by organizing meetings, not by producing. The Peeragogy Project produced a Handbook. Many groups don't produce anything. They just meet. If the container is the content, there's no content. Just meetings.

**What to do instead:** A project is defined by its output. Not by its existence. If the project produces, it's alive. If it meets and plans, it's waiting. The Peeragogy Project produced a Handbook. Then what? If the answer is "nothing," the project served its purpose. It produced. Then it ended. Not all containers need to stay full.

> **Perturbator:** *"The Peeragogy Project was a container. It produced a Handbook. Good. Then the container stayed open. Meetings continued. Discussions continued. But production stopped. The container survived without content. A container without content is just a room where nothing is built."*
