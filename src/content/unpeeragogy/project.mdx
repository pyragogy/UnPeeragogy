## Summary

Peer projects die from governance starvation, not resource scarcity. Theory says collaboration emerges naturally — practice shows it needs explicit decision structures. Without formal mechanisms, productive work devolves into perpetual coordination overhead.

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern works when explicit governance structures are established from project inception. The Apache Software Foundation's consensus-based governance (1999-present) and GitLab's documented stewardship boundaries show that formal decision-making mechanisms enable sustained peer production across decades.

### Negative Rule

> Do not assume collaborative goodwill alone sustains peer projects. The Peeragogy Project's decision-stagnation after handbook completion shows that projects without formal governance structures devolve into social gatherings rather than productive collaborations.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern claims containers provide structure for sustained peer production. Partial verification — but only when governance precedes collaboration, not the reverse. Apache's decades-long consensus protocols and GitLab's documented stewardship boundaries prove formal mechanisms work, while Node.js required institutional negotiation to merge competing factions and Peeragogy stagnated once handbook completion removed clear objectives. The real mechanism isn't the container metaphor but decision-making infrastructure that operates independently of participant enthusiasm. Projects without governance are just expensive coffee shops with version control.
