### Il Dogma

Groups should regularly review their patterns to identify which ones are working and which ones need adjustment, using this reflection to improve their collaborative processes.

### La Realtà

The IETF has been auditing its working group processes for decades. Rough consensus, running code, working group chairs — the patterns are documented, reviewed, refined. Yet the same complaints surface every year: decision paralysis, dominated discussions, chairs with too much power.

Not because the audit process failed. Not because the patterns are wrong. Because auditing without authority produces recommendations without implementation.

OpenStreetMap's Data Working Group doesn't just audit — it resolves. Binding decisions with published reasoning. Fedora's Engineering Steering Committee doesn't just review — it governs. Elected representatives with defined scope and transparent records.

The difference between pattern audit and governance is the authority to implement what the audit discovers. Most peer groups audit beautifully and change nothing.

### Perturbatore

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if auditing without authority is just organized complaining with better documentation — and the audit becomes the thing that replaces the change it was supposed to produce?"*

### Evidenze di Tenuta

**wiki.osmfoundation.org** — Formal dispute resolution bodies with binding authority and published reasoning resolve peer production conflicts. OpenStreetMap shows that audit bodies with enforcement authority produce real change.

**docs.fedoraproject.org** — Elected technical steering committees with defined scope and transparent records provide sustainable peer governance. Fedora FESCo demonstrates that elected bodies with decision-making authority can audit and implement simultaneously.

### Evidenze di Faglia

**ietf.org** — Rough consensus governance only works when backed by hierarchical escalation paths; pure consensus without appeals fails. IETF demonstrates that pattern auditing without implementation authority produces repeated recommendations without change.

**drupal.org** — Intentional governance transition from BDFL to community model required years of structural work. Drupal shows that auditing patterns without implementation authority produces documentation of dysfunction without resolution.

### Euristiche Operative

**Regola Positiva:** L'audit dei pattern funziona quando il corpo di audit ha autorità decisionale vincolante e processi di ragionamento pubblicati. OpenStreetMap e Fedora dimostrano che strutture di autorità formale con trasparenza permettono audit e implementazione simultaneamente.

**Regola Negativa:** Non applicare l'audit dei pattern quando il gruppo di audit manca di autorità per implementare i cambiamenti. IETF mostra che l'audit a puro consenso fallisce senza processi di appello, creando paralisi decisionale invece di miglioramento.

### Sintesi & Tracciabilità

**Stato:** PROVVISORIO

La differenza tra pattern audit e governance è l'autorità di implementare ciò che l'audit scopre. I gruppi peer che funzionano non separano queste funzioni — OpenStreetMap e Fedora mostrano che gli stessi organismi che analizzano i pattern hanno anche il potere di cambiarli. L'IETF mostra il costo di separarle: audit accurati che producono raccomandazioni senza implementazione. L'audit circolare è un rischio di ogni sistema riflessivo: l'osservazione diventa un sostituto dell'azione.
