## Summary

Pattern audits without enforcement power become documentation theater. The theory promises iterative refinement — practice delivers endless meetings about meetings. Authority to implement changes separates functional review from organized procrastination.

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern works when audit bodies have binding decision-making authority and published reasoning processes. The OpenStreetMap Data Working Group and Fedora Engineering Steering Committee demonstrate that formal authority structures with transparent records successfully resolve conflicts that informal auditing cannot address.

### Negative Rule

> Do not apply this pattern when the auditing group lacks authority to implement changes. The IETF rough consensus model shows that pure consensus auditing fails without appeals processes, creating decision paralysis rather than improvement.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern claims systematic review creates adaptive collections. Partial verification — but only when auditors can act on findings. OpenStreetMap's Data Working Group and Fedora's Engineering Committee demonstrate that binding authority plus transparent reasoning resolves what consensus cannot, while IETF working groups show how rough consensus without appeals creates decision paralysis rather than pattern evolution. The mechanism missing from most implementations: the gap between identifying problems and having power to fix them. Auditing becomes circular when reviewers can only recommend.
