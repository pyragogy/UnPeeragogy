"They evaluate the patterns based on their experience at the event." "They identify patterns that work and those that need adjustment."

Pattern audit: checking whether the group's patterns are still working.

**Why it matters:** Auditing patterns is another meta-activity that tends to replace the activity. The group audits its patterns, discovers which ones are broken, and instead of fixing them, audits again. Because auditing is easier than fixing. Fixing requires effort. Auditing requires a meeting.

The pattern audit is useful if it's followed by concrete action. "Pattern X isn't working. We'll replace it with Y by next meeting." Without a decision and a deadline, the audit becomes a report. Reports don't change behavior.

**What to do instead:** Every audit must generate at least one action. Not a recommendation. An action. If the audit generates no actions, it's not an audit. It's a conversation. And the group doesn't need more conversations. It needs decisions.

> **Perturbator:** *"You audited the patterns. Found five that don't work. Now what? If the answer is 'we'll discuss how to fix them at the next meeting,' the audit didn't work. The audit worked when one of the five patterns was replaced by the end of the meeting. Action is the only valid output of an audit. Everything else is conversation pretending to be analysis."*
