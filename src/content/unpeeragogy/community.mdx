The theory says: you're part of a community. And there are other communities out there you're not part of. Maybe you should get to know them.

In reality, the problem isn't knowing other communities. It's that your community doesn't exist.

**Why it matters:** It's called a community, but it's a WhatsApp group with 47 members where the last message was three weeks ago. It's called a community, but it has a forum where the last post is from 2022. It's called a community, but people don't know each other, don't see each other, don't produce together. Calling it a "community" is a narrative fiction. It's a group of strangers sharing a label — not a project, not a bond, not a common practice.

The theory invites you to know other communities. Practice says: find yours first. If it exists.

**The meta-community case:** The Peeragogy Project itself calls itself a community. It has a "Community" page in the Handbook. But the real community — the one that wrote, discussed, produced — has dispersed. The patterns are written. The people are gone. The community became a document that talks about itself. Meta-community. Paper community.

**What to do instead:** A community isn't a group that shares an interest. It's a group that shares a cost. If no one pays — in time, energy, attention — there's no community. There's a label. Build the bond before you name it.

> **Perturbator:** *"Call it a 'community' all you want. If people aren't exchanging anything — favors, critiques, resources — it's not a community. It's an address book. An address book has no pulse. No rhythm. No conflict. Just names you'll never call."*
