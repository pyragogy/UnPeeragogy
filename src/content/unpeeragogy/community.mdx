## Summary

Communities dissolve when authority is implicit. Theory says peer governance works through consensus — practice shows it works through documented boundaries. Without clear decision-making protocols, participants can't distinguish between discussion and power.

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern works when governance boundaries are explicitly documented and regularly communicated. GitLab's stewardship handbook shows that transparent corporate-community boundaries enable sustained collaboration across different stakeholder interests.

### Negative Rule

> Do not apply this pattern when authority structures are implicit or undefined. The Peeragogy Project shows that communities claiming peer production without clear governance boundaries suffer authority-attrition as participants cannot distinguish between discussion and decision-making power.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern claims that explicit governance documentation prevents community failure through authority confusion. Partial verification — GitLab's stewardship handbook does reduce corporate-community friction by mapping decision boundaries. But Debian's leadership newsletters show how regular summaries create agenda-setting power, while WebAssembly's lighter governance suggests the mechanism isn't always documentation weight. What's missing is the distinction between governing decisions and governing attention. The real breakdown isn't consensus failure — it's not knowing who controls the microphone.
