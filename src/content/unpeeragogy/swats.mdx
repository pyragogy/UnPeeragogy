"SWATS: Some Wonderful Architecture for Tutoring Systems." "A peer-to-peer architecture for learning."

A technology platform for peer learning. A specific architectural proposal.

**Why it matters:** SWATS is a technology solution. Technology is the easiest part of peeragogy. The hardest part is human coordination. SWATS solves the technology part and assumes the human part will follow. It rarely does.

The Handbook gives space to technology. It has a whole section. But the technology section is the least used of the entire Handbook. Why? Because peeragogy's problems aren't technological. They're relational, organizational, motivational. Better software won't make a group more trusting, more committed, or more productive.

**What to do instead:** Technology should follow practice. Not precede it. First, understand how the group works. Then, find or build technology that supports that work. SWATS was built before the practice was understood. It's architecture looking for a group. Architecture without a group is just architecture.

> **Perturbator:** *"SWATS is a technology platform. Beautiful architecture. Well-designed. It also doesn't matter. Because the problem isn't the platform. It's the group. A group that doesn't know how to cooperate won't be saved by better software. Better software just makes a failing group fail faster. Technology amplifies existing behavior. It doesn't create new behavior."*

**Reference:** Peeragogy Handbook, "SWATS" section (Technologies, Services, and Platforms).
