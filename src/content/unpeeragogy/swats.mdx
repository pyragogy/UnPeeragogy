## Summary

Perfect tools amplify broken processes. SWATS assumes technology enables peer learning — but GitLab's stewardship handbook and Blender's governance structure prove tools only work when authority boundaries exist first. Without governance, collaboration software becomes expensive chaos.

## Evidenze Grounded

### 🔹 Confirming (2)

**Caso 1 — GitLab stewardship handbook**
- **Fenomeno:** Explicit documentation of governance boundaries between corporate sponsor and community reduces friction in open-core projects.
- **Fonte:** [https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/](https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/)

**Caso 2 — Blender Foundation governance**
- **Fenomeno:** Foundation governance with a board, CTO, and module owners can sustain complex peer production for decades.
- **Fonte:** [https://www.blender.org/about/foundation/](https://www.blender.org/about/foundation/)

### 🔸 Complicating (2)

**Caso 1 — P2P University governance challenges**
- **Fenomeno:** Peer learning communities inevitably face credentialing and quality assurance crises that require either hierarchy or external validation frameworks.
- **Fonte:** [https://www.p2pu.org/en/about/](https://www.p2pu.org/en/about/)

**Caso 2 — WebAssembly Community Group governance**
- **Fenomeno:** Choosing lighter governance models for standards development trades authority for speed, creating different failure modes for peer participation.
- **Fonte:** [https://www.w3.org/community/webassembly/](https://www.w3.org/community/webassembly/)

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern works when governance boundaries are documented before tool deployment. GitLab's stewardship handbook and Blender Foundation's board-CTO-module owner structure show that explicit authority divisions enable tools to enhance rather than complicate peer coordination.

### Negative Rule

> Do not apply this pattern when groups lack explicit governance structures. P2P University's credentialing crisis and WebAssembly Community Group's authority-speed trade-offs show that tools cannot substitute for governance — they only amplify whatever coordination patterns already exist.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern claims technology enables peer coordination in learning environments. Partial verification — but only when governance precedes deployment. GitLab's explicit stewardship boundaries and Blender's board-CTO-module structure sustain complex peer production, while P2P University's credentialing crisis and WebAssembly's authority-speed conflicts show tools cannot substitute for missing coordination patterns. The mechanism isn't about technology adoption. It's about governance maturity — whether groups can document who decides what before they start deciding together.
