## Summary

Peeragogy patterns fail when dropped into any context. GitLab's stewardship handbook works — Drupal's governance transition doesn't. The difference is scaffolding, not methodology.

## Evidenze Grounded

### 🔹 Confirming (2)

**Caso 1 — GitLab stewardship handbook**
- **Fenomeno:** Explicit documentation of governance boundaries between corporate sponsor and community reduces friction in open-core projects.
- **Fonte:** [https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/](https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/)

**Caso 2 — LWN.net kernel development summaries**
- **Fenomeno:** Independent third-party summaries of peer production activity create shared awareness that enables distributed governance at scale.
- **Fonte:** [https://lwn.net/Kernel/](https://lwn.net/Kernel/)

### 🔸 Complicating (2)

**Caso 1 — Drupal governance / community working groups**
- **Fenomeno:** Intentional governance transition from BDFL to community model requires years of structural work, not organic emergence.
- **Fonte:** [https://www.drupal.org/governance](https://www.drupal.org/governance)

**Caso 2 — IETF working group rough consensus**
- **Fenomeno:** Rough consensus governance only works when backed by hierarchical escalation paths; pure consensus without appeals fails.
- **Fonte:** [https://www.ietf.org/about/](https://www.ietf.org/about/)

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> Peeragogy patterns work when contextual scaffolding is explicitly constructed first. GitLab's stewardship handbook and LWN's kernel summaries show that documented governance boundaries and independent awareness mechanisms enable collaborative patterns to function at scale.

### Negative Rule

> Do not apply Peeragogy patterns assuming they work universally across contexts. The Drupal governance transition shows that moving between collaborative models requires years of structural work — patterns without contextual scaffolding produce governance theater, not actual peer learning.

## Sintesi Obliqua

Context-as-pattern claims that putting problems into communication with solutions manifests relationships. Partial verification — when the scaffolding exists first. GitLab's explicit boundaries and LWN's independent summaries create the infrastructure for peer governance to actually function, while Drupal's years-long transition from BDFL shows that structural work can't be skipped through pattern application. The theory misses the mechanism: patterns don't create context, they require it. You can't manifest relationships without first building the channels.
