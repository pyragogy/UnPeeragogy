### Il Dogma

The Peeragogy Handbook presents patterns as universal frameworks for peer learning, with minimal specification of contextual prerequisites or boundary conditions for their application. The implicit claim: a pattern that works in one peer setting will work in any peer setting, because the structure of peer learning is independent of the environment where it unfolds.

### La Realtà

A friend runs a hackerspace in Milan. Same tools, same maker ethos, same Arduino workshops as hackerspaces everywhere. But it dies within eighteen months. The universal pattern — open access, collaborative tools, community governance — fits perfectly on paper. In practice, it crashes against Italian bureaucracy, insurance requirements that assume corporate liability structures, and a membership base that expects someone else to handle the boring parts.

Not the wrong pattern. Not bad execution. Not insufficient funding.

The context ate the theory.

You see this everywhere once you start looking. Agile works brilliantly in Silicon Valley startups with venture funding and disposable talent pools. Deploy it in a government agency with union contracts and procurement cycles, and watch the ceremonies become elaborate theater while the actual work happens in email threads and hallway conversations. The pattern holds in isolation — but isolation doesn't exist.

### Perturbatore

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if the most important part of any pattern is the list of contexts where it breaks — and until you have that list, you don't actually have a pattern?"*

### Evidenze di Tenuta

**about.gitlab.com** — Explicit documentation of governance boundaries between corporate sponsor and community reduces friction in open-core projects. GitLab works because its governance design matches its specific corporate-community hybrid context.

**lwn.net** — Independent third-party summaries of peer production activity create shared awareness that enables distributed governance at scale. LWN's kernel summaries work because the Linux development community's distributed, asynchronous workflow specifically needs a curation layer.

### Evidenze di Faglia

**drupal.org** — Intentional governance transition from BDFL to community model required years of structural work, not organic emergence. Drupal shows that moving between collaborative models requires structural preparation — the pattern doesn't transfer, it rebuilds.

**ietf.org** — Rough consensus governance only works when backed by hierarchical escalation paths. Pure consensus without appeals fails. The IETF shows that even the most peer-driven standards body needs context-specific fallback mechanisms that no universal pattern predicts.

### Euristiche Operative

**Regola Positiva:** Applica i pattern di peeragogy solo quando l'impalcatura contestuale è costruita esplicitamente prima. GitLab e LWN dimostrano che confini di governance documentati e curation layer indipendenti rendono i pattern sostenibili nel loro contesto specifico.

**Regola Negativa:** Non applicare i pattern di peeragogy assumendo che funzionino universalmente attraverso contesti diversi. Drupal dimostra che muoversi tra modelli collaborativi richiede anni di lavoro strutturale — il pattern non si trasferisce, si ricostruisce.

**Riferimento:** audit-v2 (delta tensione: 1.6 → 1.5417, −0.0583)

**Stato:** PROVVISORIO

L'evidenza sfida l'universalità dei pattern. Il contesto non è rumore di fondo da filtrare per trovare il pattern puro — il contesto è il segnale. L'hackerspace di Milano non è un caso di implementazione sbagliata: è il caso che mostra che il pattern esisteva solo nelle condizioni che non erano state dichiarate. I pattern universali sono come chiavi universali — non aprono niente perché non si adattano a niente.
