### Claim

The context in which Peeragogy is applied is explored in several case studies.

### Reality

A friend runs a hackerspace in Milan. Same tools, same maker ethos, same Arduino workshops as hackerspaces everywhere. But it dies within eighteen months. The universal pattern — open access, collaborative tools, community governance — fits perfectly on paper. In practice, it crashes against Italian bureaucracy, insurance requirements that assume corporate liability structures, and a membership base that expects someone else to handle the boring parts.

Not the wrong pattern. Not bad execution. Not insufficient funding.

The context ate the theory.

You see this everywhere once you start looking. Agile works brilliantly in Silicon Valley startups with venture funding and disposable talent pools. Deploy it in a government agency with union contracts and procurement cycles, and watch the ceremonies become elaborate theater while the actual work happens in email threads and hallway conversations. The pattern holds in isolation — but isolation doesn't exist.

> What if the most important part of any pattern is the list of contexts where it breaks?

### Evidence That Supports

**about.gitlab.com** -- Explicit documentation of governance boundaries between corporate sponsor and community reduces friction in open-core projects.

**lwn.net** -- Independent third-party summaries of peer production activity create shared awareness that enables distributed governance at scale.


### Evidence That Complicates

**drupal.org** -- Intentional governance transition from BDFL to community model requires years of structural work, not organic emergence.


### Evidence That Contradicts

*No case in the current corpus directly contradicts the pattern.*

### Evidence That Is Ambiguous

**ietf.org** -- Rough consensus governance only works when backed by hierarchical escalation paths; pure consensus without appeals fails.


### Synthesis

These failures suggest something the handbooks rarely admit: context isn't background noise you filter out to find the pure pattern. Context is the signal. The hackerspace pattern works when embedded in a regulatory environment that treats informal organizations as legitimate, a culture comfortable with distributed responsibility, and an economic base that can absorb the hidden costs of coordination.

Remove any of those conditions and you're not implementing the pattern badly — you're implementing a different pattern entirely. What appears as the same structure performing differently may actually be different structures wearing the same name. The useful work isn't perfecting universal patterns but mapping the precise conditions where specific approaches hold their shape.
