## Summary

Motivation gets consumed by the work it was supposed to fuel. Theory says measure individual commitment — practice shows governance structures either exist or they don't. When crisis hits, asking 'what brings me here' becomes performance art.

## Evidenze Grounded

- **Fenomeno:** Cooperative governance structures with multiple councils and internal financial institutions can manage crises that would destroy flat organizations.

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern works when formal governance structures with binding authority already exist. The Mondragon cooperative and OpenStreetMap Data Working Group show that established institutional mechanisms can manage crises that would destroy projects relying solely on individual motivation management.

### Negative Rule

> Do not apply this pattern when governance structures are absent or contested. The OpenSSL Heartbleed aftermath shows that technical failures expose governance deficits that cannot be resolved through motivation measurement — external institutional support becomes necessary.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern claims motivation measurement prevents project collapse through self-reflection and contribution mapping. Partial verification — but only when formal authority already exists. Mondragon's cooperative councils and OpenStreetMap's Data Working Group resolve crises because they have binding mechanisms, not because participants measured their motivations. WebAssembly's governance contests and OpenSSL's institutional gaps show the opposite: when power is unclear, motivation becomes irrelevant noise. The real mechanism isn't individual honesty about commitment. Authority decides.
