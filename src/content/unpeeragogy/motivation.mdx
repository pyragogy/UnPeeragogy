"Motivation: what drives participation in a peer group." "Intrinsic and extrinsic factors."

The Motivation section tries to answer: why do people participate? What keeps them coming back?

**Why it matters:** Motivation is a consumable resource. The group starts with a tank full of enthusiasm. Every meeting, every delay, every unclear decision burns fuel. If nothing refills the tank, the group runs dry.

The theory lists motivation factors. Recognition, purpose, belonging. All valid. All static. The practice knows motivation is dynamic. It fluctuates. It depends on the last interaction, the last success, the last failure. A group that doesn't actively manage motivation will lose it. Not because people aren't committed. Because motivation is a resource, not a trait. It depletes.

**What to do instead:** Manage motivation like inventory. Measure it. "On a scale of 1-10, how motivated are you to work on this project?" Ask regularly. When the number drops below 5, the group has a motivation problem. Not a project problem. Fixing the project won't fix motivation. Fixing motivation requires success, recognition, or a break. Sometimes all three.

> **Perturbator:** *"Motivation isn't a personality trait. It's a resource. It depletes. Every useless meeting, every decision postponed, every promise broken burns motivation. The group started with a full tank. Now it's running on fumes. And no one is refueling. Because no one treats motivation as something that needs maintenance. They treat it as something that should be there. It's not. Not anymore."*
