### Il Dogma

Motivation is a consumable resource that depletes over time, requiring regular measurement and management to prevent depletion that leads to project failure.

### La Realtà

Three months into the project, Sarah stopped showing up to meetings. When pressed, she admitted the weekly check-ins had become performance theater — everyone reciting their contributions like confession. The original energy that brought her to collaborate on the community garden had curdled into obligation.

Not burnout. Not scheduling conflicts. Not the usual suspects that kill volunteer projects. The motivation itself had been consumed by the machinery meant to sustain it. The handbook promised that asking "What brings me here?" would make things percolate, but Sarah's group had turned introspection into inventory — cataloguing contributions instead of discovering them.

The honest answer to "What brings me here?" changes. Week one: curiosity about permaculture. Week eight: social pressure to justify her presence. Week twelve: the sunk cost of meetings attended. The question that was supposed to unlock engagement had become a trap that measured it to death.

### Perturbatore

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if you can't measure your way out of a power struggle — and the most motivated people are the ones who never have to explain why they're there?"*

### Evidenze di Tenuta

**mondragon-corporation.com** — Cooperative governance structures with multiple councils and internal financial institutions can manage crises that would destroy flat organizations. Mondragon shows that institutional mechanisms survive long after individual motivation depletes.

**wiki.osmfoundation.org** — Formal dispute resolution bodies with binding authority and published reasoning resolve peer production conflicts. OpenStreetMap demonstrates that authority structures sustain participation when individual drive fluctuates.

### Evidenze di Faglia

**w3.org** — Choosing lighter governance models for standards development trades authority for speed, creating different failure modes for peer participation. WebAssembly shows that lightweight structures expose motivation to structural stress that heavier governance absorbs.

**coreinfrastructure.org** — Technical failures expose governance and resource deficits in critical peer-produced infrastructure. OpenSSL's Heartbleed aftermath demonstrates that motivation management cannot resolve governance deficits — external institutional support is required.

### Euristiche Operative

**Regola Positiva:** Applica questo pattern quando strutture di governance formali con autorità vincolante esistono già. Mondragon e OpenStreetMap mostrano che meccanismi istituzionali consolidati possono gestire crisi che distruggerebbero progetti che dipendono dalla motivazione individuale.

**Regola Negativa:** Non applicare questo pattern quando le strutture di governance sono assenti o contestate. OpenSSL mostra che i fallimenti tecnici rivelano deficit di governance che non possono essere risolti misurando la motivazione — il supporto istituzionale esterno è necessario.

### Sintesi & Tracciabilità

**Stato:** PROVVISORIO

L'evidenza suggerisce qualcosa di scomodo sulla relazione del peer learning con l'auto-consapevolezza. Le strutture di governance cooperativa sopravvivono alle crisi proprio perché non dipendono dalla motivazione individuale come carburante — creano sistemi che funzionano indipendentemente dal perché le persone partecipano. La risoluzione dispute formale ha successo dove il consenso fallisce perché rimuove il peso del lavoro emotivo da ogni interazione. La domanda aperta: se chiedere alle persone di esaminare la loro motivazione rafforzi l'impegno o insegni a performarlo invece.
