"Everyone must bring self-awareness and honesty." The Motivation section of the handbook says this on page 1.

The unspoken implication: if the project fails, it's because you weren't motivated enough.

**When structural problems get personalized**
A group that doesn't function often has a structural problem, not a motivational one. Workload is unevenly distributed. Decisions don't get made. Roles are unclear. No one is paying for people's time. But it can feel easier to diagnose "lack of motivation" than to redistribute work. The first is an individual attribution. The second is organizational labor.

The theory leverages self-awareness as if it were a lever. Self-awareness can help you recognize you're demotivated. It may not give you the energy to stop being demotivated. Understanding why you're stuck doesn't always help you get unstuck — sometimes it can deepen the stuckness.

> **Perturbator:** *"Ask yourself why you're not doing things. The answer is almost never 'I'm not motivated enough.' More often it's: the cost is too high, it's not clear who's supposed to do it, or no one will do it anyway. Motivation is the last variable, not the first. Stop talking about it and change the conditions."*

**The case of declared motivation**
One specific pattern the theory may underestimate: declared motivation. What people say they feel when they join a group. "I'm very motivated, I want to learn, I want to contribute." Three weeks later, they're silent. Not because they were lying. Because declared motivation costs nothing. Real motivation costs time, energy, attention — and they didn't know how much it would cost.

The theory doesn't always distinguish between the two. It can take the declaration at face value. Then it's surprised when people disappear.

**What remains**
Motivation, in practice, is less a thing to cultivate and more a thing to observe. People who are motivated do things. People who aren't motivated say they want to do things. If after two weeks someone hasn't produced anything, they may not have a motivation problem — they may have a priority problem. And priorities don't change with a pattern.
