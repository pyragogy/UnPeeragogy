### The Assumption

Motivation is a consumable resource that depletes over time, requiring regular measurement and management to prevent depletion that leads to project failure.

### Operational Reality

Three months into the project, Sarah stopped showing up. Not the usual suspects — no burnout, no scheduling collapse, no drama. The weekly check-ins had become performance theater: everyone reciting their contributions like confession. The original energy that brought her to the community garden had curdled into obligation.

The handbook promised that asking "What brings me here?" would make things percolate. But Sarah's group had turned introspection into inventory — cataloguing contributions instead of discovering them.

The honest answer to "What brings me here?" changes. Week one: curiosity about permaculture. Week eight: social pressure to justify her presence. Week twelve: the sunk cost of meetings attended. The question that was supposed to unlock engagement had become a trap that measured it to death.

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if you can't measure your way out of a power struggle — and the most motivated people are the ones who never have to explain why they're there?"*

### Empirical Evidence

**Resilience Cases (Confirming):**
- [Mondragon Governance](https://www.mondragon-corporation.com/en/about-us/governance/) — Cooperative governance structures with multiple councils and internal financial institutions survive crises that destroy flat organizations. Institutional mechanisms persist long after individual motivation depletes.
- [OpenStreetMap Data Working Group](https://wiki.osmfoundation.org/wiki/Data_Working_Group) — Formal dispute resolution bodies with binding authority sustain participation when individual drive fluctuates. Authority structures outlast motivation.

**Fracture Cases (Complicating):**
- [OpenSSL Heartbleed / Core Infrastructure Initiative](https://www.coreinfrastructure.org/) — Technical failures expose governance and resource deficits that motivation management cannot resolve. External institutional support, not introspection, addressed the crisis.
- [WebAssembly Community Group](https://www.w3.org/community/webassembly/) — Lighter governance structures expose peer participation to structural stress that heavier governance absorbs. Lightweight governance trades authority for speed, creating different failure modes.

### Operational Heuristics

**Positive Boundary:** Apply this pattern when formal governance structures with binding authority already exist — institutional mechanisms survive crises that destroy projects relying on individual motivation.

**Negative Boundary:** Do not apply this pattern when governance structures are absent or contested — technical failures expose governance deficits that measuring motivation cannot fix, and external institutional support is required.

### Epistemic Synthesis

The evidence suggests something uncomfortable about peer learning's relationship with self-awareness. Cooperative governance structures survive crises precisely because they do not depend on individual motivation as fuel — they create systems that function regardless of why people participate. Formal dispute resolution succeeds where consensus fails because it removes the weight of emotional work from every interaction. The open question: whether asking people to examine their motivation strengthens commitment or teaches them to perform it instead.
