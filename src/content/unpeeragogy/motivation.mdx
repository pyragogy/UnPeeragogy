### Claim

Some of the primary motivators reported by participants in the Pee

### Reality

Three months into the project, Sarah stopped showing up to meetings. When pressed, she admitted the weekly check-ins had become performance theater — everyone reciting their contributions like confession. The original energy that brought her to collaborate on the community garden had curdled into obligation.

Not burnout. Not scheduling conflicts. Not the usual suspects that kill volunteer projects. The motivation itself had been consumed by the machinery meant to sustain it. The handbook promised that asking "What brings me here?" would make things percolate, but Sarah's group had turned introspection into inventory — cataloguing contributions instead of discovering them.

The honest answer to "What brings me here?" changes. Week one: curiosity about permaculture. Week eight: social pressure to justify her presence. Week twelve: the sunk cost of meetings attended. The question that was supposed to unlock engagement had become a trap that measured it to death.

> What if the most motivated people are the ones who never have to explain why they're there?

### Synthesis

The evidence suggests something uncomfortable about peer learning's relationship with self-awareness. Cooperative governance structures survive crises precisely because they don't rely on individual motivation as fuel — they create systems that work regardless of why people participate. Formal dispute resolution succeeds where consensus fails because it removes the burden of emotional labor from every interaction.

But lighter governance models that trade authority for speed reveal the cost of this approach. When you optimize for motivated participation, you may be optimizing for the wrong thing entirely. The most sustainable peer learning happens when the work itself generates reasons to continue — not when participants must continuously justify their presence to themselves and others.

What remains to be tested is whether asking people to examine their motivations strengthens commitment or teaches them to perform it instead.
