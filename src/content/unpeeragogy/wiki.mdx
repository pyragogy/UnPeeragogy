"A wiki is a website that allows the collaborative development of content." "The Peeragogy wiki was the main tool for writing the Handbook."

The wiki is the tool that produced the Handbook. It was the group's workshop.

**Why it matters:** The wiki is where the Handbook was built. It's also a ghost town now. The pages are there, the discussions are there, the edits are there. But no one edits anymore. The workshop is empty.

The wiki's ghostness is a lesson: even successful peeragogical tools have a life cycle. The wiki served its purpose. It produced the Handbook. Then it stopped. The group that used it dispersed. The tool didn't sustain the group. The group sustained the tool. When the group left, the tool died.

**What to do instead:** Accept that tools have life cycles. A wiki isn't a community. It's a place where a community works. If the community stops working, the wiki becomes an archive. Archives are useful. They preserve history. But they don't create future. Don't mistake a wiki for a community. A wiki is a tool. A community is a group of people who decide to use it.

> **Perturbator:** *"The wiki was the workshop. Now it's a museum. Beautiful pages, ancient edits, discussions that will never be resumed. The workshop is empty. The wiki didn't die. The group left. The wiki stayed. Empty spaces are the saddest proof that peeragogy has a cycle. It's born, it works, it ends. The wiki proves it."*
