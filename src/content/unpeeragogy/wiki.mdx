### The Assumption

Wikis are the natural tools for peeragogical collaboration due to their openness, editability, and accessible structure, enabling communities to co-create knowledge with minimal barriers to participation.

### Operational Reality

Wikis do not create collaboration. They create graveyards of unfinished, conflicted, or abandoned knowledge. The editing interface is simple enough to invite contribution and complex enough to produce contradiction.

OpenStreetMap's Data Working Group resolves wiki disputes through binding authority — the wiki is a content layer, not a governance layer. GitLab's stewardship handbook shows that explicit governance boundaries reduce friction more effectively than wiki features. Debian's init system transition shows that wiki edits without recourse produce contested outcomes that feel imposed.

The wiki is a technology. Collaboration is a governance outcome. The pattern mistakes the technology for the outcome.

### Empirical Evidence

**Resilience Cases (Confirming):**
- [OpenStreetMap Data Working Group](https://wiki.osmfoundation.org/wiki/Data_Working_Group) — Formal dispute resolution bodies with binding authority resolve wiki disputes. Binding authority makes wiki collaboration productive by providing escalation paths.
- [GitLab Stewardship Handbook](https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/) — Explicit documentation of governance boundaries reduces friction. Governance determines wiki effectiveness, not the reverse.

**Fracture Cases (Complicating):**
- [Debian Init System Transition](https://wiki.debian.org/Init) — Wiki edits without recourse produce contested outcomes that feel imposed. Wiki modifications without escalation produce conflict.
- [WebAssembly Community Group](https://www.w3.org/community/webassembly/) — Lighter governance models trade authority for speed, creating different failure modes for wiki-based collaboration.

### Operational Heuristics

**Positive Boundary:** The wiki works when paired with formal dispute resolution mechanisms and explicit governance boundaries — binding authority for dispute resolution makes wiki collaboration productive.

**Negative Boundary:** Do not implement wiki as a governance mechanism — wiki edits without escalation produce conflict. The wiki is a content layer, not a governance layer.

### Epistemic Synthesis

The wiki is a technology. Collaboration is a governance outcome. The pattern mistakes the technology for the outcome. OpenStreetMap resolves wiki disputes through binding authority. GitLab shows that governance determines wiki effectiveness. Debian shows that wiki edits without escalation produce conflict.
