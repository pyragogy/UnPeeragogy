## Summary

Equal access to the delete button doesn't make everyone an editor. Theory says wikis democratize knowledge — practice shows they need explicit governance boundaries before collaborative editing begins. The mechanism is authority structures, not participation structures.

## Evidenze Grounded

### 🔹 Confirming (2)

**Caso 1 — GitLab stewardship handbook**
- **Fenomeno:** Explicit documentation of governance boundaries between corporate sponsor and community reduces friction in open-core projects.
- **Fonte:** [https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/](https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/)

**Caso 2 — OpenStreetMap Data Working Group resolution**
- **Fenomeno:** Formal dispute resolution bodies with binding authority and published reasoning resolve peer production conflicts that informal consensus cannot.
- **Fonte:** [https://wiki.osmfoundation.org/wiki/Data_Working_Group](https://wiki.osmfoundation.org/wiki/Data_Working_Group)

### 🔸 Complicating (2)

**Caso 1 — WebAssembly Community Group governance**
- **Fenomeno:** Choosing lighter governance models for standards development trades authority for speed, creating different failure modes for peer participation.
- **Fonte:** [https://www.w3.org/community/webassembly/](https://www.w3.org/community/webassembly/)

**Caso 2 — IETF working group rough consensus**
- **Fenomeno:** Rough consensus governance only works when backed by hierarchical escalation paths; pure consensus without appeals fails.
- **Fonte:** [https://www.ietf.org/about/](https://www.ietf.org/about/)

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern works when governance boundaries are explicitly documented before collaborative editing begins. GitLab's stewardship handbook demonstrates that explicit documentation of authority structures between different stakeholder groups reduces friction and sustains long-term collaborative knowledge work.

### Negative Rule

> Do not apply this pattern without formal dispute resolution mechanisms. OpenStreetMap's Data Working Group shows that informal consensus fails to resolve peer production conflicts, requiring binding authority with published reasoning to maintain collaborative sustainability.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern claims wikis work through equal access and emergent consensus. Partial verification — but only when governance boundaries exist before editing starts. GitLab's stewardship handbook and OpenStreetMap's Data Working Group both confirm that documented authority structures reduce friction, while WebAssembly's lighter governance shows the limits of informal consensus in resolving conflicts. What's missing is the real engine: not democratic participation, but binding dispute resolution with published reasoning. The garden dies when everyone has pruning shears.
