## Summary

Groups default to magical thinking when formal coordination fails. Theory says explicit governance replaces fantasy — practice shows the fantasy often works better than broken formal structures. It matters because premature formalization can destroy functioning informal coordination.

## Evidenze Grounded

### 🔹 Confirming (2)

**Caso 1 — GitLab stewardship handbook**
- **Fenomeno:** Explicit documentation of governance boundaries between corporate sponsor and community reduces friction in open-core projects.
- **Fonte:** [https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/](https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/)

**Caso 2 — OpenStreetMap Data Working Group resolution**
- **Fenomeno:** Formal dispute resolution bodies with binding authority and published reasoning resolve peer production conflicts that informal consensus cannot.
- **Fonte:** [https://wiki.osmfoundation.org/wiki/Data_Working_Group](https://wiki.osmfoundation.org/wiki/Data_Working_Group)

### 🔸 Complicating (2)

**Caso 1 — Drupal governance / community working groups**
- **Fenomeno:** Intentional governance transition from BDFL to community model requires years of structural work, not organic emergence.
- **Fonte:** [https://www.drupal.org/governance](https://www.drupal.org/governance)

**Caso 2 — RISC-V foundation governance / move to Switzerland**
- **Fenomeno:** Foundation-govered open-source projects can make binding legal decisions (domicile, IP) without community consent, exposing the limits of peer governance in incorporated projects.
- **Fonte:** [https://riscv.org/announcements/risc-v-foundation-to-move-to-switzerland/](https://riscv.org/announcements/risc-v-foundation-to-move-to-switzerland/)

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern works when explicit governance alternatives exist and can be implemented immediately. GitLab stewardship handbook and OpenStreetMap Data Working Group show that formal boundaries and dispute resolution mechanisms can replace magical thinking with functional coordination structures.

### Negative Rule

> Do not apply this pattern when groups lack governance infrastructure but are still coordinating effectively. The Drupal governance transition shows that moving from informal to formal structures requires years of deliberate work — premature intervention destroys working coordination without providing alternatives.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern claims magical thinking robs contexts of process and learning. Partial verification — when GitLab's stewardship handbook and OpenStreetMap's Data Working Group show formal boundaries can work. But Drupal's governance transition reveals the complication: moving from informal to formal takes years of deliberate work, and premature intervention destroys what's already functioning. The real mechanism isn't replacing magic with structure. It's recognizing when the magic is load-bearing.
