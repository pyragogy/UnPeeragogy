"When a group starts, people often imagine an idyllic community." "They project fantasies of belonging onto the group."

Magical thinking is the default state of any new group. "We'll be different. We'll collaborate better. Here, hierarchy won't exist."

**Why it matters:** Magical thinking isn't a bug. It's the engine of the first phase. Without it, groups wouldn't form. You need a dose of fantasy to start something uncertain. The problem is when magical thinking doesn't transition to realism. When the group stays in the fantasy for months and never faces operational reality.

The gap between fantasy and reality is where groups dissolve. They can't maintain the fantasy (too much friction) and can't accept reality (too much work). So they float. Meetings where nothing happens. Discussions that don't advance. Decisions that aren't made. The group is neither the paradise imagined nor the functioning group it could become. It's stuck.

**What to do instead:** Fantasy is fuel. Not a destination. Use the energy of the first phase to create something real before the fantasy fades. If after two months the group is still discussing "how wonderful it would be if...," the fuel is gone. And the vehicle hasn't moved.

> **Perturbator:** *"You imagined the group would be wonderful. It's not. There's conflict, confusion, laziness. You have two options: accept the disappointment and work, or keep fantasizing about the group that could be and do nothing. The second option is more common. It's also more comfortable. And it leads nowhere."*
