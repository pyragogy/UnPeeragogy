"Self Organised Learning Environments: method in which students learn through their own exploration." "Minimally invasive education."

SOLE is a method from Sugata Mitra. Give children a computer and a question. Let them explore independently.

**Why it matters:** SOLE is beautiful and nearly impossible to replicate. Mitra's experiments were in specific conditions: curious children, minimal distractions, strong intrinsic motivation. Recreating those conditions in a typical group is different from the controlled experiment.

The theory says: "give a question, let the group explore." The practice says: "give a question, the group stares at the question, no one starts because no one knows how." Self-organized learning works when self-organization is already a skill. It's not a skill most groups have. Self-organization must be learned first. Before it can be practiced.

**What to do instead:** SOLE works if the group already has a culture of self-direction. If it doesn't, start with guided exploration. Then move to self-organization. Don't skip the scaffolding. Scaffolding isn't cheating. It's teaching. Self-organization is taught. Not born.

> **Perturbator:** *"Give the group a question and let them explore. Sounds great. Reality: the group stares at the question for three weeks. No one explores. Because no one knows how. Self-organization isn't a default. It's a skill. And like any skill, it needs to be taught. SOLE assumes the skill is there. It rarely is."*
