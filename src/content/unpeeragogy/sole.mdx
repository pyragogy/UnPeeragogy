### Il Dogma

Self-Organizing Learning Environments (SOLE) are based on the premise that groups can naturally explore questions through emergent self-organization, with minimal external structure or facilitation.

### La Realtà

Self-organization is what happens after someone else does the organizing.

P2PU learning circles survive through defined facilitator roles and institutional partnerships — the opposite of self-organization. OpenStreetMap's Data Working Group resolves disputes through formal authority — the opposite of self-organization. IETF's rough consensus model fails without hierarchical escalation — the opposite of self-organization.

The pattern imagines learners organizing themselves. The evidence shows that the scaffolding is invisible to the people who benefit from it. Someone designed the room. Someone set the schedule. Someone defined the question. Self-organization is a luxury that depends on organizational infrastructure that SOLE doesn't acknowledge.

### Perturbatore

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if self-organization is what happens after someone else does the organizing — and the scaffolding is invisible to the people who benefit from it?"*

### Evidenze di Tenuta

**p2pu.org** — Peer learning models with defined facilitator roles and institutional partnerships are more sustainable than purely peer-driven learning. P2PU shows that sustainable self-organized learning requires facilitator scaffolding.

**wiki.osmfoundation.org** — Formal dispute resolution bodies with binding authority resolve peer production conflicts. OpenStreetMap demonstrates that informal self-organization requires formal dispute resolution to survive.

### Evidenze di Faglia

**ietf.org** — Rough consensus governance only works when backed by hierarchical escalation paths. IETF shows that pure self-organization without appeals fails.

**w3.org** — Choosing lighter governance models trades authority for speed. WebAssembly demonstrates that self-organization without governance scaffolding hits structural ceilings.

### Euristiche Operative

**Regola Positiva:** SOLE funziona quando lo scaffolding istituzionale supporta l'esplorazione peer. P2PU mostra che ruoli di facilitazione definiti e partnership istituzionali creano ambienti di peer learning sostenibili.

**Regola Negativa:** Non implementare SOLE senza ruoli di facilitazione e meccanismi di escalation. IETF mostra che il puro consenso senza appelli fallisce, e OpenStreetMap dimostra che i metodi informali hanno limiti strutturali.
