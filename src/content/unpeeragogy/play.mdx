"Play is the opposite of work." "Fun, excitement, and humor are important facets of peeragogy."

The theory says play is important. It makes learning enjoyable.

**Why it matters:** Play is work that doesn't look like work. And like all work, it requires energy. Play isn't automatic. It doesn't happen just because the group is together. It requires someone to organize it, propose it, sustain it.

The problem with play in peeragogical groups is that it's undervalued. "We're just having fun." That "just" devalues the work. Having fun together is one of the most difficult things a group can do. It requires trust, safety, and the willingness to be vulnerable. None of this is light. It's all heavy. And it's all necessary.

**What to do instead:** Value play as work. Give it space. Protect it. Don't think play is what happens when the serious work is done. Play is the serious work. It's the glue that makes the group stay together when production slows down.

> **Perturbator:** *"Play is the group's immune system. When the group plays together, it builds trust. When it builds trust, it can handle conflict. When it handles conflict, it produces. Play isn't the break from work. It's the foundation of work. Groups that don't play together don't last. They stay functional until the first crisis. Then they dissolve."*
