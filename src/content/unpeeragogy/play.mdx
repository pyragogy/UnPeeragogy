## Summary

Learning through play works until the stakes get real. Theory says fun creates lasting bonds — practice shows these bonds dissolve when money or control enters the picture. The mechanism breaks precisely when you need it most.

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern works when combined with explicit governance structures that activate during conflicts. GitLab's stewardship handbook and OpenStreetMap's Data Working Group show that formal dispute resolution mechanisms preserve community cohesion when informal trust-building reaches its limits.

### Negative Rule

> Do not rely solely on play-based trust building when groups face resource conflicts or governance crises. The Node.js/io.js merger and Drupal's BDFL transition show that informal social bonds cannot resolve structural disagreements about project direction or authority distribution.

## Sintesi Obliqua

Play-based learning claims to build durable trust through shared iteration and boundary testing. Partial verification — it works for routine collaboration when resources are abundant and conflicts stay technical. GitLab's stewardship handbook and OpenStreetMap's dispute resolution prove that even play-built communities need formal governance when informal consensus fails, while the Node.js fork and Drupal's leadership transitions show how quickly game-based bonds fracture under structural pressure. The real mechanism isn't play creating trust — it's play masking the absence of actual decision-making structures. Games end when someone has to lose.
