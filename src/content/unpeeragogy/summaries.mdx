### The Assumption

Summaries indicate the original content is too verbose, that summaries written by the same authors lead to biased essentiality, and that good summaries require an outsider's perspective.

### Operational Reality

Every summary is a coup attempt in disguise.

The pattern correctly identifies that summarization is not neutral — the summarizer decides what matters. GitLab's stewardship handbook works because the boundaries it summarizes are explicit and contestable. LWN.net's kernel summaries are trusted because they are produced by independent third parties with formal editorial processes. But Wikipedia Signpost and Debian DPL newsletters show that community-produced summaries become de facto governance channels — the summarizer's attention becomes the project's priorities.

Summarization is curation, and curation is power. The pattern identifies the bias. It does not acknowledge that the solution is not better summarizers — it is governance that makes summary effects visible and contestable.

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if every summary is a coup attempt in disguise — and summarization is curation, and curation is power?"*

### Empirical Evidence

**Resilience Cases (Confirming):**
- [GitLab Stewardship Handbook](https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/) — Explicit documentation of governance boundaries reduces friction. Formal constraints on summary authority reduce bias effects.
- [LWN.net Kernel Development](https://lwn.net/Kernel/) — Independent third-party summaries with formal editorial processes create trusted shared awareness. Formal editorial processes produce trusted summaries.

**Fracture Cases (Complicating):**
- [Wikipedia Signpost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Wikipedia_Signpost) — Community journalism becomes de facto governance channel — the summarizer's attention shapes project priorities.
- [Debian DPL Bits Newsletter](https://lists.debian.org/debian-devel-announce/) — Centralized summary authorship creates hidden editorial control where what gets summarized defines what gets done.

### Operational Heuristics

**Positive Boundary:** Summaries work when summarizer independence is institutionalized or authority is explicitly bounded — formal constraints on summary power and independent editorial processes produce trusted summaries.

**Negative Boundary:** Do not create summaries without explicit governance boundaries — community-produced summaries become de facto governance channels where the summarizer's attention defines project priorities.

### Epistemic Synthesis

Every summary is a coup attempt in disguise. Summarization is not neutral — the summarizer decides what matters. GitLab works because its boundaries are explicit and contestable. LWN is trusted because of independent third-party production. But Wikipedia Signpost and Debian DPL show that community-produced summaries become de facto governance channels. The solution is not better summarizers — it is governance that makes summary effects visible and contestable.
