## Summary

Every summary becomes a governance document. Theory says summaries inform — practice shows they control what gets remembered and who gets to frame it. The difference between documentation and power is who writes the last draft.

## Evidenze Grounded

### 🔹 Confirming (2)

**Caso 1 — GitLab stewardship handbook**
- **Fenomeno:** Explicit documentation of governance boundaries between corporate sponsor and community reduces friction in open-core projects.
- **Fonte:** [https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/](https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/)

**Caso 2 — LWN.net kernel development summaries**
- **Fenomeno:** Independent third-party summaries of peer production activity create shared awareness that enables distributed governance at scale.
- **Fonte:** [https://lwn.net/Kernel/](https://lwn.net/Kernel/)

### 🔸 Complicating (2)

**Caso 1 — Wikipedia Signpost community journalism**
- **Fenomeno:** Community-produced summaries become de facto governance communication channels that can diverge from official narratives.
- **Fonte:** [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Wikipedia_Signpost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Wikipedia_Signpost)

**Caso 2 — Debian DPL bits newsletter**
- **Fenomeno:** Regular leadership summaries in peer-governed projects create agenda-setting power — what gets summarized is what gets attention.
- **Fonte:** [https://lists.debian.org/debian-devel-announce/](https://lists.debian.org/debian-devel-announce/)

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern works when summarizer independence is institutionalized or authority is explicitly bounded. GitLab's stewardship handbook and LWN.net's kernel summaries show that formal constraints on summarizer authority enable distributed governance while preventing shadow leadership formation.

### Negative Rule

> Do not create summaries without explicit governance boundaries. Wikipedia Signpost and Debian DPL newsletters show that community-produced summaries become de facto governance channels that can diverge from official narratives and concentrate agenda-setting power in the summarizer's hands.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern claims summaries reduce coordination friction through shared awareness. Partial verification — but only when authority boundaries are hard-coded from the start. GitLab's stewardship handbook and LWN's kernel summaries work because independence is institutionalized, while Wikipedia's Signpost and Debian newsletters show how community-produced summaries drift into shadow governance channels. The real mechanism isn't about information flow — it's about who controls the editorial frame. Summaries don't just document decisions. They become the decisions.
