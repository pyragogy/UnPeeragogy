"Summaries: condensed versions of the Handbook's content."

Summaries of the Handbook. Shorter versions for those who don't have time to read the full text.

**Why it matters:** Summaries are useful. They also admit that the main text is too long. If you need a summary, the original is too verbose. The Handbook needs a summary. That says something about the Handbook.

The problem with summaries is that they're written by the same people who wrote the original. They can't see what's essential because everything feels essential. A good summary requires a different writer. Someone who didn't write the original. Someone who can see the structure from the outside.

**What to do instead:** If you need a summary, ask someone who didn't write the original to write it. They'll know what's essential because they'll remember what they retained. The author remembers everything. The reader remembers little. The real summary is what a careful reader remembers after a week. Not what the author selected.

> **Perturbator:** *"The Handbook needs a summary. That means the Handbook is too long. If it were concise, it wouldn't need a summary. Writing a summary is admitting the original is verbose. The solution isn't a summary. The solution is a shorter original. But rewriting is harder than summarizing. So you write a summary."*
