"Welcome to the Peeragogy Workbook: a practical introduction to peeragogy." The very first page of the Handbook.

**Why it matters:** Welcome pages are invitations. "Come in. See what we have." The Handbook's welcome is warm, a bit generic, and long. By the time the reader reaches the patterns, they've read five pages of welcome.

The problem is that a welcome that's too long doesn't welcome. It wears out. The reader wants to know what peeragogy is and how to use it. Not a history of how the Handbook was written. Not a list of contributors. Not an explanation of the structure. A welcome should be one page. Then the content.

**What to do instead:** A welcome page should: (1) say what peeragogy is in one sentence, (2) say how the Handbook is structured, (3) point to the Quickstart Guide. That's it. Everything else can go in the preface. Welcome is a door. Doors don't need decoration. They need to open.

> **Perturbator:** *"Welcome. Then 300 pages of theory. Welcome is the edge of the forest. The patterns are the clearing. The Handbook makes you walk through the entire forest before reaching the clearing. The path should be shorter. If you've already read five pages of welcome, you're not welcomed anymore. You're tired."*

**Reference:** Peeragogy Handbook, "Welcome to the Peeragogy Workbook" section (Introduction).
