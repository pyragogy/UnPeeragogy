## Summary

Welcome manuals kill peer learning. Theory promises horizontal collaboration — practice creates documentation bureaucracy. The friction isn't in the doing, it's in the explaining how to do.

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern works when governance boundaries are made explicit and participation structures account for varying time availability. GitLab's stewardship handbook shows that documented boundaries between different stakeholder roles reduce friction and create sustainable open collaboration.

### Negative Rule

> Do not assume that peer governance is naturally egalitarian or that extensive welcome content improves accessibility. Apache Software Foundation governance shows that meritocracy-based systems create class barriers based on time availability, while vault incidents demonstrate that long introductions tire readers before they reach content.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern claims that extensive onboarding reduces barriers to peer participation. Partial verification — when boundaries are explicit and time constraints acknowledged upfront. GitLab's stewardship handbook works because it maps power, not process, while Apache's meritocracy and micro:bit's structured governance both prove that peer systems naturally stratify around availability rather than capability. The real mechanism isn't welcome content but power distribution design. Documentation as invitation becomes documentation as gate.
