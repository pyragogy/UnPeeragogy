### The Assumption

The Peeragogy Workbook presents itself as an open invitation to collaborative learning, designed to welcome newcomers into peer-to-peer educational practices through accessible introductory content.

### Operational Reality

Nothing says "peer learning" like a 20-page manual on how to be peers.

The workbook's open invitation assumes that accessibility of content creates accessibility of participation. It does not. GitLab's stewardship handbook shows that documented boundaries between corporate sponsor and community reduce friction more effectively than extensive welcome content. BBC micro:bit foundation governance shows that even intentional educational foundations face participation barriers that welcome content cannot address. Apache Software Foundation's meritocracy governance shows that even open systems create de facto hierarchies through time availability and domain expertise.

The welcome is necessary. It is also insufficient. The workbook that focuses on welcoming without addressing the structural barriers to participation is like a greeter at a building with no ramp.

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if nothing says 'peer learning' like a 20-page manual on how to be peers — and a welcome without structural access is like a greeter at a building with no ramp?"*

### Empirical Evidence

**Resilience Cases (Confirming):**
- [GitLab Stewardship Handbook](https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/) — Explicit documentation of governance boundaries between corporate sponsor and community reduces friction. Documented participation boundaries welcome newcomers more effectively than extensive introductory content.

**Fracture Cases (Complicating):**
- [BBC micro:bit Foundation](https://microbit.org/foundation/) — Even intentional educational foundations face participation barriers that welcome content cannot address.
- [Apache Foundation How It Works](https://www.apache.org/foundation/how-it-works.html) — Meritocracy-based systems create de facto hierarchies through time availability — the most welcoming content cannot compensate for structural barriers.

### Operational Heuristics

**Positive Boundary:** Welcome works when governance boundaries are made explicit and participation structures account for unequal time availability — documented boundaries between different stakeholders reduce friction more than welcome content.

**Negative Boundary:** Do not assume peer governance is naturally egalitarian or that extensive welcome content improves accessibility — meritocracy-based systems create de facto hierarchies through time availability.

### Epistemic Synthesis

The welcome is necessary. It is also insufficient. Content accessibility does not create participation accessibility. GitLab shows that documented boundaries reduce friction more than welcome content. Apache shows that even open systems create de facto hierarchies. The workbook that focuses on welcoming without addressing structural barriers is like a greeter at a building with no ramp.
