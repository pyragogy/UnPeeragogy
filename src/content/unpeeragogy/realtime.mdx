"Real-time interaction and synchronous media often support quick decision-making and help with community building."

**Why it matters:** Real-time interaction is powerful and expensive. It creates connection. It also consumes attention. A group that relies on real-time interaction limits its capacity to those who can be present at the same time. This excludes time zones, work schedules, and family obligations.

The theory says real-time helps decision-making. True. But it also creates an invisible hierarchy: those who can be present decide more than those who can't. A group that decides everything in real-time meetings is a group where absent members have less power. The hierarchy isn't named. It's just factual.

**What to do instead:** Use real-time for connection, not decisions. Decisions should be asynchronous. Real-time for building trust, discussing complex issues. Asynchronous for deciding. If you decide everything during calls, the call becomes the center of power. And power in peeragogy should be distributed. Asynchronous tools distribute it better.

> **Perturbator:** *"You decide everything during the weekly call. Good for those who attend. Bad for those who can't. Every real-time decision is a decision made without the group. It's made with the subgroup that showed up. Real-time is useful. It's not democratic. The faster you decide, the more people you exclude."*
