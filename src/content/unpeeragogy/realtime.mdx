### The Assumption

Real-time meetings create opportunities for immediate feedback and dynamic collaboration, allowing groups to adapt quickly and build on each other's contributions in the moment.

### Operational Reality

The person who shows up to every real-time meeting accumulates authority invisibly. Not because they are manipulating the group. Because they are present when decisions get made, and presence becomes authorship. The three people who can attend Tuesday's call decide the agenda for the fifty who cannot.

OpenStreetMap's Data Working Group resolves disputes through structured asynchronous processes — not because real-time is bad, but because real-time democracy is tyranny of the available. Apache Software Foundation has governed for 25+ years through async consensus and email lists. Debian's systemd transition showed that real-time decision-making without async recourse produces contested outcomes that feel imposed.

Real-time is a privilege. Treating it as a neutral mechanism is a governance failure disguised as process.

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if real-time democracy is just tyranny of the unemployed — and the person who shows up to every meeting accumulates authority invisibly?"*

### Empirical Evidence

**Resilience Cases (Confirming):**
- [OpenStreetMap Data Working Group](https://wiki.osmfoundation.org/wiki/Data_Working_Group) — Formal dispute resolution bodies with binding authority and published reasoning resolve peer production conflicts. Structured async processes produce more equitable governance than real-time meetings.

**Fracture Cases (Complicating):**
- [Apache Foundation How It Works](https://www.apache.org/foundation/how-it-works.html) — Twenty-five-plus years of consensus-based governance showing that those with more free time accumulate authority, creating a class system invisible to those who can always attend.
- [Debian Init System Transition](https://wiki.debian.org/Init) — Debian's init system transition (systemd) demonstrates that real-time decision-making without async recourse produces contested outcomes that feel imposed.

### Operational Heuristics

**Positive Boundary:** Real-time decisions work when paired with formal dispute resolution mechanisms that operate asynchronously — binding authority with published reasoning allows equitable real-time decisions.

**Negative Boundary:** Do not rely on real-time decision-making when members have unequal time availability — those with more free time accumulate authority invisibly, creating a class system invisible to those who can always attend.
