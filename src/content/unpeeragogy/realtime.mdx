### Il Dogma

Real-time meetings create opportunities for immediate feedback and dynamic collaboration, allowing groups to adapt quickly and build on each other's contributions in the moment.

### La Realtà

The person who shows up to every real-time meeting accumulates authority invisibly. Not because they're manipulating the group. Because they're present when decisions get made, and presence becomes authorship. The three people who can attend Tuesday's call decide the agenda for the fifty who can't.

OpenStreetMap's Data Working Group resolves disputes through structured asynchronous processes — not because real-time is bad, but because real-time democracy is tyranny of the available. Apache Software Foundation has governed for 25+ years through async consensus and email lists. Debian's systemd transition showed that real-time decision-making without async recourse produces contested outcomes that feel imposed.

Real-time is a privilege. Treating it as a neutral mechanism is a governance failure disguised as process.

### Perturbatore

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if real-time democracy is just tyranny of the unemployed — and the person who shows up to every meeting accumulates authority invisibly?"*

### Evidenze di Tenuta

**wiki.osmfoundation.org** — Formal dispute resolution bodies with binding authority and published reasoning resolve peer production conflicts. OpenStreetMap shows that structured async processes produce more equitable governance than real-time meetings.

### Evidenze di Faglia

**apache.org** — Apache Software Foundation's 25+ year consensus-based governance shows that those with more free time accumulate authority, creating a class system invisible to those who can always attend.

**wiki.debian.org** — Debian's init system transition (systemd) shows that real-time decision-making without async recourse produces contested outcomes. Debian demonstrates that time-availability bias creates legitimacy problems.

### Euristiche Operative

**Regola Positiva:** Le decisioni in tempo reale funzionano quando abbinate a meccanismi formali di risoluzione dispute che operano in asincrono. OpenStreetMap mostra che l'autorità vincolante con ragionamento pubblicato permette decisioni real-time eque.

**Regola Negativa:** Non fare affidamento sul decision-making in tempo reale quando i membri hanno disponibilità di tempo diseguale. Apache mostra che quelli con più tempo libero accumulano autorità, creando un sistema di classe invisibile.
