### Il Dogma

The Peeragogy Handbook states that convening is easy but often leads to unproductive meetings without a clear plan, and that groups often die between invitation and first meeting due to lack of specific proposals. The implication: fix the planning, fix the convening.

### La Realtà

Fabrizio calls a meeting for Tuesday. By Monday, three people have scheduling conflicts, two haven't responded, and one suggests moving to Slack instead. The original agenda — ambitious, necessary — dissolves into logistics. This is convening in practice: the gap between intention and attendance, between the group you imagine and the group that shows up.

Not coordination failure. Not poor planning. Not the wrong tools. The friction lives deeper — in the assumption that gathering creates alignment, that proximity produces purpose. Every convener learns this the hard way: the people who need to be there most are often the ones who can't make it work.

The handbook talks about "suitable groups" as if suitability were obvious, as if the right people would recognize each other and naturally cohere. But convening exposes what remains hidden in theory — that someone must decide who belongs, someone must hold the center when others drift away.

### Perturbatore

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if you can't convene your way out of the fact that someone has to make the hard calls — and the people who made them before the meeting started are the ones who hold the power when it ends?"*

### Evidenze di Tenuta

**cpan.org** — Simple namespace ownership with easy transfer and clear permissions can scale decentralized peer production with minimal governance overhead. CPAN shows that convening with clear ownership rules from day one prevents the invitation-to-collapse pipeline.

**p2pu.org** — Peer learning models with defined facilitator roles and institutional partnerships are more sustainable than purely peer-driven learning. P2PU demonstrates that convening works when someone is responsible for holding the container — not just issuing invitations.

### Evidenze di Faglia

**drupal.org** — Intentional governance transition from BDFL to community model required years of structural work, not organic emergence. Drupal shows that convening without planning the authority transition creates crises that no agenda can fix.

**researchgate.net** — The divide in open education between peer-governed and institution-governed models shows that peer governance has known scalability limits. cMOOCs failed not because of poor convening, but because pure peer models don't scale beyond the intimacy threshold.

### Euristiche Operative

**Regola Positiva:** Applica questo pattern quando il gruppo rimane piccolo o adotta governance ibrida dall'inizio. CPAN (namespace ownership) e P2PU (partnership istituzionali) dimostrano che meccanismi di autorità definiti rendono la convocazione sostenibile.

**Regola Negativa:** Non applicare questo pattern quando il gruppo supererà 15 membri attivi o gestirà risorse contese senza strutture di autorità esplicite. Drupal e cMOOCs dimostrano che convocare senza pianificare la transizione di autorità crea crisi che nessuna agenda può risolvere.

### Sintesi & Tracciabilità

**Riferimento:** audit-v2 (delta tensione: 1.5 → 1.5319, +0.0319)
**Evidenze esaminate:** 4 (2 confermanti, 2 complicanti)
**Stato:** PROVVISORIO

Il pattern teorico assume che la convocazione sia un problema logistico — bastano un'agenda chiara e una data. L'evidenza mostra che convocare crea potere: qualcuno decide chi appartiene, qualcuno tiene il centro quando gli altri si allontanano. Convocare non è l'inizio del peer learning. È il momento in cui l'autorità si istituisce — e non ammetterlo è il primo fallimento.
