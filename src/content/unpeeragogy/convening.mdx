"Convening: bringing together a group of people with a shared interest." "The act of convening is the first step in peer learning."

**Why it matters:** Convening is the step that costs least and commits most. Sending an invitation is easy. Following up is work. The gap between the invitation and the first meeting is where groups die.

Convening is presented as a simple act. It's not. It's a promise. "Let's work together." The promiser doesn't know if the work will happen. They're taking a risk. And the risk materializes when people actually show up. Then you have to do something with them.

**What to do instead:** Before convening, know what you're proposing. Not "let's explore peeragogy." "Let's write a pattern about X by June." A specific proposal separates curious from committed. Curious people are the audience. Committed people are the group. Convening for curiosity is a lecture. Convening for production is peeragogy. Don't confuse the two.

> **Perturbator:** *"You convened the group. Sent emails, scheduled the call, picked the platform. Now people are here. What do you do with them? If you don't have a clear answer, the convening was premature. Convening is easy. Convening productively is hard. Most groups convene without a plan. Then they spend months figuring out what to do. The convening that cost nothing produced nothing."*
