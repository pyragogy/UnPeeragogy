"Learning happens through self-organizing networks of participants." "Individuals and groups create blogs or wikis around their own interpretations." "RSS feeds, decentralization."

Connectivism theory is beautiful. It describes a distributed learning ecosystem where everyone produces and consumes knowledge in a continuous flow. In reality, it's a hundred blogs that no one reads.

**Why it matters:** Opening a blog is easy. Writing it is easy. Getting it read is nearly impossible. Connectivism assumes that individual production generates collective value. Instead it generates noise. A hundred people writing their own interpretations produce a hundred texts that no one has time to read. The network isn't self-organizing. It's self-isolating.

RSS feeds were supposed to fix this. In theory, you subscribe to blogs that interest you and build your learning network. In practice, feeds accumulate thousands of unread articles. The learning curve becomes a mountain of reading debt. The group doesn't learn together — everyone drowns in their own content bubble.

**What to do instead:** Connectivism needs a core that produces for others. The rest is audience. The network works when someone curates, summarizes, and connects. Without curation, it's noise. If your group uses connectivism, assign someone to synthesize. The rest is just production without consumption.

> **Perturbator:** *"Connectivism works if everyone produces and everyone reads. In reality, 1% produces and 99% ignores. The network isn't a learning ecosystem. It's a library where only the librarian writes books. For themselves."*
