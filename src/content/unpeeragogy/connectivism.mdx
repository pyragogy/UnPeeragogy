## Summary

Networks multiply connections, not learning. Theory promises self-organization — practice delivers chaos without clear ownership. The difference between sustainable peer learning and abandoned forums is who decides what.

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern works when networks include defined facilitation roles and clear ownership structures. P2PU learning circles (institutional partnerships + facilitators) and CPAN governance (namespace ownership + transfer mechanisms) show that successful networked learning requires structural scaffolding, not just connection maximization.

### Negative Rule

> Do not apply this pattern assuming that more connections automatically improve learning outcomes. P2PU learning circles show that pure peer networks without facilitator roles fail to sustain engagement, while CPAN demonstrates that decentralized systems need ownership structures to prevent chaos.

## Sintesi Obliqua

Connectivism claims that learning emerges from network effects and distributed knowledge creation. Partial verification, but only when structural scaffolding exists. P2PU learning circles work because facilitators exist, CPAN scales because namespace ownership is clear — but Wikipedia deletion debates show how consensus without hierarchy creates shadow power structures, and most MOOCs collapse into ghost towns of abandoned blogs. The missing mechanism isn't connection density but responsibility assignment. Networks are fuel. Someone still has to drive.
