## Summary

Meetings without authority are expensive group therapy. Theory says regular rhythm sustains groups — practice shows rhythm needs binding power behind it. The difference between coordination and governance is who can say no and make it stick.

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern works when regular meetings are backed by formal dispute resolution mechanisms and defined decision-making authority. The OpenStreetMap Data Working Group and Fedora Engineering Steering Committee demonstrate that binding authority with transparent processes sustains peer governance across years of operation.

### Negative Rule

> Do not apply this pattern when groups face contested decisions or resource conflicts without formal authority structures. The WebAssembly Community Group shows that trading authority for speed creates different failure modes, while informal rhythms cannot resolve substantive disputes.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern claims regular contact maintains group cohesion through shared rhythm. Partial verification — but only when meetings carry decision-making weight. OpenStreetMap's Data Working Group and Fedora's Engineering Steering Committee work because they resolve disputes with binding authority, not just regular check-ins. WebAssembly's lighter governance trades authority for speed, creating different breakage modes. The real mechanism isn't rhythm — it's the authority to close open loops. Heartbeat without power to stop the bleeding.
