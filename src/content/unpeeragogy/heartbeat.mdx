### Il Dogma

Regular meetings and check-ins provide rhythm and continuity for peer learning groups, keeping participants connected and projects moving forward through consistent touchpoints. The theory assumes that scheduled interaction creates sustainable collaboration.

### La Realtà

A weekly standup call dies after six weeks. The Slack channel goes quiet. Three people still care about the project, but nobody knows who they are. This happens to every distributed team that mistakes communication for connection — they schedule heartbeats without checking if anyone has a pulse.

Not ritual. Not regular check-ins. Not another meeting where people report what they did yesterday. The heartbeat that actually works is the one you feel when something breaks. When the server goes down and two people jump on a call at midnight. When someone posts a half-formed idea and three others start building on it immediately. When the work itself generates the rhythm.

Most teams engineer their heartbeat backwards. They create the meeting first, then hope motivation follows. But motivation doesn't respond to calendar invites.

### Perturbatore

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if meetings without authority are just expensive group therapy sessions — and the strongest heartbeat is the one that skips a beat, the irregular pulse of people who only connect when they actually need each other?"*

### Evidenze di Tenuta

**wiki.osmfoundation.org** — Formal dispute resolution bodies with binding authority and published reasoning resolve peer production conflicts that informal consensus cannot. OpenStreetMap shows that heartbeat works when backed by authority, not scheduling.

**docs.fedoraproject.org** — Elected technical steering committees with defined scope and transparent records provide sustainable peer governance. Fedora FESCo demonstrates that decision-making authority creates pulse more reliably than calendar invites.

### Evidenze di Faglia

**w3.org** — Choosing lighter governance models for standards development trades authority for speed, creating different failure modes. WebAssembly shows that heartbeat without authority creates rhythm without substance.

**drupal.org** — Intentional governance transition from BDFL to community model required years of structural work. Drupal shows that sustainable heartbeat requires governance design, not meeting scheduling.

### Euristiche Operative

**Regola Positiva:** Applica questo pattern quando le riunioni regolari sono supportate da meccanismi formali di risoluzione dispute e autorità decisionale definita. OpenStreetMap e Fedora dimostrano che l'autorità crea impulso — il calendario no.

**Regola Negativa:** Non applicare questo pattern quando il gruppo affronta decisioni contestate o conflitti di risorse senza strutture di autorità formali. WebAssembly e Drupal mostrano che scambiare autorità per velocità crea riunioni senza sostanza.

### Sintesi & Tracciabilità

**Riferimento:** audit-v2 (delta tensione: 1.4 → 1.4337, +0.0338)

**Stato:** PROVVISORIO

L'evidenza suggerisce che il heartbeat sostenibile emerge da stake condivisi, non da calendari condivisi. I comitati tecnici che funzionano non sono quelli con più riunioni — sono quelli dove i membri si sentono responsabili per risultati che non possono raggiungere da soli. L'autorità crea impulso. La responsabilità crea ritmo. L'attrito utile può essere accettare che alcune collaborazioni non meritano di sopravvivere al loro primo incontro mancato.
