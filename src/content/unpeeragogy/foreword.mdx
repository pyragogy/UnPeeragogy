## Summary

Ideas arrive without permission. Theory says they need consensus to survive — practice shows they die from asking for it too early. The mechanism that matters is boundary-setting, not validation-seeking.

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern works when validation mechanisms are explicitly designed to preserve peer equality while providing external credibility. GitLab's stewardship model shows that transparent governance boundaries between sponsors and community create sustainable peer dynamics rather than authority conflicts.

### Negative Rule

> Do not assume peer projects can operate without any validation framework. ArXiv's endorsement system and Zappos' holacracy failure show that peer governance without explicit boundaries creates either hidden hierarchies or imposed contradictions that undermine the peer ideal.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern claims peer projects can operate without external authority if they maintain internal coherence. Partial verification — but only when governance boundaries are explicit, not assumed. GitLab's stewardship handbook works because it names the sponsor-community divide rather than pretending it doesn't exist, while arXiv's endorsement system creates hidden hierarchies that contradict peer equality and Zaptos' holacracy failed because executive fiat cannot create peer governance. What's missing is the mechanism for recognizing when boundaries are necessary versus when they become barriers. Independence that refuses all structure is just isolation with better marketing.
