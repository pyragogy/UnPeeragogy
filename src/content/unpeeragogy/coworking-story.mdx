"Julian, eager to use his new peeragogic insights, suggests an approach where individuals research specific factors." The story of a failed coworking. A tale of how theory collides with reality.

**Why it matters:** Stories are powerful. But a story isn't a pattern. A story says "this happened." It doesn't say "this will always happen." The Handbook uses stories as evidence. But a story proves nothing. It only proves that something happened once.

The coworking story is beautiful. It talks about Julian, peeragogic insights, researching specific factors. But it doesn't say whether the project worked. It doesn't say whether Julian succeeded. The story is incomplete. It's a presumed happy ending. The reality of coworking is made of stories that don't end well. The Handbook tells those that end well. Or at least it doesn't tell those that end badly.

**What to do instead:** Stories are useful if they're complete. If they say what worked, what didn't, and what could be done differently. A story without failure is a fairy tale. Fairy tales don't teach. They entertain. The Handbook needs more real stories. With real failures. With open endings. With the dirt of practice.

> **Perturbator:** *"The coworking story is a story. Beautiful, inspiring, incomplete. You don't know how it ended. The Handbook doesn't say. Because maybe it didn't end well. Success stories are easy to tell. Failure stories are more useful. The Handbook has few failure stories. This is one of them. It just doesn't admit it."*
