### The Assumption

The coworking story illustrates how peeragogic principles can transform workplace dynamics through collaborative learning and shared decision-making, creating environments where traditional hierarchies dissolve into peer-based cooperation. The pattern can replicate across contexts.

### Operational Reality

Julian walks into the housing association boardroom with a folder of peeragogical principles and finds twelve people who need to cut £2.3 million from next year's budget. The government changed the benefits formula. Construction loans dried up. Three board members represent tenant groups who will lose services.

Not collaboration theory. Not consensus building. Not dissolved hierarchies.

The work happens in the space between what sounds good in principle and what survives contact with a spreadsheet. Julian's approach — research factors, draw themes together, find strategic options — assumes the luxury of exploration. But when you are managing 4,000 housing units and the funding model just collapsed, exploration feels like procrastination.

GitLab shows that transparent boundary documentation enables genuine peer participation even within asymmetric power structures. Signal demonstrates that the story of dissolved hierarchies can mask the reality of continued central control — even technically open projects can become single-vendor dependencies, creating governance illusions that collapse when the vendor changes direction.

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if beautiful stories about dissolved hierarchies do not pay the rent or decide who gets fired — and the most honest thing Julian could do is admit that some decisions cannot be distributed?"*

### Empirical Evidence

**Resilience Cases (Confirming):**
- [GitLab Stewardship Handbook](https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/) — Explicit documentation of governance boundaries between corporate sponsor and community reduces friction in open-core projects. Transparent boundary documentation enables genuine peer participation even within asymmetric power structures.

**Fracture Cases (Complicating):**
- [Signal Foundation Governance](https://signal.org/blog/signal-foundation/) — Single-vendor "open-source" creates governance dependency that contradicts peer governance principles, even when code is technically open. The story of dissolved hierarchies can mask the reality of continued central control.

### Operational Heuristics

**Positive Boundary:** Apply this pattern only when governance boundaries are documented explicitly before hierarchy dissolution begins — transparent boundary documentation enables genuine peer participation even in asymmetric power structures.

**Negative Boundary:** Do not apply this pattern in corporate-sponsored contexts without explicit governance documentation — undocumented power asymmetries reassert themselves as implicit control; the beautiful story of dissolved hierarchy becomes a trap.

### Epistemic Synthesis

The evidence challenges the replicability of the story. GitLab shows explicit governance works, but Signal shows the same model can be a facade. The housing association boardroom exists because someone must sign contracts and someone must be fired when things go wrong. Stories of dissolved hierarchies do not pay the rent and do not decide who gets fired. The open question is whether peer learning can coexist with the weight of institutional responsibility — or only works where the stakes are low.
