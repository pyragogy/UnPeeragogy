## Summary

Dissolved hierarchies reassert themselves as covert power when rent is due. Theory promises peer governance — practice delivers who-gets-fired decisions made behind closed doors. The gap between story and structure kills more cooperatives than market competition.

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern works when governance boundaries are explicitly documented before hierarchy dissolution begins. GitLab's stewardship model shows that transparent boundary documentation enables genuine peer-corporate collaboration across sustained periods.

### Negative Rule

> Do not apply this pattern in corporate-sponsored contexts without explicit governance documentation. GitLab's stewardship handbook shows that undocumented power asymmetries reassert themselves as covert hierarchy when formal structures are dissolved.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern claims that documented governance boundaries enable peer-corporate collaboration across sustained periods. Partial verification — when explicit stewardship frameworks exist upfront. GitLab's handbook shows transparent boundary documentation works, but Signal's single-vendor control reveals how 'open-source' becomes governance dependency dressed as peer cooperation. The mechanism missing: who enforces the boundaries when corporate interests shift. Beautiful stories about dissolved hierarchies don't survive the first budget cut.
