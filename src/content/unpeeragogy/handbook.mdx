## Summary

Handbooks document governance after it works, not before. Theory says peer learning needs philosophical grounding — practice shows it needs calendar invites first. The difference between a manual and a manifesto is who sends the meeting links.

## Evidenze Grounded

### 🔹 Confirming (2)

**Caso 1 — GitLab stewardship handbook**
- **Fenomeno:** Explicit documentation of governance boundaries between corporate sponsor and community reduces friction in open-core projects.
- **Fonte:** [https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/](https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/)

**Caso 2 — P2PU learning circles sustainability**
- **Fenomeno:** Peer learning models with defined facilitator roles and institutional partnerships are more sustainable than purely peer-driven learning.

### 🔸 Complicating (2)

- **Fenomeno:** Peer governance in federated systems creates governance externalities — admin decisions affect users who have no governance rights in that instance.
- **Fonte:** [https://docs.joinmastodon.org/admin/moderation/#federation](https://docs.joinmastodon.org/admin/moderation/#federation)

**Caso 2 — P2P University governance challenges**
- **Fenomeno:** Peer learning communities inevitably face credentialing and quality assurance crises that require either hierarchy or external validation frameworks.
- **Fonte:** [https://www.p2pu.org/en/about/](https://www.p2pu.org/en/about/)

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This handbook works when used as philosophical grounding after governance infrastructure is established. GitLab's stewardship model shows that explicit documentation of boundaries between different stakeholder groups enables sustainable peer collaboration over years.

### Negative Rule

> Do not apply this handbook's patterns without first establishing facilitator roles and governance boundaries. P2PU's experience shows that peer learning without institutional partnerships and defined roles leads to coordination collapse and sustainability crises.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern claims handbooks enable peer coordination through shared understanding. Partial verification — but only when governance infrastructure already exists. GitLab's stewardship boundaries and P2PU's facilitator roles prove documentation works after roles crystallize, while Mastodon's defederation chaos shows peer governance creates externalities that no handbook anticipates. The real mechanism isn't philosophical alignment but operational scaffolding. A handbook without someone willing to enforce it is just a very expensive bookmark.
