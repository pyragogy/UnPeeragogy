### Reality

The Peeragogy Handbook sits on my shelf next to *Getting Things Done* and three different guides to Scrum. All of them promise systematic approaches to collaboration. All of them gather dust while the real work happens in Signal threads and coffee conversations.

Version 3 exists. Version 4 is "in progress." The pattern repeats across every collaborative project I've watched — the handbook becomes the thing we're always about to finish, never the thing we use. Not because handbooks are useless. Not because documentation doesn't matter. Because we mistake the map for the territory, then wonder why following the directions doesn't get us there.

I've seen teams spend months perfecting their collaboration framework while their actual collaboration falls apart. The handbook becomes a beautiful artifact of how we think we work — not how we actually work when someone's sick, the deadline moves up, or the funding disappears.

> What if the handbook isn't supposed to capture how we collaborate, but expose where our collaboration breaks?

### Evidence That Is Ambiguous

**docs.joinmastodon.org** -- Peer governance in federated systems creates governance externalities — admin decisions affect users who have no governance rights in that instance.

**p2pu.org** -- Peer learning communities inevitably face credentialing and quality assurance crises that require either hierarchy or external validation frameworks.


### Synthesis

The evidence suggests something uncomfortable about peer learning infrastructure. Explicit governance reduces friction, but only when someone enforces it. Defined facilitator roles create sustainability, but only when institutions back them with actual resources.

The handbook pattern may work best as diagnostic tool rather than instruction manual. When the documented process diverges from lived practice, that gap reveals where the useful friction lives — the places where human judgment matters more than systematic approach.

What remains to be tested is whether handbooks can hold this tension deliberately, becoming less about perfect process and more about honest inventory of what breaks, what holds, what requires the human part we can't systematize.
