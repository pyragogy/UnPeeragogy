"The Peeragogy Handbook is a resource for peer learning." "It contains patterns, examples, and reflections."

**Why it matters:** A handbook suggests practical utility. "Take this and use it." But the Handbook is more theory than practice. The patterns are described, not tested. The examples are few. The reflections are meta.

Calling it a "handbook" is an act of branding, not description. It's a book. A dense one. Handbooks are quick references. This one requires weeks of reading. The label sets the wrong expectation. Users expect recipes. They get philosophy.

**What to do instead:** Be honest about what it is. It's a pattern language for peer learning. Not a handbook in the practical sense. Documenting patterns is valuable. But it's not the same as providing a tool. A tool works out of the box. A pattern language requires interpretation. Both are valid. But they're different. Use the right label.

> **Perturbator:** *"It's called a Handbook. It's 300+ pages of patterns, references, and meta-discussion. A handbook is a quick reference. This is not quick. This is also not a reference. It's a theory book wearing a handbook costume. The costume is misleading. It promises utility. It delivers complexity."*
