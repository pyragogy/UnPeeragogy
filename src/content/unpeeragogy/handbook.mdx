### Il Dogma

The Peeragogy Handbook presents itself as a practical guide for peer learning and collaboration, offering actionable patterns and methods for distributed knowledge creation. The implicit claim: follow the handbook and peer learning will work.

### La Realtà

The Peeragogy Handbook sits on my shelf next to *Getting Things Done* and three different guides to Scrum. All of them promise systematic approaches to collaboration. All of them gather dust while the real work happens in Signal threads and coffee conversations.

Version 3 exists. Version 4 is "in progress." The pattern repeats across every collaborative project I've watched — the handbook becomes the thing we're always about to finish, never the thing we use. Not because handbooks are useless. Not because documentation doesn't matter. Because we mistake the map for the territory, then wonder why following the directions doesn't get us there.

I've seen teams spend months perfecting their collaboration framework while their actual collaboration falls apart. The handbook becomes a beautiful artifact of how we think we work — not how we actually work when someone's sick, the deadline moves up, or the funding disappears.

### Perturbatore

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if peer learning without someone willing to send the calendar invites is just a book club that never meets — and the handbook isn't supposed to capture how we collaborate, but expose where our collaboration breaks?"*

### Evidenze di Tenuta

**about.gitlab.com** — Explicit documentation of governance boundaries between corporate sponsor and community reduces friction in open-core projects. GitLab shows that the handbook works when used as governance infrastructure, not philosophical grounding.

**p2pu.org** — Peer learning models with defined facilitator roles and institutional partnerships are more sustainable than purely peer-driven learning. P2PU demonstrates that institutional backing makes the handbook actionable.

### Evidenze di Faglia

**docs.joinmastodon.org** — Peer governance in federated systems creates governance externalities — admin decisions affect users who have no governance rights. Mastodon shows that even well-documented handbooks can't govern spillover effects.

**p2pu.org** — Peer learning communities inevitably face credentialing and quality assurance crises that require hierarchy or external validation. P2PU's own challenges show that handbooks can't resolve structural tensions — they can only document them.

### Euristiche Operative

**Regola Positiva:** Il manuale funziona quando usato come grounding filosofico dopo che l'infrastruttura di governance è stata stabilita. GitLab mostra che la documentazione esplicita dei confini tra stakeholder riduce l'attrito — ma solo quando qualcuno la enforce.

**Regola Negativa:** Non applicare i pattern del manuale senza prima stabilire ruoli di facilitazione e confini di governance. P2PU mostra che il peer learning senza partnership istituzionali e ruoli definiti non è sostenibile — il manuale da solo non basta.

**Stato:** PROVVISORIO

La governance esplicita riduce l'attrito, ma solo quando qualcuno la enforce. I ruoli di facilitazione definiti creano sostenibilità, ma solo quando le istituzioni li sostengono con risorse reali. Il manuale funziona meglio come strumento diagnostico che come istruzione — quando il processo documentato diverge dalla pratica vissuta, quel gap rivela dove vive l'attrito utile. I posti dove il giudizio umano conta più dell'approccio sistematico.
