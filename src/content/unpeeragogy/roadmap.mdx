"Here is a rough outline of what we need." "And here's another view." "Roadmap."

The Roadmap pattern: mapping the direction of the project.

**Why it matters:** A roadmap is a promise. "We'll go there." The promise is useful if it's kept. If not, the roadmap becomes a record of broken promises. The Handbook has a roadmap. It's been updated through versions. Each version adds new promises.

The problem with roadmaps is that they're directional. They say where you want to go, not how you'll get there. The "how" is the hard part. The "where" is the easy part. Groups create beautiful roadmaps and never start the journey. Because starting requires leaving. And leaving means abandoning the plan that felt safe.

**What to do instead:** A roadmap should have milestones. Not just direction. A direction without milestones is a dream. A milestone is a concrete, measurable output. "By June, we have published three case studies." That's a milestone. "Understand peer learning better" is not a milestone. It's a wish.

> **Perturbator:** *"Roadmap: 'We want to finish the patterns by the end of the year.' The end of the year arrives. Patterns aren't finished. What happens? Nothing. The roadmap is updated. The promise is deferred. A roadmap without consequences is a fantasy calendar. It organizes nothing. It just postpones the discomfort of not delivering."*
