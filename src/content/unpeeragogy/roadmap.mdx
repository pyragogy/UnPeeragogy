### The Assumption

Groups can create shared direction through collaborative planning, where milestones emerge from collective input and commitment builds through transparent progress tracking.

### Operational Reality

A roadmap without owners is just a wish list with dates.

The vault records show roadmap failures: broken promises, milestone-free planning, beautiful roadmaps that never translated to actual direction. Not because the planning was not thorough. Because roadmap creation is collaborative but roadmap ownership is individual. Someone must own each milestone.

Debian's stable release process works because it has defined release managers and explicit policies — governance that distributes ownership along with dates. GitLab's stewardship handbook documents who owns which decisions. Wikipedia's ArbCom shows that shared governance without individual ownership produces contested roadmaps that satisfy no one. The cMOOC divide demonstrates that collaborative planning without structure produces direction statements too vague to disagree with — and too vague to follow.

### Empirical Evidence

**Resilience Cases (Confirming):**
- [Debian Stable Release Process](https://www.debian.org/doc/debian-policy/) — Debian's stable release process with defined release managers and explicit policies. Roadmap ownership along with dates enables successful collaborative planning.
- [GitLab Stewardship Handbook](https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/) — Explicit documentation of governance boundaries between corporate sponsor and community reduces friction. Documenting who owns which decisions enables roadmap execution.

**Fracture Cases (Complicating):**
- [Wikipedia Arbitration Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arbitration_Committee_(Wikimedia)) — Shared governance without individual ownership produces contested roadmaps that satisfy no one.
- [cMOOC vs xMOOC Pedagogical Governance](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/259999056_Connectivist_MOOCs_and_xMOOCs) — Collaborative planning without structure produces direction statements too vague to disagree with — and too vague to follow.

### Operational Heuristics

**Positive Boundary:** The roadmap works when governance boundaries are explicit and roles are defined before planning begins — transparent policies and documented ownership enable effective collaborative planning.

**Negative Boundary:** Do not apply this pattern when the group exceeds informal coordination limits or when domain complexity requires specialized decision-making — shared governance without individual ownership produces contested roadmaps.

### Epistemic Synthesis

Roadmap creation is collaborative. Roadmap ownership is individual. Debian has defined release managers. GitLab documents who owns which decisions. Wikipedia's ArbCom shows that shared governance without individual ownership produces contested roadmaps. The cMOOC divide shows that collaborative planning without structure produces statements too vague to follow. A roadmap without owners is just a wish list with dates.
