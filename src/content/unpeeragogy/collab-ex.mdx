## Summary

Collaborative exploration generates process, not outcomes. Theory promises shared discovery through structured inquiry — practice delivers governance theater. The real work happens when someone decides the deciding is done.

## Evidenze Grounded

### 🔹 Confirming (2)

**Caso 1 — KDE Community Council governance**
- **Fenomeno:** Elected peer governance councils with defined authority and transparent records provide stable long-term community governance.
- **Fonte:** [https://community.kde.org/Community_Council](https://community.kde.org/Community_Council)

- **Fenomeno:** Formal dispute resolution bodies with binding authority and published reasoning resolve peer production conflicts that informal consensus cannot.
- **Fonte:** [https://wiki.osmfoundation.org/wiki/Data_Working_Group](https://wiki.osmfoundation.org/wiki/Data_Working_Group)

- **Fenomeno:** DAO governance mechanisms designed for normal operations can fail catastrophically under stress, requiring emergency governance that existing processes don't cover.

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern works when formal authority structures with defined scope and transparent processes govern the exploration domain. The KDE Community Council and OpenStreetMap Data Working Group show that elected bodies with binding authority can successfully conclude exploration phases without community fracture.

### Negative Rule

> Do not apply this pattern when exploration touches contested governance domains or technical decisions with political implications. The Debian init system transition shows that time-boxed exploration becomes a governance crisis when authority to conclude exploration is disputed.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern claims time-boxed peer exploration leads to collective learning and discovery. Partial verification — but only when authority structures are already settled. KDE's Community Council and OpenStreetMap's Data Working Group work because elected bodies can close exploration loops with binding decisions, while Debian's systemd transition shows how exploration becomes permanent crisis when no one has authority to conclude. The mechanism missing from the theory: exploration without exit authority isn't learning, it's performance. Democracy has no natural stopping point.
