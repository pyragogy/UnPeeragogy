"Here is a step-by-step guide to getting started with peeragogy." The Quickstart Guide is an attempt to simplify entry.

**Why it matters:** The Quickstart Guide is one of the most useful pages in the Handbook. Because it doesn't theorize. It tells you what to do. Step by step. It's short. It's direct.

The problem is that following the guide requires the group to move fast. "Form a group. Identify a common interest. Start a project." Fast for a theory. Slow for practice. Real groups don't form from a guide. They form from a need. The guide gives steps. It doesn't create the need. Without a need, the steps are theoretical.

**What to do instead:** The Quickstart Guide should be the first page of the Handbook. Not a buried resource. If peeragogy is a practice, it should start with practice. The guide should come before the theory. But the Handbook is structured inversely. Theory first. Practice later. The Quickstart Guide is a resource, not the opening. That's backward.

> **Perturbator:** *"The Quickstart Guide tells you what to do step by step. It's one of the most useful pages in the Handbook. And it's buried in Resources. Why isn't the Quickstart Guide the first page? Because the Handbook prioritizes explanation over instruction. That's the academic bias. Theory first. Practice later. In reality, it should be the opposite."*
