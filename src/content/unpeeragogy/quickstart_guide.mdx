## Summary

Fast group formation works like fast food — fills you up quickly, leaves you hungry later. Theory says organic emergence builds community. Practice shows explicit governance boundaries prevent years of structural debt. GitLab's stewardship handbook proves documentation beats emergence for productive collaboration.

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern works when governance boundaries are explicitly documented upfront. Evidence from GitLab's stewardship handbook shows that clear documentation of authority boundaries between different stakeholders reduces friction and enables rapid productive collaboration.

### Negative Rule

> Do not apply this pattern for ongoing collaborative projects or work requiring quality validation. Evidence from Drupal's governance transition shows that community-based work without explicit authority structures leads to years of structural debt that must eventually be paid.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern claims rapid group formation through minimal structure creates sustainable collaboration. Partial verification — it works for short-term projects with clear endpoints. GitLab's explicit governance boundaries reduce friction in open-core development, while Drupal's organic transition required years of structural work to escape BDFL debt. Linux's Reviewed-by tags formalize peer assessment but reveal the accountability gaps that emerge when collaboration scales. The real mechanism isn't emergence versus structure — it's timing the transition from speed to sustainability.
