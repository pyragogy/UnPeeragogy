### Claim

The Quickstart Guide presents a structured approach for rapid peer learning group formation, emphasizing immediate action over extended planning phases. It assumes groups can quickly establish working relationships and begin productive collaboration without extensive governance setup.

### Reality

The quickstart guide promises immediate results. Click here, follow these steps, get productive in minutes. But watch what actually happens when someone opens your project's README at 2 AM, coffee cold, deadline tomorrow. They scan the installation steps, hit the first error, check Stack Overflow, find three contradictory answers from different years. Twenty minutes later they're installing Docker because someone said it might help.

Not the smooth onboarding you designed. Not the frictionless experience you tested on your own machine. The quickstart guide works perfectly — for you, with your specific setup, your accumulated context, your muscle memory of which steps the documentation skips because "everyone knows that part."

The gap widens with every assumption. Your guide assumes they have the right version of Python, that their PATH is configured correctly, that they understand the difference between development and production environments. Each assumption is a trapdoor waiting to open under someone who just wants to see if your thing works.

> What if the quickstart guide's real job isn't to get people started quickly, but to expose exactly where your project breaks for newcomers?

### Synthesis

These installation failures suggest something useful about community formation. The person who struggles through your broken setup instructions and still contributes a pull request — they've already demonstrated persistence under friction. They've shown they can work with incomplete information, debug problems you didn't anticipate, fill gaps in your documentation.

The quickstart guide becomes an accidental filter. Not for technical skill, but for tolerance of useful friction. The contributors who emerge from installation hell often become the ones who improve your onboarding process, who remember what it felt like to be lost in your assumptions. They've earned their understanding through struggle — and that changes how they help others navigate the same territory.
