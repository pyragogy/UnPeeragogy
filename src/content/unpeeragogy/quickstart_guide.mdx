### The Assumption

The Quickstart Guide presents a structured approach for rapid peer learning group formation, emphasizing immediate action over extended planning phases. Groups can quickly establish working relationships and begin productive collaboration without extensive governance setup.

### Operational Reality

Fast group formation is like fast food — it fills you up quickly but leaves you hungry an hour later.

The assumption that groups can skip governance setup and go straight to collaboration is the most expensive shortcut in peer learning. GitLab's stewardship handbook shows that explicit governance boundaries reduce friction — but only when documented upfront. Drupal's governance transition took years precisely because initial formation skipped the structural work. The Linux kernel's Reviewed-by system shows that even technical communities need formal quality mechanisms that quickstart guides do not mention.

Quickstart works for a conversation. Not for a project that needs to survive its first disagreement.

### Empirical Evidence

**Resilience Cases (Confirming):**
- [GitLab Stewardship Handbook](https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/) — Explicit documentation of governance boundaries between corporate sponsor and community reduces friction in open-core projects. Upfront governance documentation is essential even in quick-start contexts.

**Fracture Cases (Complicating):**
- [Drupal Governance](https://www.drupal.org/governance) — Intentional governance transition from BDFL to community model required years of structural work. Skipping governance setup produces long-term costs that exceed any short-term gain.
- [Linux Kernel Submitting Patches](https://docs.kernel.org/process/submitting-patches.html) — Formal peer assessment accountability through tooling reveals review scarcity as the bottleneck. Even rapid technical projects need formal quality mechanisms that quickstart guides omit.

### Operational Heuristics

**Positive Boundary:** Rapid formation works when governance boundaries are documented explicitly in advance — clear documentation of authority boundaries between different stakeholders reduces friction even in quick-start contexts.

**Negative Boundary:** Do not apply this pattern for sustained collaborative projects or work that requires quality validation — community work without explicit authority structures produces long-term governance costs that exceed any short-term gain.

### Epistemic Synthesis

The assumption that groups can skip governance setup and go straight to collaboration is the most expensive shortcut in peer learning. Drupal's transition took years precisely because initial formation skipped the structural work. Quickstart works for a conversation. Not for a project that needs to survive its first disagreement.
