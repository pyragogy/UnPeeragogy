### Il Dogma

Groups should focus on discerning patterns rather than rushing to apply them, emphasizing careful observation and analysis before action. The theory assumes that recognizing patterns — building vocabulary for what's happening — is the foundation of effective peer practice.

### La Realtà

The Rust community documented their RFC process in 2014. Clear stages, public discussion, explicit decision points — a pattern for technical governance that dozens of projects copied. Mozilla's engineers had spent months watching feature requests pile up in GitHub issues while core contributors burned out making ad-hoc decisions in private channels.

Not pattern recognition. Not emergence. Deliberate structural work.

The Apache Software Foundation runs 350+ projects under identical governance templates. Same voting procedures, same contributor agreements, same legal frameworks. Every project gets the "Apache Way" handbook on day one. This isn't organic pattern discovery — it's institutional replication of what works at scale.

Compare this to the graveyard of "self-organizing" developer communities that never shipped anything. They recognized patterns everywhere. Meeting rhythms, decision-making styles, conflict resolution approaches. Perfect ethnographic observers of their own dysfunction — but the code stayed broken.

### Perturbatore

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if pattern recognition is the consolation prize for groups that can't ship — and you can pattern-spot your way to beautiful descriptions of a project that never delivers?"*

### Evidenze di Tenuta

**about.gitlab.com** — Explicit documentation of governance boundaries between corporate sponsor and community reduces friction in open-core projects. GitLab shows that pattern recognition combined with explicit implementation frameworks produces sustainable governance.

**en.wikipedia.org** — Peer assessment with transparent criteria and independent reviewers creates sustainable quality tiers in open communities. Wikipedia's Good Article process demonstrates that patterns work when embedded in enforceable structures.

### Evidenze di Faglia

**drupal.org** — Intentional governance transition from BDFL to community model required years of structural work, not organic emergence. Drupal shows that pattern recognition without implementation frameworks produces observation without action.

### Euristiche Operative

**Regola Positiva:** Applica questo pattern quando il riconoscimento dei pattern è accoppiato a framework di applicazione espliciti. GitLab e Wikipedia dimostrano che combinare pattern recognition con strutture decisionali produce governance sostenibile.

**Regola Negativa:** Non applicare questo pattern quando al gruppo mancano strutture decisionali esplicite o framework di implementazione. Drupal mostra che l'emergenza organica dal pattern recognition non produce governance — solo descrizioni sempre più accurate della propria disfunzione.

### Sintesi & Tracciabilità

**Riferimento:** audit-v2 (delta tensione: 1.5 → 1.4417, −0.0583)
**Evidenze esaminate:** 4 (2 confermanti, 1 complicante, 1 ambigua)
**Stato:** PROVVISORIO

Il riconoscimento dei pattern serve due funzioni distinte che i gruppi spesso confondono. La documentazione crea memoria istituzionale — il processo Rust RFC sopravvive al turnover dei contributori perché vive fuori dalla testa di ogni individuo. Ma il pattern recognition senza implementazione diventa un sostituto del lavoro più duro di costruire sistemi che persistono. La tensione è temporale: le comunità sane alternano periodi di conscious pattern-building a periodi di deliberata ignoranza dei pattern per consegnare qualcosa di concreto.
