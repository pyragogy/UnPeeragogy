"The classic example of an architectural pattern is 'a place to wait.'" Discerning a Pattern: recognizing when a behavior repeats and formalizing it.

**Why it matters:** Recognizing a pattern is only the first step. But the Handbook presents it as if it were the whole process. "Here's a pattern." Then what? How is it applied? How do you verify that it works? A recognized but unapplied pattern is an intellectual exercise. Not a practical tool.

The problem with discerning is that it's an activity groups enjoy. "We identified a pattern." It's a sentence that makes people feel smart. But it produces no change. Groups can spend months discerning patterns without applying a single one. Pattern collection becomes an end. Not a means.

**What to do instead:** Discerning is 10% of the work. The other 90% is application, verification, revision. The Handbook gives a lot of space to discerning. Very little to applying. Maybe because application is boring. Maybe because it often fails. But without application, patterns are literature. Not tools. And peeragogy needs tools, not literature.

> **Perturbator:** *"You've identified a pattern. Good. Now apply it. If you don't apply it, it's not a pattern. It's an observation. Observations are easy. Application is hard. Your group has observed a lot. How much has it applied? The difference between a group that discerns and a group that practices is the difference between reading a map and walking. The map doesn't tire you. Walking does."*
