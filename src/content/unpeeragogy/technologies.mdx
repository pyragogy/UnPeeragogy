### The Assumption

Peer-driven technology selection where communities collectively evaluate and adopt tools based on shared needs rather than top-down mandates or vendor influence.

### Operational Reality

Democracy is two wolves and a sheep voting on what to have for lunch. Peer technology selection is five developers and a designer voting on whether to rewrite everything in Rust.

GitLab's stewardship handbook shows that explicit boundaries between corporate sponsor and community make technology selection less contested — not because the right tool is chosen, but because it is clear who decides. Mastodon's defederation mechanics show that peer technology choices create externalities for non-participants — the group that chooses ActivityPub imposes protocol decisions on everyone it interacts with. The Linux kernel's Reviewed-by system shows that technology selection without formal peer review produces quality variation.

The problem is not technology selection method. It is that technology decisions have externalities that peer voting cannot contain.

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if democracy is two wolves and a sheep voting on lunch — and peer technology selection is five developers and a designer voting on whether to rewrite everything in Rust?"*

### Empirical Evidence

**Resilience Cases (Confirming):**
- [GitLab Stewardship Handbook](https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/) — Explicit documentation of governance boundaries between corporate sponsor and community reduces friction. Explicit authority boundaries make technology selection less contested.

**Fracture Cases (Complicating):**
- [Mastodon Defederation](https://docs.joinmastodon.org/admin/moderation/#federation) — Peer technology choices create externalities for non-participants — the group that chooses ActivityPub imposes protocol decisions on everyone it interacts with.
- [Linux Kernel Submitting Patches](https://docs.kernel.org/process/submitting-patches.html) — Technology selection without formal peer review produces quality variation. Peer technology evaluation requires formal mechanisms.

### Operational Heuristics

**Positive Boundary:** Peer technology selection works when governance boundaries are explicit and peer input is bounded by clear authority structures — explicit boundaries between stakeholders make technology selection less contested.

**Negative Boundary:** Do not apply this pattern when technology choices create externalities for non-participants or when decision-making authority is fully distributed — peer technology choices have consequences that voting cannot contain.

### Epistemic Synthesis

The problem is not the technology selection method. It is that technology decisions have externalities that peer voting cannot contain. GitLab shows that explicit authority boundaries reduce contestation. Mastodon shows that peer technology choices have consequences beyond the voting group. Linux shows that peer technology evaluation requires formal mechanisms.
