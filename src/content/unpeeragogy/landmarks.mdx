"Landmarks are a way of documenting the patterns we see." "Developing a shared language."

Landmarks are the common reference points a group uses to orient itself. "Remember when we talked about X? That's landmark."

**Why it matters:** Landmarks are useful — for those who were there. For everyone else, they're noise. A group that has been together for months has dozens of landmarks. "Remember the Blob? Remember the server crash? Remember when Joe said...?" Each landmark is a story that not everyone has heard. The more landmarks, the harder it is for newcomers.

Landmarks work as cohesion tools for veterans. They're entry barriers for newcomers. Every landmark is a password that separates those who were there from those who arrived later. The group becomes a club with secret handshakes. Handshakes that must be learned. And the list to learn keeps growing.

**What to do instead:** If you use landmarks, document them. Write down what they refer to, what they mean, why they matter. An undocumented landmark excludes. A documented landmark orients. The Handbook documents landmarks in the patterns. But the real landmarks — the ones that formed during the writing — haven't been documented. And they're the most important ones.

> **Perturbator:** *"'Remember the Blob?' You say it. Everyone who was there smiles. Everyone else feels excluded. That's what a landmark does. It unites and divides. It tells who belongs and who doesn't. Landmarks are the walls of the group. Beautiful to those inside. Impenetrable to those outside."*
