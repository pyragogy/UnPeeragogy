### Il Dogma

Shared reference points help groups navigate complex discussions and maintain coherence. These landmarks serve as anchors for collective understanding and enable productive peer learning by providing common ground for participants.

### La Realtà

The P2PU course organizers discovered something most peer learning advocates miss. Their best experiences happened when they stopped trying to organize and started pointing instead. Not at curriculum. Not at outcomes. At the moments when someone else stepped forward.

You can document governance boundaries all you want — the real landmarks emerge when people test what actually holds under pressure. Complex processes designed to include everyone end up serving the people who already know how to navigate complexity. Lighter models trade one kind of failure for another. The friction doesn't disappear. It just moves.

What the organizers learned wasn't theory. It was recognition — that the thing you're pointing at might not be the thing people need to see.

### Perturbatore

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if your landmarks are someone else's barriers — and barriers don't care about your good intentions?"*

### Evidenze di Tenuta

**about.gitlab.com** — Explicit documentation of governance boundaries between corporate sponsor and community reduces friction in open-core projects. GitLab demonstrates that transparent boundary documentation enables productive participation when landmarks are explicit and accessible.

### Evidenze di Faglia

**wikidata.org** — Complex governance processes in peer production projects create participation barriers that entrench existing contributors and exclude newcomers. Wikidata shows that landmarks designed without accessibility testing become obstacles, not anchors.

**w3.org** — Choosing lighter governance models for standards development trades authority for speed, creating different failure modes for peer participation. WebAssembly demonstrates that every landmark choice creates a corresponding failure mode.

### Euristiche Operative

**Regola Positiva:** I landmark funzionano quando i confini di governance sono documentati esplicitamente e accessibili a tutti i partecipanti. GitLab dimostra che la documentazione trasparente dei confini riduce l'attrito e permette partecipazione produttiva.

**Regola Negativa:** Non applicare questo pattern quando il controllo dei landmark rimane concentrato tra i membri esistenti. Wikidata dimostra che sistemi di landmark complessi e non documentati creano barriere di partecipazione che radicano i veterani ed escludono i nuovi arrivati.

### Sintesi & Tracciabilità

**Riferimento:** audit-v2 (delta tensione: 1.4 → 1.5217, +0.1217)

**Stato:** PROVVISORIO

Più rendiamo espliciti i confini, più radichiamo le persone che già capiscono come costruire confini. La governance che riduce l'attrito per gli insider può generare attrito diverso per tutti gli altri. La domanda aperta: se i landmark possono servire la navigazione senza diventare barriere — se la cosa che aiuta a indicare la strada può resistere al diventare la cosa che la blocca.
