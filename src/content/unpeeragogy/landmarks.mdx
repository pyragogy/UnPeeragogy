## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern works when landmark governance boundaries are explicitly documented and accessible to all participants. GitLab's stewardship handbook demonstrates that transparent boundary documentation reduces friction and enables productive participation across different stakeholder groups.

### Negative Rule

> Do not apply this pattern when landmark control remains concentrated among existing members. Wikidata governance disputes show that complex, undocumented landmark systems create participation barriers that entrench veterans and exclude newcomers.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern claims explicit landmarks reduce navigation friction in peer groups. Partial verification — when boundaries are documented like GitLab's stewardship handbook, participation barriers drop. But Wikidata's governance maze and WebAssembly's committee structures show landmarks can entrench existing power rather than distribute it. The real mechanism isn't documentation — it's who gets to define what counts as a landmark. Someone always holds the map.
