### The Assumption

Shared reference points help groups navigate complex discussions and maintain coherence. Landmarks serve as anchors for collective understanding and enable productive peer learning by providing common ground for participants.

### Operational Reality

P2PU course organizers discovered something most peer learning advocates miss. Their best experiences happened when they stopped trying to organize and started pointing instead. Not at curriculum. Not at outcomes. At the moments when someone else stepped forward.

You can document governance boundaries all you want — the real landmarks emerge when people test what actually holds under pressure. Complex processes designed to include everyone end up serving the people who already know how to navigate complexity. Lighter models trade one kind of failure for another. The friction does not disappear. It just moves.

GitLab demonstrates that transparent boundary documentation enables productive participation when landmarks are explicit and accessible. Wikidata shows that landmarks designed without accessibility testing become obstacles, not anchors. WebAssembly demonstrates that every landmark choice creates a corresponding failure mode.

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if your landmarks are someone else's barriers — and barriers do not care about your good intentions?"*

### Empirical Evidence

**Resilience Cases (Confirming):**
- [GitLab Stewardship Handbook](https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/) — Explicit documentation of governance boundaries between corporate sponsor and community reduces friction in open-core projects. Transparent boundary documentation enables productive participation when landmarks are explicit and accessible.

**Fracture Cases (Complicating):**
- [Wikidata Governance](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Wikidata:Governance) — Complex governance processes in peer production projects create participation barriers that entrench existing contributors and exclude newcomers. Landmarks designed without accessibility testing become obstacles, not anchors.
- [WebAssembly Community Group](https://www.w3.org/community/webassembly/) — Choosing lighter governance models for standards development trades authority for speed, creating different failure modes. Every landmark choice creates a corresponding failure mode.

### Operational Heuristics

**Positive Boundary:** Landmarks function when governance boundaries are documented explicitly and accessible to all participants — transparent boundary documentation reduces friction and enables productive participation.

**Negative Boundary:** Do not apply this pattern when landmark control remains concentrated among existing members — complex undocumented landmark systems create participation barriers that entrench veterans and exclude newcomers.

### Epistemic Synthesis

The more explicit we make the boundaries, the more we entrench people who already understand how to build boundaries. Governance that reduces friction for insiders can generate different friction for everyone else. The open question: whether landmarks can serve navigation without becoming barriers — whether the thing that helps point the way can resist becoming the thing that blocks it.
