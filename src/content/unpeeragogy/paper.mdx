### The Assumption

Academic papers are inefficient for spreading ideas compared to open-source projects. The handbook prioritizes theory over practice, limiting its effectiveness. Papers are read by those already familiar with peeragogy, limiting outreach.

### Operational Reality

GitLab's stewardship handbook is the most-cited document in this entire audit. Not because it is peer-reviewed. Not because it is published in a prestigious journal. Not because it advances theory. Because it documents operational boundaries that people need to navigate their actual work.

Compare that to the academic papers on peer learning. The theories are elegant. The citations are thorough. The contribution to knowledge is real. And nobody building a community garden or starting a co-learning group reads them.

The gap is not between theory and practice. It is between documentation that answers "what should I do on Monday morning?" and documentation that explains "why this matters in general." The Linux kernel's Reviewed-by tag system is a paper-worthy innovation — but its real impact comes from being embedded in a tool that enforces it, not from being read. Debian shows that communication practices embedded in governance structures matter more than abstract models of peer coordination.

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if papers do not kill ideas — bad papers kill ideas — and good papers kill bad governance, but only if someone actually reads them before the crisis hits?"*

### Empirical Evidence

**Resilience Cases (Confirming):**
- [GitLab Stewardship Handbook](https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/) — Explicit documentation of governance boundaries between corporate sponsor and community reduces friction in open-core projects. Operational documentation is more effective than theoretical exposition for changing how people collaborate.

**Fracture Cases (Complicating):**
- [Linux Kernel Reviewed-by Tag System](https://docs.kernel.org/process/submitting-patches.html) — Formal peer assessment accountability through tooling reveals review scarcity as the bottleneck. Operational documentation with enforcement mechanisms outperforms theoretical papers.
- [Debian DPL Bits Newsletter](https://lists.debian.org/debian-devel-announce/) — Regular leadership summaries in peer-governed projects create agenda-setting power. Communication practices embedded in governance structures matter more than abstract models of peer coordination.

### Operational Heuristics

**Positive Boundary:** The pattern works when documentation serves operational boundary-setting rather than theoretical exposition — formal documentation reduces friction when it clarifies governance boundaries, not when it expands theory.

**Negative Boundary:** Do not apply this pattern when documentation concerns boundary-setting rather than theory — the Reviewed-by system shows that formal peer assessment creates accountability but reveals queuing problems that papers alone cannot solve.

### Epistemic Synthesis

Papers are less efficient than open projects for spreading ideas not because theory is useless, but because the medium privileges completeness over operability. GitLab's stewardship handbook is more cited than any peer-reviewed paper on open source not because it is more true, but because it answers the question practitioners have at 9 AM on Monday. The tension is not between theory and practice, but between documentation that serves those who operate and documentation that serves those who publish.
