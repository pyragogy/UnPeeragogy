"GETTING STARTED WITH PEERAGOGY." "A conference paper."

A paper about peeragogy. Presented at a conference. One paper. Among thousands.

**Why it matters:** Academic papers are the currency of academia. They're also the most inefficient way to spread an idea. A paper reaches hundreds of people. An open-source project reaches thousands. A practice reaches those who practice it.

The Handbook is full of academic references. It has a section of Resources. But the resources are almost exclusively textual. Books, papers, bibliographies. The Handbook is an academic document about a practice. It suffers from the same bias it criticizes: privileging theory over practice.

**What to do instead:** Papers are fine for tenure. For spreading peeragogy, you need examples, tools, stories. Not more text about text. If you want to spread peeragogy, create something people can use. Not something they can cite.

> **Perturbator:** *"You wrote a paper about peeragogy. Good. Now look at the citation count. Low, right? Because papers about peeragogy are read by people who already know peeragogy. The paper isn't spreading the practice. It's feeding the theory. And the theory doesn't need more food. The practice needs more practitioners."*

**Reference:** Peeragogy Handbook, "Paper" section (Resources).
