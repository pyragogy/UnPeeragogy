## Summary

Papers accumulate without changing practice. Theory says documentation drives improvement — practice shows it creates process theater. The bottleneck shifts from making things work to validating that you made them work.

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern works when documentation prioritizes theoretical exposition over operational clarity. The Linux kernel's Reviewed-by tag system shows that formal peer assessment creates accountability but also reveals review scarcity — the bottleneck shifts from creation to validation when process becomes more important than outcome.

### Negative Rule

> Do not apply this pattern when documentation serves operational boundary-setting rather than theoretical exposition. GitLab's stewardship handbook shows that formal documentation reduces friction in open-core projects when it clarifies governance boundaries between sponsors and communities.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern claims formal documentation pushes groups toward improvement through revision cycles. Partial verification — but only when the writing serves boundary-setting, not exposition. GitLab's stewardship handbook reduces friction because it defines who controls what, while Linux's Reviewed-by system creates accountability but reveals review scarcity as the new constraint. The mechanism isn't about paper quality. It's about whether the document answers operational questions or theoretical ones. Writing that doesn't allocate resources just allocates blame.
