## Summary

Scrapbooks become permanent junk drawers. Theory says temporary storage — practice shows accumulation zones that never get sorted. The difference is taxonomic authority: who decides what belongs where.

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern works when explicit governance boundaries define who has authority to revise organizational structure. GitLab's stewardship handbook shows that clear authority boundaries enable smooth organizational evolution and prevent accumulation zones from becoming permanent fixtures.

### Negative Rule

> Do not implement scrapbooks when taxonomic authority is unclear or contested. The pattern assumes natural organizational evolution, but evidence shows that without explicit governance boundaries, scrapbooks become permanent junk drawers rather than temporary holding spaces.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern claims scrapbooks enable natural organizational evolution through temporary categorization. Partial verification — it works when governance boundaries are explicit, like GitLab's stewardship handbook defining corporate versus community authority. But Drupal's governance transition shows that structural reorganization requires years of intentional work, not organic emergence from accumulated materials. The real mechanism isn't temporary storage but contested authority over classification systems. Good intentions pile up where no one has permission to throw things away.
