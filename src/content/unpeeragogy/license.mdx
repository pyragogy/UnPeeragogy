## Summary

Open licenses protect communities from their own success. Theory says freedom through code. Practice shows power through governance — GitLab's stewardship handbook works because it names who decides what.

## Evidenze Grounded

### 🔹 Confirming (2)

**Caso 1 — GitLab stewardship handbook**
- **Fenomeno:** Explicit documentation of governance boundaries between corporate sponsor and community reduces friction in open-core projects.
- **Fonte:** [https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/](https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/)

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern works when license choice is paired with explicit governance documentation and formal dispute resolution mechanisms. GitLab's stewardship handbook and OpenStreetMap's Data Working Group (ongoing) show that transparent power boundaries and binding conflict resolution can sustain open communities across corporate-community tensions.

### Negative Rule

> Do not assume open licensing solves community sustainability when governance power remains concentrated and opaque. React's patent clause controversy (2017) and Terraform's license change (2023) show that license control without transparent governance creates exploitative relationships that eventually trigger community revolt.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern claims licensing creates sustainable peer production through legal protection. Partial verification — but only when paired with explicit governance boundaries and formal dispute resolution. GitLab's stewardship documentation and OpenStreetMap's Data Working Group show transparent power structures can sustain communities, while React's patent clause controversy and Terraform's license change reveal how concentrated control undermines open licensing promises. The real mechanism isn't legal protection but political accountability. Licenses are constitutions that only work when someone enforces them.
