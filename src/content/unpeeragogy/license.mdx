### Il Dogma

The license is a means, not an end; sharing knowledge is the goal. Open licenses protect content but not the community's sustainability.

### La Realtà

The Peeragogy Handbook carries no copyright. CC0 — complete surrender to the public domain. This isn't generosity. It's recognition that collaborative work already belongs to everyone who touched it, and claiming ownership over collective intelligence is both impossible and dishonest.

Watch what happens when projects try to maintain control while inviting contribution. Redis changed its license in 2018, pushing commercial users toward paid versions. The community didn't just complain — they forked. Valkey emerged with backing from AWS, Google, Oracle. Not because the code was better, but because the license respected what contributors had built together. The original project now competes with its own offspring.

Not ideology. Not principle. Not some abstract commitment to openness.

The CC0 choice admits what most collaborative projects refuse to acknowledge — once you invite real participation, you've already lost control. The question isn't whether to give up ownership, but whether to do it honestly or pretend you still have it while the work happens anyway.

### Perturbatore

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if open licenses are like constitutions — they only work if someone's willing to enforce them, and enforcement is always political?"*

### Evidenze di Tenuta

**about.gitlab.com** — Explicit documentation of governance boundaries between corporate sponsor and community reduces friction in open-core projects. GitLab shows that pairing license choice with documented governance stabilizes open communities.

**wiki.osmfoundation.org** — Formal dispute resolution bodies with binding authority and published reasoning resolve peer production conflicts that informal consensus cannot. OpenStreetMap demonstrates that license enforcement requires institutional mechanisms.

### Evidenze di Faglia

**code.facebook.com** — Corporate-governed open-source can alienate the community through license terms, and community pressure can force governance reversals. React's patent clause controversy (2017) shows that license terms without community consent create fragility.

**opentofu.org** — License changes away from open-source can unite competitors into a fork, demonstrating that ecosystem dependency creates collective bargaining power. Terraform's license change (2023) shows that license unilateralism triggers structural resistance.

### Euristiche Operative

**Regola Positiva:** La licenza funziona quando abbinata a documentazione esplicita di governance e meccanismi formali di risoluzione dispute. GitLab e OpenStreetMap mostrano che confini di potere trasparenti e risoluzione dispute vincolante stabilizzano le comunità open.

**Regola Negativa:** Non assumere che la licenza open risolva la sostenibilità della comunità quando il potere di governance rimane concentrato e opaco. React e Terraform mostrano che il controllo della licenza senza governance decisionale trasparente produce fork e alienazione.

### Sintesi & Tracciabilità

**Riferimento:** audit-v2 (delta tensione: 1.0 → 1.0619, +0.0619)
**Evidenze esaminate:** 4 (2 confermanti, 2 complicanti)
**Stato:** PROVVISORIO

La licenza formale diventa autoprotezione quando corrisponde alle dinamiche di potere reali del lavoro collaborativo. Il fork di Redis dimostra che le comunità detengono più leverage di quanto i fondatori assumano — ma solo quando il framework legale permette loro di esercitarlo. La tensione utile: se CC0 crei relazioni collaborative più forti delle licenze protettive, o semplicemente renda le lotte di potere più oneste.
