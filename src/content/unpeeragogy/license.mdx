The Handbook is CC0. "Released to the public domain." "You can do whatever you want." "But we ask that you cite the source."

**Why it matters:** Licensing is the group's relationship with the outside world. Open licenses are one of the foundational pillars of peeragogy. And they're used as self-protection: "we're open, therefore we're good." The license becomes a badge.

But an open license says nothing about quality. It says nothing about participation. It says nothing about sustainability. It says: "you can use this content however you want." It doesn't say: "here's how to keep this community alive." The license protects content. It doesn't protect the community.

**What to do instead:** An open license is a means, not an end. The end is sharing knowledge. The license is the legal container. Don't confuse the container with the content. Having a public domain Handbook doesn't make you a peeragogic group. It makes you a group that chose an open license. Important. Not sufficient.

> **Perturbator:** *"Great, your Handbook is CC0. So what? Open licenses are the easiest part of peeragogy. The hardest part is building a group that produces. A license doesn't produce. It distributes. Distribution without production is just rearranging emptiness."*
