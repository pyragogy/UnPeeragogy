"Wrapper: providing a structure that sets up a learning experience." "The wrapper creates a space in which peeragogy can happen."

A wrapper is the structure around peer learning. A syllabus, a schedule, a set of rules that create a container for the process.

**Why it matters:** A wrapper is useful. It creates boundaries. But boundaries also limit. The wrapper that protects can also constrain. A too-tight wrapper leaves no room for emergence. A too-loose wrapper offers no support.

The theory says the wrapper creates a space for peeragogy to happen. Practice says the wrapper can become the thing. The group organizes the wrapper. It spends energy on the wrapper. It confuses the wrapper with the learning. The wrapper is the container. The learning is the content. The Handbook describes wrappers in detail. It spends less energy on what happens inside them.

**What to do instead:** Design the wrapper to be temporary. A wrapper should dissolve as the group matures. The goal of peeragogy is self-sufficiency. A group that still needs a wrapper after six months hasn't learned to self-organize. The wrapper should be scaffolding. Not a permanent structure.

> **Perturbator:** *"You have a wrapper. A structure around the group. Good. Now, can the group function without it? If not, the wrapper isn't support. It's a dependency. The goal of peeragogy is a group that self-organizes. A wrapper is training wheels. Training wheels are meant to be removed. If they're still there after a year, they're not training. They're a crutch."*
