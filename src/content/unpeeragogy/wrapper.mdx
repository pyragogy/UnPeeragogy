## Summary

Wrappers become permanent the moment you call them temporary. The pattern assumes someone wants to manage interfaces — practice shows people avoid wrapper roles until crisis forces assignment. It matters because projects die waiting for volunteers to step forward.

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern works when wrapper evolution is explicitly designed with clear escalation paths and defined transition points. GitLab's stewardship model and P2PU's facilitator roles show that sustainable peer structures maintain governance boundaries while allowing community growth within those boundaries.

### Negative Rule

> Do not apply this pattern when the group lacks capacity to absorb wrapper functions or when no succession plan exists. IETF working groups and cMOOC governance failures show that removing structure without replacement mechanisms produces decision paralysis and community fragmentation.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern claims wrapper roles solve coordination problems through designated interface management. Partial verification — GitLab's stewardship handbook and P2PU's facilitator structure show governance boundaries can work when explicitly designed with succession paths. But cMOOC governance failures and IETF working group paralysis reveal the real mechanism: wrappers become single points of failure precisely because they work too well. Groups become dependent on wrapper functions without developing internal capacity to absorb them. The missing piece isn't role definition — it's planned obsolescence. Wrappers should be designed to eliminate themselves.
