### The Assumption

The wrapper's casual framing positions peeragogical concepts as accessible and intuitive, from cultural contexts like parlor games to structured conventions in source code licensing — presenting peer learning as natural and easy to adopt.

### Operational Reality

Framing peer learning as "just like a parlor game" is how you get groups of people playing parlor games when they should be making decisions.

GitLab's stewardship handbook and OpenStreetMap's Data Working Group show that explicit governance documentation — not accessible framing — creates effective adoption conditions. Drupal's governance transition and Lido DAO's centralization demonstrate that surface accessibility without structural depth produces the failure modes the vault records: knowledge-hoarding, decision-stagnation, authority-attrition.

The wrapper that presents peer learning as natural and easy produces groups that feel smart identifying patterns and never practice them — because practice requires confronting the structural complexity the wrapper hides.

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if framing peer learning as 'just like a parlor game' is how you get groups playing parlor games when they should be making decisions — and a wrapper that hides structural complexity produces groups that feel smart without ever practicing?"*

### Empirical Evidence

**Resilience Cases (Confirming):**
- [GitLab Stewardship Handbook](https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/) — Explicit documentation of governance boundaries between corporate sponsor and community reduces friction. Governance documentation creates more effective adoption conditions than accessible framing.
- [OpenStreetMap Data Working Group](https://wiki.osmfoundation.org/wiki/Data_Working_Group) — Formal dispute resolution bodies with binding authority resolve peer production conflicts. Structural depth precedes effective adoption.

**Fracture Cases (Complicating):**
- [Drupal Governance](https://www.drupal.org/governance) — Intentional governance transition from BDFL to community model required years of structural work. Surface accessibility without structural depth produces governance failures.
- [cMOOC vs xMOOC Pedagogical Governance](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/259999056_Connectivist_MOOCs_and_xMOOCs) — Peer coordination has known scalability limits that accessible framing cannot resolve.

### Operational Heuristics

**Positive Boundary:** The wrapper works when structural complexity is made explicit alongside surface accessibility — governance documentation creates more effective adoption conditions than accessible framing.

**Negative Boundary:** Do not present peer learning as natural or easy — surface accessibility without structural depth produces governance failures; groups feel smart identifying patterns and never practice them.

### Epistemic Synthesis

The wrapper that presents peer learning as natural and easy produces groups that feel smart identifying patterns and never practice them — because practice requires confronting the structural complexity the wrapper hides. GitLab and OpenStreetMap show that explicit governance documentation creates effective adoption. Drupal and cMOOCs show that surface accessibility without structural depth fails.
