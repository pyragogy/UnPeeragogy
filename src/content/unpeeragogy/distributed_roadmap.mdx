## Summary

Emergent roadmaps never emerge. Theory says peer coordination generates direction — practice shows endless deliberation without decisions. The mechanism only works when bounded by governance that kills infinite options.

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern works when combined with explicit scaffolding structures that channel emergence toward actionable decisions. Drupal's working groups and WebAssembly's authority-speed tradeoffs show that 'emergence' succeeds when bounded by intentional governance architecture that prevents indefinite option proliferation.

### Negative Rule

> Do not apply this pattern when facing time pressure or governance transitions. Drupal's BDFL-to-community transition and WebAssembly's governance model both show that successful distributed coordination requires intentional structural work, not patient waiting for optimal paths to emerge naturally.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern claims organic coordination will produce actionable roadmaps through distributed input. Partial verification — but only when scaffolded by intentional governance architecture. Drupal's working groups required years of structural transition work to move beyond BDFL paralysis, while WebAssembly's lighter governance traded authority for speed but created new failure modes for peer participation. What's missing is the admission that 'emergence' means designed constraints, not organic flow. The roadmap that emerges is the one someone built the rails for.
