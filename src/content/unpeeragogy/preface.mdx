## Summary

Peer learning handbooks dedicate themselves to volunteers, then document patterns that require institutions to work. The preface honors community spirit — the content describes governance scaffolding. This gap between dedication and documentation reveals what peer models actually need to survive.

## Evidenze Grounded

### 🔹 Confirming (2)

**Caso 1 — GitLab stewardship handbook**
- **Fenomeno:** Explicit documentation of governance boundaries between corporate sponsor and community reduces friction in open-core projects.
- **Fonte:** [https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/](https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/)

**Caso 2 — P2PU learning circles sustainability**
- **Fenomeno:** Peer learning models with defined facilitator roles and institutional partnerships are more sustainable than purely peer-driven learning.
- **Fonte:** [https://www.p2pu.org/en/learning-circles/](https://www.p2pu.org/en/learning-circles/)

### 🔸 Complicating (2)

**Caso 1 — P2P University governance challenges**
- **Fenomeno:** Peer learning communities inevitably face credentialing and quality assurance crises that require either hierarchy or external validation frameworks.
- **Fonte:** [https://www.p2pu.org/en/about/](https://www.p2pu.org/en/about/)

**Caso 2 — cMOOC vs xMOOC pedagogical governance**
- **Fenomeno:** The divide in open education between peer-governed and institution-governed models shows that peer governance has known scalability limits.
- **Fonte:** [https://www.researchgate.net/publication/259999056_Connectivist_MOOCs_and_xMOOCs](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/259999056_Connectivist_MOOCs_and_xMOOCs)

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This handbook works when readers understand that peer learning patterns require institutional context to succeed. Evidence from P2PU learning circles and GitLab's stewardship model shows that explicit governance boundaries and defined roles make peer collaboration sustainable over time.

### Negative Rule

> Do not present peer learning patterns as self-sufficient solutions. Evidence from P2PU and GitLab shows that peer models without governance scaffolding face sustainability and quality assurance crises that pure peer dynamics cannot resolve.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The handbook claims peer learning patterns are self-sustaining community practices. Partial verification — when explicit boundaries exist. GitLab's stewardship model and P2PU's facilitator roles prove that defined governance makes peer collaboration durable, while P2P University's credentialing crisis and quality assurance failures show what happens when pure peer dynamics meet real-world sustainability pressures. The missing piece is honest accounting of institutional dependency. The preface writes checks that peer learning alone can't cash.
