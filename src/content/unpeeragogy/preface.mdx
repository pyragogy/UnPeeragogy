"This book is a practical guide to peer learning." "We hope you will find it useful."

The Handbook's preface. Friendly. Open. A bit generic.

**Why it matters:** A preface introduces the text. It also introduces the authors. "We are a group that wrote about peer learning." The preface presents peeragogy as a solution to the loneliness of self-directed learning. It invites the reader to join.

The problem with the preface is that it promises more than the Handbook delivers. "A practical guide." Then 300+ pages of patterns, theory, and meta-discussion. It's practical in intention. Not in execution. The preface sets an expectation that the rest of the Handbook struggles to meet.

**What to do instead:** Write the preface last. Not first. Write it after you know what the Handbook actually does. A preface written before the content is a promise. A preface written after is a summary. A summary is more honest.

> **Perturbator:** *"'A practical guide to peer learning.' Practical. Then the Handbook spends 50 pages explaining what peeragogy is and why it needs a name. That's not practical. That's theoretical. A practical guide starts with 'do this.' Not 'this is why we exist.'"*

**Reference:** Peeragogy Handbook, "Preface" section (Introduction).
