"A set of interactive exercises that help people wrap hearts and minds around peeragogy." "In the context of education, this can be a rebellious activity."

The peeragogy course promises interactive exercises. But most peeragogic "courses" are a shared Google Doc and a weekly call.

**Why it matters:** Calling a study group a "course" is the first step toward not finishing it. A course has a syllabus, a schedule, an evaluation method. A study group has people who meet when they can. The peeragogy "course" is almost always a study group in disguise. The difference is a study group can fail quietly. A course can't. A course promises results. Then doesn't deliver. And no one says anything because it was free.

The theory says: "peeragogy thrives in open source settings." True. But open source doesn't mean "without structure." It means "different structure." Peeragogic courses confuse the absence of hierarchy with the absence of organization. And the absence of organization doesn't produce learning. It produces confusion.

**What to do instead:** Don't call it a course. Call it a "reading group." Courses have teachers, schedules, exams. Your group has a chat and goodwill. It's not a course. It's a circle. And that's fine. But calling it a course is the first lie you tell yourselves.
