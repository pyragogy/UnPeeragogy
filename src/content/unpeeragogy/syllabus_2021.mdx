"A syllabus for a peeragogy course offered in 2021." A concrete plan for a specific course.

**Why it matters:** The 2021 syllabus is the most recent syllabus. It shows what a peeragogy course looked like in practice. It's a data point.

The problem is that a syllabus is a plan. Not a report. It shows what was intended. Not what happened. Without outcomes (who attended, what was produced, what feedback was received), the syllabus is a promise. Promises are interesting. Results are more interesting.

**What to do instead:** Include outcomes with the syllabus. "10 students attended. 3 completed the course. 2 continued in the project. The main difficulty was X." This turns a plan into a case study. Case studies are more useful than plans. The Handbook has too many plans. Not enough case studies.

> **Perturbator:** *"Syllabus 2021. It's a plan. What happened? Did students finish? Did they find it useful? Was anything produced? No data. No outcomes. A plan without outcomes is a letter to yourself. It says what you intended. It doesn't say what you achieved. Letters are private. Syllabuses should be public."**
