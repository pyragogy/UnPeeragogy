## Summary

Documentation without authority creates planning theater. Theory says clear syllabi enable peer learning. Practice shows they become wish lists when implementation hits friction. The gap is governance — who decides when the plan breaks.

## Evidenze Grounded

### 🔹 Confirming (2)

**Caso 1 — GitLab stewardship handbook**
- **Fenomeno:** Explicit documentation of governance boundaries between corporate sponsor and community reduces friction in open-core projects.
- **Fonte:** [https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/](https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/)

**Caso 2 — Debian Stable Release Process**
- **Fenomeno:** Peer governance with defined roles, transparent policies, and scheduled milestones produces reliable long-term outcomes.
- **Fonte:** [https://www.debian.org/doc/debian-policy/](https://www.debian.org/doc/debian-policy/)

### 🔸 Complicating (2)

- **Fenomeno:** The Wikipedia Arbitration Committee (ArbCom) case in 2021 highlighted conflicts in community governance, where differing views on content moderation led to heated debates. The theoretical pattern posits that shared governance leads to consensus, yet this case illustrates how diverse opinions can complicate decision-making processes, resulting in protracted disputes.
- **Fonte:** [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arbitration_Committee_(Wikimedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arbitration_Committee_(Wikimedia))

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern works when documentation is paired with explicit governance boundaries and defined roles. GitLab's stewardship handbook and Debian's stable release process show that transparent policies with clear authority structures produce reliable long-term outcomes across complex collaborative projects.

### Negative Rule

> Do not rely on syllabus documentation alone without governance structures. The syllabus_2021 incidents show that clear documentation without authority mechanisms leads to decision-stagnation and knowledge-hoarding when implementation challenges arise.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern claims documented learning paths enable peer coordination across distributed teams. Partial verification — it works when paired with explicit authority structures. GitLab's stewardship handbook and Debian's release process demonstrate that transparent policies with defined roles produce reliable outcomes, while Wikipedia's ArbCom conflicts and syllabus_2021 stagnation show how clear documentation without governance mechanisms leads to decision paralysis when deviations arise. The real mechanism isn't the documentation itself but the authority to modify it. Plans are fuel, not engines.
