### Il Dogma

Groups often get stuck defining problems rather than solving them, leading to decision paralysis and endless discussion without progress.

### La Realtà

Three engineers at a fintech startup spent four months defining their deployment problem. "We need to reduce cycle time." "Actually, the problem is test coverage." "No, the real problem is that we don't have a staging environment." Every definition accurate. Every definition leading to a different solution. Every definition consuming time the competitor used to ship.

The pattern says problem-definition is paralyzing. It's not — when someone has the authority to end the definition phase. GitLab's stewardship handbook documents who decides when the problem is sufficiently defined. OpenStreetMap's Data Working Group has binding authority to rule on what counts as a data quality problem. The IETF rough consensus model fails without hierarchical escalation — not because the problems are hard, but because nobody can say "enough defining, time to solve."

The problem isn't problem-definition. The problem is that groups define problems without defining who gets to stop defining them.

### Perturbatore

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if the problem isn't that groups define problems too much — the problem is they define problems without defining who gets to solve them?"*

### Evidenze di Tenuta

**about.gitlab.com** — Explicit documentation of governance boundaries between corporate sponsor and community reduces friction in open-core projects. GitLab shows that authority to end problem-definition enables problem-solving.

**wiki.osmfoundation.org** — Formal dispute resolution bodies with binding authority and published reasoning resolve peer production conflicts. OpenStreetMap demonstrates that binding authority to define problems enables groups to move past analysis paralysis.

### Evidenze di Faglia

**ietf.org** — Rough consensus governance only works when backed by hierarchical escalation paths. IETF demonstrates that pure consensus without appeals prolongs problem-definition indefinitely.

**w3.org** — Choosing lighter governance models trades authority for speed, creating different failure modes. WebAssembly shows that lightweight models extend problem-definition because no one can terminate it.

### Euristiche Operative

**Regola Positiva:** Questo pattern funziona quando la definizione del problema è accoppiata a chiara autorità di risoluzione. GitLab e OpenStreetMap mostrano che confini di governance espliciti e autorità decisionale vincolante permettono ai gruppi di superare la paralisi analitica.

**Regola Negativa:** Non evitare la definizione del problema quando le strutture di autorità non sono chiare. IETF mostra che anche il "rough consensus" fallisce senza escalation paths — il puro consenso senza appelli prolunga la definizione indefinitamente.
