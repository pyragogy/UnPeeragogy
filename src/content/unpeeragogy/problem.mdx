### The Assumption

Groups often get stuck defining problems rather than solving them, leading to decision paralysis and endless discussion without progress.

### Operational Reality

Three engineers at a fintech startup spent four months defining their deployment problem. "We need to reduce cycle time." "Actually, the problem is test coverage." "No, the real problem is that we do not have a staging environment." Every definition accurate. Every definition leading to a different solution. Every definition consuming time the competitor used to ship.

The pattern says problem-definition is paralyzing. It is not — when someone has the authority to end the definition phase. GitLab's stewardship handbook documents who decides when the problem is sufficiently defined. OpenStreetMap's Data Working Group has binding authority to rule on what counts as a data quality problem. The IETF rough consensus model fails without hierarchical escalation — not because the problems are hard, but because nobody can say "enough defining, time to solve."

The problem is not problem-definition. The problem is that groups define problems without defining who gets to stop defining them.

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if the problem isn't that groups define problems too much — the problem is they define problems without defining who gets to solve them?"*

### Empirical Evidence

**Resilience Cases (Confirming):**
- [GitLab Stewardship Handbook](https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/) — Explicit documentation of governance boundaries between corporate sponsor and community reduces friction in open-core projects. Authority to end problem-definition enables problem-solving.
- [OpenStreetMap Data Working Group](https://wiki.osmfoundation.org/wiki/Data_Working_Group) — Formal dispute resolution bodies with binding authority and published reasoning resolve peer production conflicts. Binding authority to define problems enables groups to move past analysis paralysis.

**Fracture Cases (Complicating):**
- [IETF Rough Consensus](https://www.ietf.org/about/) — Rough consensus governance only works when backed by hierarchical escalation paths. Pure consensus without appeals prolongs problem-definition indefinitely.
- [WebAssembly Community Group](https://www.w3.org/community/webassembly/) — Choosing lighter governance models for standards development trades authority for speed, creating different failure modes. Lightweight models extend problem-definition because no one can terminate it.

### Operational Heuristics

**Positive Boundary:** This pattern works when problem-definition is coupled with clear resolution authority — explicit governance boundaries and binding decision-making authority enable groups to move past analysis paralysis.

**Negative Boundary:** Do not avoid problem-definition when authority structures are not clear — even rough consensus fails without escalation paths; pure consensus without appeals prolongs definition indefinitely.

### Epistemic Synthesis

OpenStreetMap has binding authority to rule on what counts as a problem. The problem is not problem-definition.
