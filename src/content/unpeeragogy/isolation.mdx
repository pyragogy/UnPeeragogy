## Summary

Isolation looks like a people problem but runs on power mechanics. Theory says unclear authority creates chaos — practice shows it creates rational withdrawal. When contribution paths don't map to influence, participants don't disconnect socially, they disconnect strategically.

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern resolves when governance structures clearly map contribution to influence before addressing social connection. WebAssembly's explicit authority-for-speed trade shows that transparent governance trade-offs prevent isolation by giving participants clear expectations about their decision-making power.

### Negative Rule

> Do not treat isolation as purely individual pathology requiring connection activities. Drupal's governance transition shows that unclear authority structures produce rational withdrawal — participants isolate because their contributions lack decision-making influence, not because they need more social connection.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern claims isolation stems from narrow focus versus chaotic openness — a calibration problem. Partial verification, conditional on governance visibility. WebAssembly's explicit authority-for-speed trade prevents isolation by giving clear expectations about decision-making power, while Drupal's years-long governance transition shows that structural ambiguity produces rational withdrawal, not antisocial behavior. But the mechanism isn't about social connection or focus width — it's about whether contribution translates to influence in predictable ways. The real isolation happens when you can't see the power structure you're feeding.
