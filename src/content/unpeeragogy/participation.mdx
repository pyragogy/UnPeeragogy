## Summary

Participation creates hierarchy. Theory says peer groups self-organize through consensus — practice shows differential participation creates stealth authority structures. The groups that survive design governance for inequality, not against it.

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern works when governance structures are explicitly designed to handle participation inequality. OpenStreetMap's formal dispute resolution and WebAssembly's lighter governance models both acknowledge that participation creates authority and design structures accordingly.

### Negative Rule

> Do not assume informal consensus can handle all conflicts in peer groups. OpenStreetMap's Data Working Group exists because informal consensus failed to resolve data disputes that required binding decisions with published reasoning.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern claims equilibrium between order and chaos allows everyone to participate at their own pace. Partial verification — but only when governance structures are explicitly designed for participation inequality. OpenStreetMap's formal dispute resolution works because it acknowledges that informal consensus fails when binding decisions need published reasoning, while WebAssembly's lighter governance trades authority for speed, creating different failure modes but still designing for differential participation. The mechanism missing from the theory: participation isn't just about pace or involvement — it's about who shows up to every meeting and what that attendance means for power. Three wolves deciding consensus.
