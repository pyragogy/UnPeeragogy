### Il Dogma

Most members in a peer group do not participate, only a few do. Participation is often seen as a cost rather than a benefit, and lack of recognition leads to decreased participation.

### La Realtà

Three wolves and a sheep vote on dinner. Democracy in action. Peeragogy replaces the three wolves with the people who show up to every meeting, the sheep with the people who couldn't make the call because they had childcare. The vote is unanimous. Everyone consented. The people who didn't vote now eat what the regulars decided.

Not manipulation. Not bad faith. Just the physics of participation inequality.

OpenStreetMap's Data Working Group exists precisely because informal consensus failed to resolve data disputes. The people who showed up consistently had disproportionate influence, and the people who didn't show up lost trust in the process. Formal dispute resolution with binding authority didn't reduce participation — it protected the legitimacy of decisions by making authority explicit.

Participation inequality isn't a bug. It's a feature of any system where attendance is voluntary and stakes are unequal. The question isn't how to fix it, but how to govern its consequences.

### Perturbatore

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if democracy is two wolves and a sheep voting on dinner — and peeragogy is three wolves who showed up to every meeting deciding what counts as consensus?"*

### Evidenze di Tenuta

**wiki.osmfoundation.org** — Formal dispute resolution bodies with binding authority and published reasoning resolve peer production conflicts that informal consensus cannot. OpenStreetMap shows that explicit authority structures protect legitimacy when participation is unequal.

### Evidenze di Faglia

**w3.org** — Choosing lighter governance models for standards development trades authority for speed, creating different failure modes for peer participation. WebAssembly shows that participation inequality persists regardless of governance weight — lighter models just make it harder to challenge.

**en.wikipedia.org** — Wikipedia's consensus processes produce de facto hierarchies through participation inequality — those who show up consistently gain disproportionate influence. Wikipedia's deletion debates show that participation creates authority and transparent consensus legitimation reifies existing hierarchies.

### Euristiche Operative

**Regola Positiva:** Questo pattern funziona quando le strutture di governance sono progettate esplicitamente per gestire la disuguaglianza di partecipazione. OpenStreetMap e WebAssembly mostrano che la partecipazione crea autorità — il design di governance o la gestisce esplicitamente o la subisce come default.

**Regola Negativa:** Non assumere che il consenso informale possa gestire tutti i conflitti in gruppi con partecipazione diseguale. OpenStreetMap esiste perché il consenso informale non ha risolto dispute di dati che richiedevano decisioni vincolanti con ragionamento pubblicato.

### Sintesi & Tracciabilità

**Riferimento:** audit-v2 (delta tensione: 1.6 → 1.7492, +0.1492)
**Evidenze esaminate:** 3 (1 confermante, 2 complicanti)
**Stato:** PROVVISORIO — tensione alta

La disuguaglianza di partecipazione non è un bug. È una feature di qualsiasi sistema dove la presenza è volontaria e le poste in gioco sono diseguali. OpenStreetMap ha creato un Data Working Group non per aumentare la partecipazione ma per proteggere la legittimità delle decisioni di fronte a partecipazione strutturalmente diseguale. La tensione non è tra inclusione ed efficienza, ma tra chi partecipa e chi detiene l'autorità.
