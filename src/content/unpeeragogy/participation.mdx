"Participation: the act of contributing to the group." "Obvious. Also rare."

**Why it matters:** Most people in a peer group don't participate. They observe. Lurking is the default. Participation is the exception. The theory treats participation as the natural state of a peer learner. Practice shows it's not.

The gap between "member" and "participant" is the widest in peeragogy. A group of 50 members has, on average, 5 participants. The rest watch. The theory doesn't explain this gap. It just describes patterns for participation. It assumes the desire to participate is there. Often it's not.

**What to do instead:** Participation is a cost. It consumes time, energy, and reputation. Before asking "how do we increase participation?", ask "what would make participation worth the cost?" If the answer is "recognition" or "belonging," those aren't costs. They're returns. But they must be real. A group that doesn't recognize contributors can't expect participation.

> **Perturbator:** *"You want participation. Do you deserve it? Participation costs. It costs time, energy, vulnerability. What does the group give back? If the answer is 'a sense of belonging,' that's not enough. Belonging is the minimum. People need to feel that their contribution changes something. If the group doesn't change because of a contribution, the contribution was wasted. Participation requires the possibility of impact."*
