### The Assumption

The PARS framework describes four elements of peer learning: Peers, Action, Reflection, and Selection. These elements should be balanced to create effective peer learning environments, but the framework provides descriptive categories rather than prescriptive guidance for managing their interaction.

### Operational Reality

PARS tells you the ingredients but not the recipe. Peers, Action, Reflection, Selection. Four boxes, no arrows. The framework describes what exists in functioning peer learning groups but will not help you build one.

Watch a team try to use PARS as a design tool. They map their activities into the four quadrants. Everything fits. The mapping is clean. And after the meeting, they still do not know what to do about the member who never contributes or the decisions that never get made.

The evidence from P2PU and OpenStreetMap does not contradict PARS — it completes it. P2PU learning circles survive through institutional partnerships that PARS does not name as a precondition. OpenStreetMap resolves conflicts through formal dispute resolution that PARS describes as "Selection" but does not specify how to design. Drupal shows that peer learning systems without explicit authority structures face credentialing crises that PARS cannot resolve. P2PU's own governance challenges demonstrate that PARS's descriptive neutrality does not equip groups for the tensions of credentialing.

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if PARS tells you the ingredients but not the recipe — and when the soufflé collapses, you are left holding a framework that describes the mess?"*

### Empirical Evidence

**Resilience Cases (Confirming):**
- [P2PU Learning Circles](https://www.p2pu.org/en/learning-circles/) — Peer learning models with defined facilitator roles and institutional partnerships are more sustainable than purely peer-driven learning. PARS needs institutional scaffolding to produce sustainable peer learning.
- [OpenStreetMap Data Working Group](https://wiki.osmfoundation.org/wiki/Data_Working_Group) — Formal dispute resolution bodies with binding authority and published reasoning resolve peer production conflicts. "Selection" in PARS requires formal authority to function — not just peer judgment.

**Fracture Cases (Complicating):**
- [Drupal Governance](https://www.drupal.org/governance) — Intentional governance transition from BDFL to community model required years of structural work, not organic emergence. Peer learning systems without explicit authority structures face credentialing crises that PARS cannot resolve.
- [P2PU About / Governance Challenges](https://www.p2pu.org/en/about/) — Peer learning communities inevitably face quality assurance crises that require hierarchy or external validation. PARS's descriptive neutrality does not equip groups for the tensions of credentialing.

### Operational Heuristics

**Positive Boundary:** PARS works when combined with institutional partnerships or formal governance mechanisms — the framework needs institutional scaffolding to produce sustainable peer learning.

**Negative Boundary:** Do not apply PARS assuming peer balance will emerge organically — peer learning systems without explicit authority structures face credentialing crises that the framework describes but does not equip groups to resolve.

### Epistemic Synthesis

PARS is an excellent vocabulary, a poor architectural blueprint. The four elements describe what exists in functioning peer learning groups but do not equip a group to build one. P2PU and OpenStreetMap do not contradict PARS — they complete it. Learning circles survive through institutional partnerships that PARS does not name as a precondition. Formal dispute resolution is "Selection" but the framework does not specify how to design it.
