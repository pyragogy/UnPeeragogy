## Summary

Peer learning needs roles to survive contact with reality. The framework gives you review steps but skips the power question — who decides when the review says change course? Works when someone can actually enforce the decisions.

## Evidenze Grounded

- **Fenomeno:** Peer learning models with defined facilitator roles and institutional partnerships are more sustainable than purely peer-driven learning.

**Caso 2 — P2P University governance challenges**
- **Fenomeno:** Peer learning communities inevitably face credentialing and quality assurance crises that require either hierarchy or external validation frameworks.

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This framework works when combined with institutional partnerships or formal governance mechanisms. P2PU learning circles' sustainability through institutional partnerships and OpenStreetMap's conflict resolution through formal authority show that hybrid models with explicit governance enable peer learning elements to function effectively.

### Negative Rule

> Do not apply this framework assuming peer balance will emerge organically. P2P University's governance challenges and Drupal's transition experience show that peer learning systems without explicit authority structures face credentialing crises and decision paralysis that balance cannot resolve.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern promises structured reflection will improve peer learning outcomes. Partial verification — when backed by real authority or institutional muscle. P2PU's learning circles work because libraries provide the structure, OpenStreetMap resolves disputes because the Data Working Group has binding power, but Drupal's governance struggles and P2P University's organizational challenges show what happens when peer reflection meets actual resource allocation. The template assumes someone will act on the insights. Missing mechanism: who has the authority to implement changes when peers disagree about what the review revealed.
