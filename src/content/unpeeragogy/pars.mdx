"PARS: a system of roles for peer learning." "People, Agents, Resources, Services."

The PARS framework splits peer learning into four elements. People do the work. Agents facilitate. Resources are the material. Services are the infrastructure.

**Why it matters:** PARS is useful because it gives names to elements that are usually invisible. But naming isn't managing. A group can identify People, Agents, Resources, and Services and still not know how to make them work together.

The problem with PARS is that it's descriptive. It doesn't prescribe. It says "these are the elements." It doesn't say "here's how to balance them." A group with too many agents and too few people won't produce. A group with many resources and no facilitators won't self-organize. PARS describes the components. It doesn't describe the recipe.

**What to do instead:** Use PARS as a diagnostic tool, not a design tool. When the group isn't working, run a PARS analysis: which element is missing? Too many people and no agents? Add facilitation. Too many resources and no services? Improve infrastructure. PARS is a checklist, not a blueprint.

> **Perturbator:** *"PARS is a framework. It names things. People, Agents, Resources, Services. Useful labels. Now what? PARS doesn't tell you. It gives you categories. Categories are the beginning of analysis. Not the end. PARS is the diagnosis. The therapy is still unknown."*
