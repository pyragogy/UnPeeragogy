### Claim

Peeragogy presents assessment as a collaborative, formative practice. Peers evaluate each other not to judge but to learn together. The theory dedicates an entire section to it — self-assessment, peer feedback, iterative improvement. The promise: evaluation without hierarchy, quality without gatekeepers.

### Reality

Peer assessment creates quality hierarchies. And then those hierarchies eat the peer governance that created them.

The problem is not that people evaluate badly. The problem is that evaluation requires someone to say no — and in a peer group, saying no means risking the relationship. So nobody says no. Everyone gives 10 out of 10. Feedback becomes "nice work!" and the group never learns because it never names what went wrong.

Wikipedia's Good Article process works for exactly the opposite reason the theory predicts: not because peers evaluate each other, but because nominated reviewers with binding authority apply transparent criteria. It is not peer assessment. It is lightweight hierarchy dressed in peer language.

> **Perturbatore:** *"You don't evaluate because you are afraid of hurting each other. So nobody improves. Peer assessment requires more courage than most groups have. You keep calling it 'formative assessment' because it sounds better than 'we don't have the guts to tell each other the truth.'"*

### Positive Examples

**Wikipedia Good Article process** — Transparent criteria plus independent reviewers with binding authority. It creates sustainable quality tiers in a community of thousands. The key is not the peer mechanism. The key is that reviewers can reject a nomination without the author's consent.

**Moodle plugin QA system** — Nominated integrators review community contributions against clear criteria. Scales because maintainers are explicitly authorized to say no. The community accepts the hierarchy because the criteria are transparent and the integrators are known.

### Negative Examples

**Wikipedia Featured Article Candidate process** — The same community, stricter criteria, higher quality bar. Here the peer assessment system produces exactly the contradiction the theory ignores: quality tiers create an elite, and the elite conflicts with the egalitarian ideals of the community. The system works technically. It fails socially.

**Linux kernel Reviewed-by tag** — Formal peer review tags create accountability. But they also reveal the scarcity: the bottleneck shifts from writing code to getting it reviewed. Peer assessment does not distribute the work. It concentrates the bottleneck.

### Synthesis

What works: do not pretend assessment is flat. Accept that quality control requires someone with the authority to say no. Make the criteria transparent. Make the reviewers known. Make the rejection impersonal.

What fails: hoping that peers will evaluate each other honestly without structure. They will not. They like each other too much. And liking each other too much is the fastest path to mediocre work.

If you want assessment in a peer group, drop the pretense of horizontality. Appoint reviewers. Give them criteria. Accept that the group will stratify. The alternative is not flat assessment — it is no assessment at all.
