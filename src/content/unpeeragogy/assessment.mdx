## Summary

Peer assessment kills peer governance. Theory promises flat evaluation — practice demands someone with authority to say no. Communities that refuse this choice fragment.

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern works when communities explicitly accept quality hierarchies and nominate specific integrators with binding authority. Wikipedia's Good Article process and Moodle's plugin QA system show that transparent criteria plus empowered reviewers creates sustainable quality control across thousands of contributions.

### Negative Rule

> Do not apply this pattern expecting flat peer participation. Wikipedia's Featured Article process shows that peer assessment systems inevitably create quality hierarchies that conflict with egalitarian community ideals, leading to governance tensions.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern claims assessment can scale without hierarchy. Partial verification — when communities explicitly accept quality tiers and nominate integrators. Wikipedia's Good Article process works because reviewers have binding authority and transparent criteria. Moodle's plugin QA scales because nominated maintainers can reject contributions. But Wikipedia's Featured Article process shows the contradiction: peer assessment creates the very hierarchies that contradict peer principles. The real mechanism isn't assessment design — it's whether a community can stomach its own stratification. Quality control is arson against egalitarian ideals.
