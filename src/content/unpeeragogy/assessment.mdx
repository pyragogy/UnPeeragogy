"Article on assessment in peer learning, and also an assessment exercise in practice." 296 lines to say: we have no idea how to evaluate ourselves.

**Why it matters:** The theory dedicates a full section to Assessment. Formative evaluation, peer feedback, self-assessment. Then in practice: no one wants to judge others. Everyone gives 10/10. Feedback is "nice work! 👍". The group never learns from its mistakes because it never names them.

Honestly evaluating a peer means risking the relationship. It means saying "your contribution wasn't up to par" to someone you'll have to work with tomorrow. It's easier to smile and give a high score. Evaluation becomes celebration. And celebration doesn't make anyone grow.

**The self-assessment paradox:** The theory proposes self-assessment as a solution. Beautiful idea. Except self-assessment is the worst performance indicator that exists. Incompetent people don't know they're incompetent (Dunning-Kruger effect). Competent people systematically underestimate themselves. Result: the worst give themselves high scores, the best give themselves low scores, and no one learns anything.

**What to do instead:** If you want honest evaluation, you need: (a) anonymous identity, (b) standardized criteria, and (c) acceptance that someone will feel hurt. If you're not willing to do (a), (b), and (c), don't evaluate. At least don't pretend you're doing it.

> **Perturbator:** *"You don't evaluate because you're afraid of hurting each other. So no one ever improves. Peer evaluation requires more courage than most groups have. So you don't do it. And you know it. And you keep calling it 'formative assessment' because it sounds better than 'we don't have the guts to tell each other the truth.'"*
