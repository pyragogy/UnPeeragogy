### Claim

The Peeragogy Handbook presents assessment as a collaborative, formative practice embedded in peer learning. Self-assessment, peer feedback, iterative improvement — the theory frames evaluation as something the group does together to learn, not as a gatekeeping mechanism. The assumption is that peers can evaluate each other honestly when the process is transparent and the goal is shared growth.

### Reality

Peer assessment creates quality hierarchies. And those hierarchies can conflict with the peer governance that created them.

The problem is structural, not personal. Evaluation requires someone to say no — to reject a contribution, to name what is not good enough. In a peer group, saying no risks the relationship. So the group avoids it. Feedback becomes "nice work!" and the group never learns because it never names what went wrong.

Wikipedia's Good Article process works for reasons the theory does not fully account for: not because peers evaluate each other, but because nominated reviewers with binding authority apply transparent criteria. It is not peer assessment. It is lightweight hierarchy operating under peer language.

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if the problem is not the criteria but the courage to apply them? You can have the most transparent process in the world — if nobody is willing to reject a friend's contribution, the process produces nothing but paper. Maybe assessment is not a design problem. Maybe it is a social cost problem."*

### Evidence That Supports

**Wikipedia Good Article process** — Transparent criteria plus independent reviewers with binding authority. Creates sustainable quality tiers in a community of thousands. The key is not the peer mechanism. The key is that reviewers can reject a nomination without the author's consent.

**Moodle plugin QA system** — Nominated integrators review community contributions against clear criteria. Scales because maintainers are explicitly authorized to say no. The community accepts the hierarchy because the criteria are transparent and the integrators are known.

### Evidence That Complicates

**Wikipedia Featured Article Candidate process** — Same community, stricter criteria, higher quality bar. Here the peer assessment system produces the contradiction: quality tiers create an elite, and the elite conflicts with the egalitarian ideals of the community. The system works technically. It struggles socially.

**Linux kernel Reviewed-by tag system** — Formal peer review creates accountability. But it also reveals scarcity: the bottleneck shifts from writing code to getting it reviewed. Peer assessment does not distribute the work evenly. It concentrates the bottleneck.

### Evidence That Contradicts

*No case in the current corpus directly contradicts the pattern.*

### Evidence That Is Ambiguous

**Academic peer learning contexts** — Formative peer assessment in classrooms shows mixed results. Sometimes it works. Sometimes it produces the same social cost problem (students refuse to grade each other honestly). The controlled environment of a classroom differs significantly from open peer production communities. It is not clear which variables matter most.

### Synthesis

What these cases suggest: do not pretend assessment is flat. Accept that quality control requires someone with the authority to say no. Make the criteria transparent. Make the reviewers known. Make the rejection impersonal — not because impersonality is kind, but because it protects the relationship.

What remains unclear: whether peer assessment without hierarchy is possible at all, or whether the phrase contains a contradiction that no amount of process design can resolve. The evidence leans toward the second interpretation. But more cases are needed.
