### Il Dogma

Coworking is a foundational mode of peer production, often confused with collaboration despite lacking shared projects or goals. The theory suggests coworking can lead to collaboration but warns against assuming automatic progression from proximity to partnership.

### La Realtà

The coworking space on Via Tortona fills with MacBooks at 9 AM. Designers nod at developers. Consultants exchange cards. Everyone pays €25 for a day pass to sit near other people doing unrelated work. This isn't collaboration — it's parallel isolation with better coffee.

Real peer production happens differently. Linux kernel development runs on brutal meritocracy and clear patch acceptance rules. Wikipedia editors fight over citations and sourcing standards. PlanetMath contributors argue about mathematical rigor. Not pleasant. Not smooth. But these systems produce lasting work because they've built governance structures that survive conflict. Coworking spaces offer proximity without process, presence without purpose.

The friction matters more than the coffee.

### Perturbatore

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if coworking without governance is just expensive coffee and the illusion of productivity — and the real barrier to collaboration isn't distance but the absence of useful disagreement?"*

### Evidenze di Tenuta

**kernel.org** — Clear rules for patch acceptance and defined backporting processes produce reliable long-term maintenance in peer production. Linux demonstrates that coordination at scale requires explicit processes, not shared physical space.

**lwn.net** — Independent third-party summaries of peer production activity create shared awareness that enables distributed governance at scale. LWN shows that structured communication channels — not coworking proximity — sustain distributed coordination.

### Evidenze di Faglia

**en.wikipedia.org** — Community-produced summaries (Signpost) become de facto governance communication channels that can diverge from official narratives. Wikipedia demonstrates that even in communities with shared purpose, communication requires governance.

**drupal.org** — Intentional governance transition from BDFL to community model required years of structural work, not organic emergence. Drupal shows that moving from informal to formal coordination requires structural redesign, not just shared space.

### Euristiche Operative

**Regola Positiva:** Il coworking funziona a scala quando supportato da chiare regole di coordinamento e canali di comunicazione indipendenti. Linux e LWN dimostrano che processi espliciti per l'accettazione dei contributi e canali di comunicazione strutturati sostengono la coordinazione distribuita.

**Regola Negativa:** Non assumere che il coworking si trasformerà naturalmente in collaborazione senza governance esplicita. Drupal dimostra che muoversi da coordinamento informale a formale richiede riprogettazione strutturale — non solo condivisione dello spazio.

### Sintesi & Tracciabilità

**Riferimento:** audit-v2 (delta tensione: 1.0 → 1.0312, +0.0312)

**Stato:** PROVVISORIO

L'evidenza suggerisce che la peer production sostenibile richiede più dello spazio condiviso — richiede standard condivisi per ciò che conta come contributo. Linux non funziona perché i programmatori si piacciono, ma perché hanno concordato come valutare le patch. Il coworking può inibire la collaborazione creando l'illusione che prossimità equivalga a partnership. L'attrito conta più del caffè.
