"Examples from Linux, Wikipedia, PlanetMath." "Co-working as the opposite side of convening."

The theory takes enormous examples — Linux, Wikipedia — and uses them as proof that coworking works. But Linux and Wikipedia are exceptions, not rules. Most coworking produces little more than desk rental.

**Why it matters:** Sharing a space doesn't mean collaborating. Two people working in the same room can ignore each other completely. Sharing the bills isn't a shared project. It's a real estate transaction.

Coworking was sold as the solution to the isolation of self-employment. It partly works — less loneliness, more routine. But it doesn't automatically produce collaboration. Producing collaboration requires a shared project, common goals, managed conflict. Coworking gives none of that. It gives a desk and WiFi. The rest has to be built. Building is harder than renting.

**What to do instead:** If you want collaboration, don't put people in the same room. Give them a project to do together. Space is a means. The project is the end. Confusing the two is like buying a pot and calling it dinner.

> **Perturbator:** *"You're 12 people in the same room. Each working on their own laptop. There's no shared project between you. There's a lease agreement. Call it 'shared workspace.' Don't call it 'collaboration.' Collaboration isn't bought by the square meter."*
