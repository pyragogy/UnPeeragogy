## Summary

Coworking fragments without governance. Theory promises collaboration through proximity — practice delivers parallel work streams that never merge. The difference is explicit coordination rules, not shared space.

## Evidenze Grounded

### 🔹 Confirming (2)

**Caso 1 — Linux kernel stable tree governance**
- **Fenomeno:** Clear rules for patch acceptance and defined backporting processes produce reliable long-term maintenance in peer production.
- **Fonte:** [https://www.kernel.org/doc/html/latest/process/stable-kernel-rules.html](https://www.kernel.org/doc/html/latest/process/stable-kernel-rules.html)

**Caso 2 — LWN.net kernel development summaries**
- **Fenomeno:** Independent third-party summaries of peer production activity create shared awareness that enables distributed governance at scale.
- **Fonte:** [https://lwn.net/Kernel/](https://lwn.net/Kernel/)

### 🔸 Complicating (2)

**Caso 1 — Wikipedia Signpost community journalism**
- **Fenomeno:** Community-produced summaries become de facto governance communication channels that can diverge from official narratives.
- **Fonte:** [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Wikipedia_Signpost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Wikipedia_Signpost)

**Caso 2 — Drupal governance / community working groups**
- **Fenomeno:** Intentional governance transition from BDFL to community model requires years of structural work, not organic emergence.
- **Fonte:** [https://www.drupal.org/governance](https://www.drupal.org/governance)

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> Coworking works at scale when backed by clear coordination rules and independent communication channels. The Linux kernel stable tree governance and LWN.net summaries show that explicit processes for patch acceptance and third-party activity summaries enable reliable distributed coordination across thousands of contributors.

### Negative Rule

> Do not assume coworking will naturally scale or transition to collaboration without explicit governance. The Drupal governance transition shows that moving from informal to formal coordination requires years of structural work, and the Wikipedia Signpost shows community-produced coordination can diverge from official channels.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern claims coworking naturally evolves into collaboration through interpersonal exchange. Partial verification — but only when backed by explicit governance structures. Linux kernel stable tree rules and LWN.net summaries prove distributed coordination works at scale, while Wikipedia Signpost and Drupal transitions show community journalism becomes governance by default. The missing piece: coordination doesn't emerge from proximity. It requires designed friction — rules that force convergence rather than hoping for it.
