### Il Dogma

The About page serves as the primary entry point for newcomers to understand peeragogy principles and engage with the community through clear, accessible communication. The theory assumes that information clarity produces engagement — if the page clearly states what peeragogy is and what it offers, the right people will find their way in.

### La Realtà

Howard Rheingold convened the Peeragogy project in 2012. Eleven years later, the About page still opens with his name. The handbook speaks of collaborative courses and collected know-how, but governance remains a footnote.

Not a conspiracy. Not malice. Not even conscious choice — just the default drift toward founder mythology. Most peer projects begin with someone's vision and never quite escape that gravitational pull. The About page becomes a monument to origins rather than a map of current power.

Open-core projects that explicitly document governance boundaries between corporate sponsors and communities reduce friction. The boundaries are visible. People know where they stand. But projects that claim pure peer governance while maintaining informal hierarchies create something more dangerous — the illusion of equality with the reality of invisible barriers.

### Perturbatore

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if an About page that doesn't name current decision-makers isn't welcoming newcomers — but warning them away?"*

### Evidenze di Tenuta

**about.gitlab.com** — Explicit documentation of governance boundaries between corporate sponsor and community reduces friction in open-core projects. GitLab's stewardship handbook shows that transparent documentation of who decides what, and where corporate authority ends, makes collaboration sustainable.

### Evidenze di Faglia

**drupal.org** — Intentional governance transition from BDFL to community model required years of structural work, not organic emergence. Drupal's history demonstrates that governance transparency isn't a communication problem — it's a structural problem that only explicit redesign can solve.

**apache.org** — Meritocracy-based peer governance creates invisible barriers: those with more free time accumulate authority, creating a class system within the "flat" organization. The About page that looks open may be the one that traps newcomers most effectively.

### Euristiche Operative

**Regola Positiva:** Le pagine About funzionano quando documentano esplicitamente sia i principi di apprendimento che i confini di governance. GitLab dimostra che la trasparenza dei confini decisionali riduce l'attrito per i newcomer.

**Regola Negativa:** Non creare pagine About che spiegano i principi di apprendimento senza documentare le strutture di potere. Il modello Apache dimostra che la governance meritocratica crea sistemi di classe invisibili — e una pagina che li nasconde è una trappola, non un benvenuto.

**Stato:** PROVVISORIO

La tensione è chiara: la teoria assume che chiarezza comunicativa produca engagement, ma l'evidenza mostra che la struttura di governance conta più della chiarezza della pagina. Il problema dell'About page non è essere "troppo dettagliata" — è presentare il peeragogy come se emergesse naturalmente dalle buone intenzioni, invece di richiedere strutture esplicite per funzionare.
