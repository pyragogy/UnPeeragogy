### Claim

The *Peeragogy project* was convened by Howard Rheingold in 2012

### Reality

Howard Rheingold convened the Peeragogy project in 2012. Eleven years later, the About page still opens with his name. The handbook speaks of collaborative courses and collected know-how, but governance remains a footnote.

Not a conspiracy. Not malice. Not even conscious choice — just the default drift toward founder mythology. Most peer projects begin with someone's vision and never quite escape that gravitational pull. The About page becomes a monument to origins rather than a map of current power.

Open-core projects that explicitly document governance boundaries between corporate sponsors and communities reduce friction. The boundaries are visible. People know where they stand. But projects that claim pure peer governance while maintaining informal hierarchies create something more dangerous — the illusion of equality with the reality of invisible barriers.

Those with more free time accumulate authority. The meritocracy creates its own class system within the supposedly flat organization. Years of structural work, not organic emergence, are required to transition from benevolent dictator to genuine community governance.

> What if an About page that doesn't name current decision-makers isn't welcoming newcomers — but warning them away?

### Synthesis

These cases suggest that governance transparency isn't about ideology but friction reduction. Projects that hide their power structures behind peer rhetoric create more barriers than those that openly acknowledge hierarchy. The handbook's collaborative language may actually signal to potential contributors that their contributions won't translate to influence.

What remains to be tested is whether peer projects can survive their founders not by hiding the transition but by making it visible. The About page becomes less about origins and more about current mechanisms — who decides what, how authority transfers, what happens when the founder steps back.

The honest About page doesn't sell the dream. It exposes the work.
