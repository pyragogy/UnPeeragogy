"About this Handbook and the Peeragogy Project." A page for those who just arrived.

**Why it matters:** The About page is for outsiders. It explains what the project is, who wrote it, why it exists. It's the first impression. And first impressions are sticky.

The problem is that the About page is written by insiders. It assumes the reader already cares. It explains details (patterns, versions, contributors) that a newcomer doesn't need. A new visitor needs: (1) what this is, (2) why they should care, (3) how to start. The About page answers (1) poorly because it's too detailed. It doesn't answer (2) or (3) at all.

**What to do instead:** The About page should be one paragraph. "This is the Peeragogy Handbook. It helps groups learn and work together without hierarchy. Here's how to start." Everything else — contributors, version history, methodology — can go in a separate page. A good About page is short. A great one is shorter than you think.

> **Perturbator:** *"About: six paragraphs, three contributor lists, two version histories. Too much. About should be: 'This is peeragogy. It helps groups work together. Start here.' 12 words. Everything else is detail. Detail for those who already care. Not for those who are deciding whether to care."*
