### The Assumption

Structure should emerge from content and activity, not be imposed beforehand. Premature structure kills the organic creativity of peer groups. Decision-stagnation and authority-attrition are the primary failure mechanisms of imposed structure.

### Operational Reality

The handbook presents the library example: a neighborhood learning space that should self-organize around genuine community needs. The claim is that structure must follow practice. What the example does not document is how many neighborhood spaces fail before they reach that point.

Books disappear. Meeting rooms are booked by the same three people. New members cannot find anything. Quiet study areas become social clubs that scare off older learners. Not because people are malicious — because useful friction requires intentional design that a self-organizing group has no mechanism to produce.

P2PU shows that even successful peer learning benefits from intentional role design — defined facilitator roles and institutional partnerships create the minimum coordination that makes participation sustainable. OpenStreetMap shows that formal dispute resolution bodies resolve conflicts that informal consensus cannot. Drupal shows that structure must be designed, not awaited. The IETF shows that even the most peer-driven standards body needs formal fallback mechanisms.

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if the structures we resist most are exactly the ones that would let us take bigger creative risks?"*

### Empirical Evidence

**Resilience Cases (Confirming):**
- [P2PU Learning Circles](https://www.p2pu.org/en/learning-circles/) — Peer learning models with defined facilitator roles and institutional partnerships are more sustainable than purely peer-driven learning. Even successful peer learning benefits from intentional role design.
- [OpenStreetMap Data Working Group](https://wiki.osmfoundation.org/wiki/Data_Working_Group) — Formal dispute resolution bodies with binding authority resolve peer production conflicts that informal consensus cannot. Structure enables resolution, not paralysis.

**Fracture Cases (Complicating):**
- [Drupal Governance](https://www.drupal.org/governance) — Governance transition from BDFL to community model required years of structural work. The assumption that structure follows content is contradicted by Drupal: structure must be designed, not awaited.
- [IETF About](https://www.ietf.org/about/) — Rough consensus governance only works when backed by hierarchical escalation paths. Even the most peer-driven standards body needs formal fallback mechanisms.

### Operational Heuristics

**Positive Boundary:** Apply this pattern only when the group's primary purpose is exploration and relationship-building — and even then, define minimal roles to sustain coordination.

**Negative Boundary:** Do not apply this pattern when the group has defined deliverables or when conflicts require resolution — formal dispute resolution with binding authority resolves conflicts that informal consensus cannot handle.

### Epistemic Synthesis

The central assumption — that structure must follow content — is contradicted by the evidence. Every successful peer production case in the archive required intentional structural design. Structure does not kill creativity — it kills the excuse for not delivering. The theoretical pattern is useful only if read backwards: not "structure after content" but "minimum structure as prerequisite for content."
