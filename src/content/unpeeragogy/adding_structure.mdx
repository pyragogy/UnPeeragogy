## Summary

Peer groups avoid structure to preserve creativity. Theory says less structure means more learning. Practice shows the opposite — P2PU learning circles work because they have facilitator roles and institutional backing.

## Evidenze Grounded

### 🔹 Confirming (2)

**Caso 1 — P2PU learning circles sustainability**
- **Fenomeno:** Peer learning models with defined facilitator roles and institutional partnerships are more sustainable than purely peer-driven learning.
- **Fonte:** [https://www.p2pu.org/en/learning-circles/](https://www.p2pu.org/en/learning-circles/)

**Caso 2 — OpenStreetMap Data Working Group resolution**
- **Fenomeno:** Formal dispute resolution bodies with binding authority and published reasoning resolve peer production conflicts that informal consensus cannot.
- **Fonte:** [https://wiki.osmfoundation.org/wiki/Data_Working_Group](https://wiki.osmfoundation.org/wiki/Data_Working_Group)

### 🔸 Complicating (2)

**Caso 1 — Drupal governance / community working groups**
- **Fenomeno:** Intentional governance transition from BDFL to community model requires years of structural work, not organic emergence.
- **Fonte:** [https://www.drupal.org/governance](https://www.drupal.org/governance)

**Caso 2 — IETF working group rough consensus**
- **Fenomeno:** Rough consensus governance only works when backed by hierarchical escalation paths; pure consensus without appeals fails.
- **Fonte:** [https://www.ietf.org/about/](https://www.ietf.org/about/)

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern works when the group's primary purpose is exploration and relationship-building rather than production. P2PU learning circles show that even successful peer learning benefits from defined facilitator roles and institutional partnerships for sustainability.

### Negative Rule

> Do not apply this pattern when the group has defined deliverables or when conflicts require resolution. OpenStreetMap's Data Working Group shows that formal dispute resolution with binding authority resolves peer production conflicts that informal consensus cannot handle.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern claims structure kills peer creativity and spontaneous learning. Partial verification — it works when groups prioritize exploration over delivery. P2PU learning circles confirm that even peer learning needs defined roles for sustainability, while OpenStreetMap's Data Working Group shows formal dispute resolution handles conflicts that consensus cannot. But the pattern misses the timing question. Structure before content creation versus structure after conflict. The real mechanism isn't about more or less — it's about when.
