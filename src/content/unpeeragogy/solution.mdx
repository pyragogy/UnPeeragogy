### Il Dogma

Solution-oriented approaches where peer learning groups identify problems collectively and develop responses through shared facilitation and distributed leadership.

### La Realtà

Peer-driven solutions work great until someone has to make a decision that pisses people off.

P2PU's learning circles succeed through institutional partnerships that provide legitimacy when peer decisions need enforcement. GitLab's stewardship handbook documents explicit governance boundaries that activate when consensus fails. But IETF's rough consensus model shows that pure peer solution-seeking fails when decisions are contested — the escalation path is invisible until it's needed. Wikipedia's ArbCom exists because peer solutions without binding authority produce endless debate.

The pattern assumes collective problem-solving is sufficient. The evidence shows it's necessary but not sufficient. The missing piece is the authority to end solution-seeking and commit.

### Perturbatore

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if peer-driven solutions work great until someone has to make a decision that pisses people off — and the missing piece is the authority to end solution-seeking and commit?"*

### Evidenze di Tenuta

**p2pu.org** — Peer learning models with defined facilitator roles and institutional partnerships are more sustainable. P2PU shows that institutional legitimacy enables peer solution enforcement.

**about.gitlab.com** — Explicit documentation of governance boundaries reduces friction. GitLab demonstrates that documented governance boundaries activate when peer solutions reach consensus limits.

### Evidenze di Faglia

**ietf.org** — Rough consensus governance fails without hierarchical escalation paths. IETF shows that pure peer solution-seeking fails when decisions are contested.

**en.wikipedia.org** — Wikipedia's Arbitration Committee exists because peer solutions without binding authority produce endless debate. ArbCom demonstrates that peer governance requires a formal judicial layer.

### Euristiche Operative

**Regola Positiva:** Questo pattern funziona quando partnership istituzionali forniscono legittimità e risorse, o quando confini di governance espliciti definiscono l'autorità decisionale. P2PU e GitLab mostrano che il peer solution-seeking ha bisogno di enforcement.

**Regola Negativa:** Non applicare questo pattern per decisioni ad alto rischio o contestate senza chiari meccanismi di escalation. IETF e Wikipedia mostrano che il puro consenso peer fallisce quando le decisioni sono contestate.
