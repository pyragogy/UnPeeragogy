### The Assumption

Solution-oriented approaches where peer learning groups identify problems collectively and develop responses through shared facilitation and distributed leadership.

### Operational Reality

Peer-driven solutions work great until someone has to make a decision that pisses people off.

P2PU's learning circles succeed through institutional partnerships that provide legitimacy when peer decisions need enforcement. GitLab's stewardship handbook documents explicit governance boundaries that activate when consensus fails. But IETF's rough consensus model shows that pure peer solution-seeking fails when decisions are contested — the escalation path is invisible until it is needed. Wikipedia's ArbCom exists because peer solutions without binding authority produce endless debate.

The pattern assumes collective problem-solving is sufficient. The evidence shows it is necessary but not sufficient. The missing piece is the authority to end solution-seeking and commit.

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if peer-driven solutions work great until someone has to make a decision that pisses people off — and the missing piece is the authority to end solution-seeking and commit?"*

### Empirical Evidence

**Resilience Cases (Confirming):**
- [P2PU Learning Circles](https://www.p2pu.org/en/learning-circles/) — Peer learning models with defined facilitator roles and institutional partnerships are more sustainable than purely peer-driven learning. Institutional legitimacy enables peer solution enforcement.
- [GitLab Stewardship Handbook](https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/) — Explicit documentation of governance boundaries between corporate sponsor and community reduces friction in open-core projects. Documented governance boundaries activate when peer solutions reach consensus limits.

**Fracture Cases (Complicating):**
- [IETF Rough Consensus](https://www.ietf.org/about/) — Rough consensus governance only works when backed by hierarchical escalation paths. Pure peer solution-seeking fails when decisions are contested.
- [Wikipedia Arbitration Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arbitration_Committee_(Wikimedia)) — Peer solutions without binding authority produce endless debate. Peer governance requires a formal judicial layer.

### Operational Heuristics

**Positive Boundary:** This pattern works when institutional partnerships provide legitimacy and resources, or when explicit governance boundaries define decision-making authority — peer solution-seeking needs enforcement mechanisms.

**Negative Boundary:** Do not apply this pattern for high-stakes or contested decisions without clear escalation mechanisms — pure peer consensus fails when decisions are contested.

### Epistemic Synthesis

Collective problem-solving is necessary but not sufficient. The missing piece is the authority to end solution-seeking and commit. P2PU and GitLab show that peer solution-seeking needs enforcement. IETF and Wikipedia show that it fails when decisions are contested. Peer-driven solutions work until someone has to make a decision that pisses people off.
