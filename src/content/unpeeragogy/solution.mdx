## Summary

Solutions stop being solutions once they work. The theory says convene, organize, cooperate — practice shows you need someone willing to make unpopular decisions. Pure peer consensus breaks when stakes get real.

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern works when institutional partnerships provide legitimacy and resources, or when explicit governance boundaries define decision-making authority. P2PU learning circles and GitLab stewardship models show that hybrid approaches with clear institutional backing produce sustainable peer-driven solutions.

### Negative Rule

> Do not apply this pattern for high-stakes or contested decisions without clear escalation mechanisms. IETF rough consensus and Wikipedia ArbCom cases show that pure peer consensus fails when decisions carry significant consequences or involve irreconcilable differences.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern claims peer-driven solutions emerge through structured cooperation. Partial verification — but only when backed by institutions or explicit hierarchies. P2PU learning circles work because libraries provide legitimacy, GitLab stewardship works because corporate boundaries are explicit. IETF rough consensus and Wikipedia ArbCom fail when decisions carry weight and someone has to say no. The missing piece is authority distribution. Peer solutions need institutional fuel.
