"The Newcomer enters a group and immediately becomes a problem." Not because they're incompetent. Because they're new.

**Why it matters:** Every existing group has equilibrium. The members know each other, know the rhythms, know the unwritten rules. The newcomer arrives and breaks everything. They ask questions that veterans consider obvious. They need explanations that veterans don't want to give. They challenge assumptions that veterans have internalized. The group reacts with tolerance (visible) and resentment (hidden).

The theory presents the newcomer as a resource — fresh eyes, alternative perspectives. True. But the theory doesn't calculate the cost. Integrating a newcomer requires energy. Someone must explain, mentor, onboard. That energy comes from the veterans' production time. The group slows down. The cost is real.

**What to do instead:** Acknowledge the cost. Not everything should be optimized for newcomer integration. Sometimes the group needs production, not growth. Know the trade-off. If you accept a newcomer, accept the slowdown. If you can't afford the slowdown, don't accept the newcomer. Cruel. Honest.

> **Perturbator:** *"The Newcomer arrives. The group smiles. The group also slows down. Someone has to explain the patterns, the history, the jokes. Production drops. Resentment builds. The secret everyone knows: welcoming a newcomer costs energy. Most groups pretend it doesn't. Then they wonder why they're tired."*
