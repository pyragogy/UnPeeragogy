### Il Dogma

Welcoming newcomers requires balancing energy investment in onboarding against the disruption to existing group dynamics, with veterans potentially resenting the attention given to newcomers.

### La Realtà

Maria joins the neighborhood mutual aid network after six months of watching from the sidewalk. She shows up to the monthly meeting with a notebook full of ideas about food distribution efficiency. The organizers smile and nod while she talks about route optimization and inventory tracking. Three weeks later, she stops coming to meetings.

Not because they were mean to her. Not because they shut down her suggestions. Not because she felt unwelcome — everyone was genuinely kind, patient, encouraging even. She left because being nice to newcomers sidesteps the harder question: what happens when the newcomer's fresh perspective conflicts with how things actually get done?

The mutual aid group had spent two years building relationships with corner store owners who let them use back rooms for free. Maria's efficiency improvements would have required renting warehouse space and buying software. Her ideas weren't wrong — they were incompatible with the social infrastructure that made the work possible in the first place.

### Perturbatore

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if being nice to newcomers doesn't solve the problem of what happens when they disagree with you — and the newcomer's disruption isn't a bug to be managed, but useful friction that exposes what the system actually depends on?"*

### Evidenze di Tenuta

**wiki.osmfoundation.org** — Formal dispute resolution bodies with binding authority and published reasoning resolve peer production conflicts that informal consensus cannot. OpenStreetMap shows that structured authority resolves integration disputes that consensus cannot handle.

### Evidenze di Faglia

**w3.org** — Choosing lighter governance models for standards development trades authority for speed, creating different failure modes for peer participation. WebAssembly shows that lightweight governance without escalation paths leaves newcomer-veteran conflicts irresolvable.

**ietf.org** — Rough consensus governance only works when backed by hierarchical escalation paths; pure consensus without appeals fails. IETF demonstrates that consensus-based approaches produce irresolvable conflicts when newcomers challenge veteran practices.

### Euristiche Operative

**Regola Positiva:** L'accoglienza dei newcomer funziona quando esistono corpi formali di risoluzione dispute con autorità vincolante per gestire i conflitti veterano-nuovo arrivato. OpenStreetMap mostra che l'autorità strutturata risolve dispute di integrazione che il consenso informale non può gestire.

**Regola Negativa:** Non applicare questo pattern quando il gruppo manca di meccanismi formali di risoluzione dispute. IETF mostra che il rough consensus senza escalation paths crea conflitti irrisolvibili quando i newcomer sfidano le pratiche dei veterani — la cortesia senza autorità non basta.

### Sintesi & Tracciabilità

**Riferimento:** audit-v2 (delta tensione: 1.6 → 1.7792, +0.1792)
**Evidenze esaminate:** 3 (1 confermante, 2 complicanti)
**Stato:** PROVVISORIO — tensione alta

Accogliere i newcomer richiede più che coltivare la beginner's mind — richiede un riconoscimento onesto delle strutture di potere esistenti. La risoluzione dispute formale funziona proprio perché rende visibile l'autorità invece di nasconderla dietro il teatro del consenso. Quando i suggerimenti di Maria furono assorbiti in sessioni di ascolto cortesi, il vero processo decisionale rimase invisibile per lei. La tensione non è tra vecchie e nuove prospettive. È tra la mitologia della partecipazione aperta e la realtà che tutti i sistemi hanno dipendenze non negoziabili — relazioni, risorse, accordi non detti che i newcomer non possono vedere.
