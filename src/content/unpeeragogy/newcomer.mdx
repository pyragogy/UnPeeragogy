## Summary

Newcomers break things by design. Theory says welcome them with beginner's mind — practice shows they challenge veteran authority and create disputes that informal consensus cannot resolve. The real question is whether your group has binding arbitration when nice stops working.

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern works when formal dispute resolution bodies with binding authority exist to handle newcomer-veteran conflicts. The OpenStreetMap Data Working Group shows that structured authority resolves integration disputes that informal consensus cannot, enabling sustainable newcomer onboarding.

### Negative Rule

> Do not apply this pattern when the group lacks formal dispute resolution mechanisms. The IETF working group experience shows that rough consensus without appeals creates irresolvable conflicts when newcomers challenge veteran practices, leading to group fragmentation.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern assumes newcomer disruption can be managed through cultivated openness and beginner's mind. Partial verification — but only when formal dispute resolution exists. OpenStreetMap's Data Working Group proves that binding authority resolves integration conflicts that consensus cannot handle, while WebAssembly's lighter governance trades authority for speed, creating different failure modes entirely. The IETF's rough consensus without appeals shows how newcomer challenges fragment groups when escalation paths don't exist. The pattern describes the symptom, not the infrastructure. Nice is the starting position, not the engine.
