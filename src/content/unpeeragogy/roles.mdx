"Roles: assignments of responsibility in the group." "Who does what."

**Why it matters:** Roles are never neutral. Every role distribution is a power distribution. The person who manages the agenda has power over the meeting. The person who manages the platform has power over communication. The person who manages the budget has power over resources.

The theory presents roles as functional. "We need someone to facilitate." Practice knows that the person who facilitates also influences decisions. The role amplifies their voice. The group pretends it's flat. But roles create micro-hierarchies. Every role is a small throne. Small thrones are still thrones.

**What to do instead:** Roles should rotate. If the same person facilitates for six months, they're not facilitating. They're leading. Rotation keeps roles functional, not hierarchical. But rotation creates inefficiency. The new person takes time to learn. The trade-off is between efficiency and equality. Choose consciously. Don't pretend the trade-off doesn't exist.

> **Perturbator:** *"Roles are power. You say 'roles are functional distribution.' They are. They're also power distribution. The person who decides the agenda decides what's discussed. The person who decides what's discussed decides what's decided. Roles are stairs. The group is flat until someone climbs. And someone always climbs."*
