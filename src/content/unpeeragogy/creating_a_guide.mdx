### Il Dogma

Creating guides is a collaborative process where experienced members share knowledge with newcomers, fostering inclusive participation through accessible documentation. The theory assumes that comprehensive guides reduce barriers to entry — that more documentation means more access.

### La Realtà

Nothing says "go away" like a 47-page onboarding guide written by people who forgot what it's like to not know anything. I've watched this happen in makerspaces, open source projects, and community initiatives. The founders create elaborate documentation — flowcharts, decision trees, comprehensive FAQs — believing they're helping newcomers navigate complexity. The opposite occurs. Each additional page becomes another barrier, another signal that this space wasn't built for someone like you.

Not unclear writing. Not missing information. Not poor organization. The guide itself becomes the gatekeeper — a labyrinth that sorts the dedicated from the merely curious. The people who built it can no longer see what they've made because they know all the shortcuts.

The honest admission: every guide I've written has this problem.

### Perturbatore

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if the best guides are the ones that admit they can't guide you — and complex governance processes manufacture participation barriers that entrench existing contributors instead of welcoming newcomers?"*

### Evidenze di Faglia

**wikidata.org** — Complex governance processes in peer production projects create participation barriers that entrench existing contributors and exclude newcomers. Wikidata demonstrates that comprehensive documentation doesn't welcome — it sorts.

**drupal.org** — Intentional governance transition from BDFL to community model required years of structural work, not organic emergence. Drupal shows that even well-documented projects create invisible barriers through the sheer complexity of their governance processes.

### Euristiche Operative

**Regola Positiva:** Le guide funzionano quando vengono testate con veri newcomer e riviste in base al loro feedback. Nessuna evidenza confermante nell'archivio corrente lo dimostra ancora, ma il pattern ha successo quando la complessità della guida è inversamente proporzionale al supporto umano disponibile.

**Regola Negativa:** Non assumere che guide comprehensive aumentino la partecipazione. Wikidata dimostra che la documentazione di governance complessa crea barriere che consolidano i contributori esistenti ed escludono i newcomer — trasformando la guida da ponte a muro.

La più alta variazione di tensione nel corpus. Wikidata e Drupal mostrano che le guide comprehensive creano l'opposto del loro effetto inteso — diventano muri invece che ponti. I processi di governance complessi non documentano la complessità: la fabbricano, trasformando la partecipazione in un credenziale che i newcomer devono guadagnare invece che uno spazio che possono semplicemente attraversare. La domanda non è come fare la mappa perfetta, ma se il territorio stesso deve restare parzialmente non mappato.
