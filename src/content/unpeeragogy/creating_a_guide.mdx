### The Assumption

Creating guides is a collaborative process where experienced members share knowledge with newcomers, fostering inclusive participation through accessible documentation. Comprehensive guides reduce barriers to entry — more documentation means more access.

### Operational Reality

Nothing says "go away" like a 47-page onboarding guide written by people who forgot what it is like to not know anything. This pattern repeats across makerspaces, open source projects, and community initiatives. The founders create elaborate documentation — flowcharts, decision trees, comprehensive FAQs — believing they are helping newcomers navigate complexity. The opposite occurs. Each additional page becomes another barrier, another signal that this space was not built for someone like you.

Not unclear writing. Not missing information. Not poor organization. The guide itself becomes the gatekeeper — a labyrinth that sorts the dedicated from the merely curious. The people who built it can no longer see what they have made because they know all the shortcuts.

Wikidata demonstrates that complex governance processes in peer production projects create participation barriers that entrench existing contributors and exclude newcomers. Drupal shows that even well-documented projects create invisible barriers through the sheer complexity of their governance processes.

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if the best guides are the ones that admit they cannot guide you — and complex governance processes manufacture participation barriers that entrench existing contributors instead of welcoming newcomers?"*

### Empirical Evidence

**Resilience Cases (Confirming):**
- *None in the current corpus. This is a gap: no documented cases of guides successfully reducing barriers exist in the audit archive.*

**Fracture Cases (Complicating):**
- [Wikidata Governance](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Wikidata:Governance) — Complex governance processes in peer production projects create participation barriers that entrench existing contributors and exclude newcomers. Comprehensive documentation does not welcome — it sorts.
- [Drupal Governance](https://www.drupal.org/governance) — Intentional governance transition from BDFL to community model required years of structural work, not organic emergence. Even well-documented projects create invisible barriers through the sheer complexity of their governance processes.

### Operational Heuristics

**Positive Boundary:** Guides function when tested with actual newcomers and revised based on their feedback — guide complexity should be inversely proportional to available human support.

**Negative Boundary:** Do not assume comprehensive guides increase participation — complex governance documentation creates barriers that consolidate existing contributors and exclude newcomers, transforming the guide from bridge to wall.

### Epistemic Synthesis

The highest tension delta in the corpus. Wikidata and Drupal show that comprehensive guides create the opposite of their intended effect — they become walls instead of bridges. Complex governance processes do not document complexity: they manufacture it, transforming participation into a credential newcomers must earn instead of a space they can simply enter. The question is not how to make the perfect map, but whether the territory itself must remain partially unmapped.
