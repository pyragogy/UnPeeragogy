## Summary

Documentation that hides governance fights teaches collaboration without showing how it breaks. Theory says peer learning needs clean patterns. Practice shows that GitLab handbooks and OpenStreetMap dispute logs work because they document the friction, not despite it.

## Evidenze Grounded

### 🔹 Confirming (2)

**Caso 1 — GitLab stewardship handbook**
- **Fenomeno:** Explicit documentation of governance boundaries between corporate sponsor and community reduces friction in open-core projects.
- **Fonte:** [https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/](https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/)

**Caso 2 — OpenStreetMap Data Working Group resolution**
- **Fenomeno:** Formal dispute resolution bodies with binding authority and published reasoning resolve peer production conflicts that informal consensus cannot.
- **Fonte:** [https://wiki.osmfoundation.org/wiki/Data_Working_Group](https://wiki.osmfoundation.org/wiki/Data_Working_Group)

### 🔸 Complicating (2)

**Caso 1 — WebAssembly Community Group governance**
- **Fenomeno:** Choosing lighter governance models for standards development trades authority for speed, creating different failure modes for peer participation.
- **Fonte:** [https://www.w3.org/community/webassembly/](https://www.w3.org/community/webassembly/)

**Caso 2 — Wikipedia Arbitration Committee (ArbCom)**
- **Fenomeno:** Peer production communities develop formal judicial bodies when informal consensus fails, contradicting the assumption that peers can self-govern without hierarchy.
- **Fonte:** [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arbitration_Committee_(Wikimedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arbitration_Committee_(Wikimedia))

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern works when documentation includes explicit governance boundaries and formal dispute resolution mechanisms with published reasoning. GitLab's stewardship handbook, OpenStreetMap's Data Working Group, and Wikipedia's Arbitration Committee all demonstrate that transparency about conflict resolution enables sustainable peer collaboration.

### Negative Rule

> Do not curate out governance conflicts and dispute resolution mechanisms when documenting peer learning patterns. GitLab, OpenStreetMap, and Wikipedia all show that sanitized documentation fails to prepare participants for the governance tensions that every peer production system faces.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern claims sanitized documentation protects learners from messy governance reality. Partial verification — when you look at what actually sustains peer production. GitLab's stewardship boundaries and OpenStreetMap's Data Working Group both work precisely because they document conflicts with published reasoning, while WebAssembly's lighter governance shows the limits of consensus-only approaches. But the real mechanism isn't transparency about conflict resolution — it's that documenting friction creates the scaffolding for navigating it. Surgery without blood isn't surgery.
