### Il Dogma

The Peeragogy Handbook's Outtakes section presents curated material while omitting conflicts and arguments, preferring comfort over uncomfortable truths rather than documenting the real fights and disagreements that occurred.

### La Realtà

Every peer project has a cutting room floor. The design sprint where two team members stopped speaking. The funding proposal that revealed who really controlled the roadmap. The community vote that was decided before the votes were cast.

The handbook's Outtakes section curates these moments into neat lessons. It's not dishonest — it's editing. But editing decisions are governance decisions, and treating them as editorial neuters their political weight.

GitLab's stewardship handbook doesn't hide its governance conflicts. It documents corporate-community boundaries explicitly, naming what cannot be negotiated. OpenStreetMap's Data Working Group publishes its dispute resolutions with reasoning attached. Wikipedia's Arbitration Committee creates a permanent record of community conflicts that consensus failed to resolve.

The handbook's outtakes could do the same. Instead, they offer comfortable reflection. And comfort is the enemy of learning when the lesson is about power.

### Perturbatore

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if curating out the fights is like teaching surgery without showing the blood — and the outtakes section is where the real manual should begin?"*

### Evidenze di Tenuta

**about.gitlab.com** — Explicit documentation of governance boundaries between corporate sponsor and community reduces friction in open-core projects. GitLab shows that documentation including governance conflicts prepares participants for real tensions.

**wiki.osmfoundation.org** — Formal dispute resolution bodies with binding authority and published reasoning resolve peer production conflicts. OpenStreetMap demonstrates that publishing dispute reasoning teaches governance more effectively than curated lessons.

### Evidenze di Faglia

**w3.org** — Choosing lighter governance models for standards development trades authority for speed, creating different failure modes. WebAssembly shows that sanitizing governance trade-offs creates false expectations about peer participation.

**en.wikipedia.org** — Wikipedia's Arbitration Committee publishes permanent records of community conflicts that consensus failed to resolve. ArbCom demonstrates that even the most transparent peer community requires a formal conflict layer — the outtakes are governance, not editorial.

### Euristiche Operative

**Regola Positiva:** La documentazione funziona quando include confini di governance espliciti e meccanismi formali di risoluzione dispute con ragionamento pubblicato. GitLab, OpenStreetMap e Wikipedia mostrano che dokumentare i conflitti prepara i partecipanti alle tensioni di governance reali.

**Regola Negativa:** Non curare i conflitti di governance dalla documentazione dei pattern peer. GitLab, OpenStreetMap e Wikipedia mostrano che la documentazione sanificata non prepara i partecipanti alle tensioni di governance che incontreranno inevitabilmente.

### Sintesi & Tracciabilità

**Riferimento:** audit-v2 (delta tensione: 1.3 → 1.2533, −0.0467)
**Evidenze esaminate:** 4 (2 confermanti, 2 complicanti)
**Stato:** PROVVISORIO

Ogni progetto peer ha una sala di taglio. Il fidato che documenta i confini di governance non nasconde i suoi conflitti — li nomina esplicitamente. La risoluzione dispute di OpenStreetMap pubblica il ragionamento. L'ArbCom di Wikipedia crea un record permanente dei conflitti che il consenso non ha risolto. Le outtakes del manuale potrebbero fare lo stesso. Invece offrono riflessione confortevole. E il comfort è nemico dell'apprendimento quando la lezione riguarda il potere.
