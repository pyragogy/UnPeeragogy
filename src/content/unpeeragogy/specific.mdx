"The pattern described is specific to a context, but the derived pattern may be used in other contexts."

**Why it matters:** Every pattern comes from a specific context. The Handbook was written by a specific group. Their problems, their solutions, their language. Patterns are extracted from that context. They may not apply to yours.

The specificity problem is rarely discussed. The Handbook presents patterns as universal. They're not. The pattern that worked for a university group may not work for a community group. The pattern that worked for a face-to-face group may not work for a remote group. Context is everything.

**What to do instead:** If you take a pattern from the Handbook, test it in your context. Don't assume it works. Test it. Modify it. Or discard it. A pattern that doesn't fit your context is not a pattern. It's a story about someone else's context. Interesting. Not useful.

> **Perturbator:** *"Your group is different from the group that wrote the Handbook. Your context is different. Your resources are different. Your people are different. Why would the same pattern work? It might. It might not. The Handbook doesn't tell you how to adapt. It tells you what worked for them. You need to figure out what works for you. That's the real work."*
