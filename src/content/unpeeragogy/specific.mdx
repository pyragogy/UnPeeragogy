### The Assumption

Patterns must be adapted to specific contexts rather than applied universally. Assuming a pattern works without testing it in your context can lead to failure.

### Operational Reality

Every consultant says "it depends on your context" — that is not wisdom, that is a billable hour.

The pattern is trivially true and practically useless. Of course context matters. The question is how to adapt. GitLab's stewardship handbook does not advise "adapt to context" — it documents specific governance boundaries that transfer across contexts. OpenStreetMap's Data Working Group does not recommend "test in your environment" — it publishes enforceable dispute resolution mechanisms. WebAssembly's lighter governance model shows that even intentional context-specific choices have predictable failure modes. Drupal's governance transition demonstrates that context adaptation without governance design produces the same failure modes regardless of context.

The pattern mistakes truism for insight. The adaptation that matters is structural, not attitudinal. Document what will not change, not what might.

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if every consultant says 'it depends on your context' — that's not wisdom, that's a billable hour — and the adaptation that matters is structural, not attitudinal?"*

### Empirical Evidence

**Resilience Cases (Confirming):**
- [GitLab Stewardship Handbook](https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/) — Explicit documentation of governance boundaries between corporate sponsor and community reduces friction. Cross-context adaptation works when governance boundaries are explicitly documented, not when adaptation is advised generically.
- [OpenStreetMap Data Working Group](https://wiki.osmfoundation.org/wiki/Data_Working_Group) — Formal dispute resolution bodies with binding authority and published reasoning resolve peer production conflicts. Portable governance mechanisms outperform context-specific advice.

**Fracture Cases (Complicating):**
- [WebAssembly Community Group](https://www.w3.org/community/webassembly/) — Choosing lighter governance models for standards development trades authority for speed, creating different failure modes. Even intentional context-specific choices have predictable failure modes.
- [Drupal Governance](https://www.drupal.org/governance) — Intentional governance transition from BDFL to community model required years of structural work. Context adaptation without governance design produces the same failure modes regardless of context.

### Operational Heuristics

**Positive Boundary:** Contextual adaptation works when it includes explicit documentation of governance boundaries and formal mechanisms for handling edge cases — portable mechanisms outperform context-specific advice.

**Negative Boundary:** Do not apply this pattern by merely advising "adapt to your context" without defining adaptation mechanisms — even intentional context-specific choices have predictable failure modes.

### Epistemic Synthesis

The pattern is trivially true and practically useless. Of course context matters. The question is how to adapt. GitLab does not advise "adapt to context" — it documents specific governance boundaries that transfer across contexts. OpenStreetMap publishes enforceable dispute resolution mechanisms. The adaptation that matters is structural, not attitudinal. Document what will not change, not what might.
