### Il Dogma

Patterns must be adapted to specific contexts rather than applied universally. Assuming a pattern works without testing it in your context can lead to failure.

### La Realtà

Every consultant says "it depends on your context" — that's not wisdom, that's a billable hour.

The pattern is trivially true and practically useless. Of course context matters. The question is how to adapt. GitLab's stewardship handbook doesn't advise "adapt to context" — it documents specific governance boundaries that transfer across contexts. OpenStreetMap's Data Working Group doesn't recommend "test in your environment" — it publishes enforceable dispute resolution mechanisms. WebAssembly's lighter governance model shows that even intentional context-specific choices have predictable failure modes.

The pattern mistakes truism for insight. The adaptation that matters is structural, not attitudinal. Document what won't change, not what might.

### Perturbatore

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if every consultant says 'it depends on your context' — that's not wisdom, that's a billable hour — and the adaptation that matters is structural, not attitudinal?"*

### Evidenze di Tenuta

**about.gitlab.com** — Explicit documentation of governance boundaries reduces friction. GitLab shows that cross-context adaptation works when governance boundaries are explicitly documented, not when adaptation is advised generically.

**wiki.osmfoundation.org** — Formal dispute resolution bodies with binding authority publish enforceable mechanisms. OpenStreetMap demonstrates that portable governance mechanisms outperform context-specific advice.

### Evidenze di Faglia

**w3.org** — Choosing lighter governance models trades authority for speed. WebAssembly shows that even intentional context-specific choices have predictable failure modes.

**drupal.org** — Intentional governance transition required years of structural work. Drupal demonstrates that context adaptation without governance design produces the same failure modes regardless of context.

### Euristiche Operative

**Regola Positiva:** L'adattamento contestuale funziona quando include documentazione esplicita dei confini di governance e meccanismi formali per gestire i casi limite. GitLab e OpenStreetMap mostrano che i meccanismi portabili funzionano meglio dei consigli specifici al contesto.

**Regola Negativa:** Non applicare questo pattern limitandosi a consigliare "adatta al tuo contesto" senza definire meccanismi di adattamento. WebAssembly mostra che anche scelte contestuali intenzionali hanno modalità di fallimento prevedibili.

**Stato:** PROVVISORIO

Il pattern è banalmente vero e praticamente inutile. Certo che il contesto conta. La domanda è come adattarsi. GitLab non consiglia "adatta al contesto" — documenta confini di governance specifici che trasferiscono attraverso contesti. OpenStreetMap pubblica meccanismi di risoluzione dispute applicabili. L'adattamento che conta è strutturale, non attitudinale.
