## Summary

Context adaptation without structure is consulting theater. The pattern claims specificity beats generalization — but only when you document boundaries and build mechanisms for edge cases. Otherwise you're just saying 'it depends' with extra steps.

## Evidenze Grounded

### 🔹 Confirming (2)

**Caso 1 — GitLab stewardship handbook**
- **Fenomeno:** Explicit documentation of governance boundaries between corporate sponsor and community reduces friction in open-core projects.
- **Fonte:** [https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/](https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/)

**Caso 2 — OpenStreetMap Data Working Group resolution**
- **Fenomeno:** Formal dispute resolution bodies with binding authority and published reasoning resolve peer production conflicts that informal consensus cannot.
- **Fonte:** [https://wiki.osmfoundation.org/wiki/Data_Working_Group](https://wiki.osmfoundation.org/wiki/Data_Working_Group)

### 🔸 Complicating (2)

**Caso 1 — WebAssembly Community Group governance**
- **Fenomeno:** Choosing lighter governance models for standards development trades authority for speed, creating different failure modes for peer participation.
- **Fonte:** [https://www.w3.org/community/webassembly/](https://www.w3.org/community/webassembly/)

**Caso 2 — Drupal governance / community working groups**
- **Fenomeno:** Intentional governance transition from BDFL to community model requires years of structural work, not organic emergence.
- **Fonte:** [https://www.drupal.org/governance](https://www.drupal.org/governance)

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern works when context adaptation includes explicit documentation of governance boundaries and formal mechanisms for handling edge cases. GitLab's stewardship handbook and OpenStreetMap's Data Working Group show that successful context-specific governance requires systematic structural work, not intuitive adjustment.

### Negative Rule

> Do not apply this pattern by simply advising 'adapt to your context' without defining adaptation mechanisms. WebAssembly's lighter governance model shows that even intentional context-specific choices create different failure modes if the structural implications are not systematically analyzed.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern promises that context-specific governance beats generic templates. Partial verification — when adaptation includes explicit boundary documentation. GitLab's corporate-community handbook and OpenStreetMap's binding dispute resolution prove systematic structural work succeeds where intuitive adjustment fails, while WebAssembly's intentionally lighter governance shows even deliberate context choices create different failure modes than expected. The real mechanism isn't context sensitivity — it's the boring work of documenting what doesn't transfer. Quietly, if necessary.
