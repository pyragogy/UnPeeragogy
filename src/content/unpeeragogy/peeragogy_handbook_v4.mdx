## Summary

Peer learning handbooks accumulate contributors but lose editors. Theory promises emergent curation — practice delivers knowledge bloat. The difference between a living document and a digital hoarder house is removal mechanisms.

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern works when explicit stewardship roles and content governance boundaries are established before community contribution begins. GitLab's stewardship handbook shows that documented boundaries between different stakeholder roles reduce friction and enable sustainable knowledge curation.

### Negative Rule

> Do not apply this pattern when content curation lacks explicit governance boundaries. The Peeragogy Handbook's own evolution shows that organic community contribution without removal mechanisms produces knowledge bloat and decision-stagnation rather than iterative refinement.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern claims organic peer contribution creates iterative refinement through collective intelligence. Partial verification — when explicit stewardship boundaries exist first. GitLab's documented governance reduces friction by naming who removes what, while Drupal's transition required years of structural work to move beyond benevolent dictatorship. But the Peeragogy Handbook itself shows the missing piece: decision-making authority for pruning obsolete content. Addition is democratic. Subtraction needs a scalpel.
