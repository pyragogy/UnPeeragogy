## Summary

Visibility doesn't dissolve power — it reorganizes it. GitLab's explicit governance boundaries work because they create structural limits, not because they expose hidden dynamics. The difference between documentation and redistribution is the difference between knowing where the lever is and being able to pull it.

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern works when visibility is paired with explicit redistribution mechanisms. GitLab's stewardship handbook shows that documented governance boundaries between different stakeholder groups can reduce friction by creating structural limits on power concentration rather than relying on informal negotiation.

### Negative Rule

> Do not assume that making power dynamics visible will automatically dissolve them. Wikipedia's transparent consensus processes and Debian's public leadership communications show that visibility can institutionalize power imbalances rather than eliminate them when structural redistribution mechanisms are absent.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern claims that making power visible will naturally lead to more equitable distribution. This works partially — when visibility is paired with explicit redistribution mechanisms. GitLab's stewardship handbook creates structural limits between corporate and community interests, while Wikipedia's transparent deletion debates institutionalize participation inequality and Debian's public leadership communications maintain informal hierarchies despite formal openness. The mechanism missing is active redistribution design — visibility alone just gives power better talking points. Dragons with good PR are still dragons.
