## Summary

Peer learning communities fail at institutional translation. Theory assumes shared understanding — practice requires governance structures that administrators recognize. Without explicit roles and measurable outcomes, peeragogy remains invisible to institutional resource allocation.

## Evidenze Grounded

### 🔹 Confirming (2)

**Caso 1 — P2PU learning circles sustainability**
- **Fenomeno:** Peer learning models with defined facilitator roles and institutional partnerships are more sustainable than purely peer-driven learning.
- **Fonte:** [https://www.p2pu.org/en/learning-circles/](https://www.p2pu.org/en/learning-circles/)

**Caso 2 — Fedora Engineering Steering Committee (FESCo)**
- **Fenomeno:** Elected technical steering committees with defined scope and transparent records provide sustainable peer governance for large distributions.
- **Fonte:** [https://docs.fedoraproject.org/en-US/fesco/](https://docs.fedoraproject.org/en-US/fesco/)

### 🔸 Complicating (2)

- **Fenomeno:** Intentional governance transition from BDFL to community model requires years of structural work, not organic emergence.
- **Fonte:** [https://www.drupal.org/governance](https://www.drupal.org/governance)

**Caso 2 — P2P University governance challenges**
- **Fenomeno:** Peer learning communities inevitably face credentialing and quality assurance crises that require either hierarchy or external validation frameworks.
- **Fonte:** [https://www.p2pu.org/en/about/](https://www.p2pu.org/en/about/)

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern works when peer learning communities develop explicit governance structures and institutional translation mechanisms. P2PU learning circles and Fedora's FESCo demonstrate that defined facilitator roles and transparent decision-making processes enable sustainable institutional partnerships while preserving peer learning principles.

### Negative Rule

> Do not apply this pattern when seeking institutional resources or partnerships without translation mechanisms. P2P University's credentialing crisis and Drupal's governance transition show that peer communities without institutional interfaces face legitimacy and sustainability crises.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern claims peer learning can scale through institutional partnerships if communities develop translation mechanisms. Partial verification — P2PU learning circles work because they package peer dynamics in facilitator roles that libraries understand, and Fedora's FESCo provides transparent technical governance that Red Hat can fund. But Drupal's BDFL-to-community transition created decision paralysis, and P2P University's credentialing crisis shows that peer networks without institutional interfaces lose legitimacy when they need resources. The real mechanism isn't translation — it's dual citizenship. Communities that survive institutional engagement maintain two operating systems simultaneously.
