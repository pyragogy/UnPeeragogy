### Il Dogma

Peer learning should be self-evident — so self-evident that explaining it to an institutional authority constitutes a performative contradiction. If you need to argue that peer learning works, you've already lost sight of the principle. The theory holds that peeragogy resists institutional mediation because its value is immediately visible in practice, not in a compliance framework.

### La Realtà

Laura Ritchie walks into the Pro Vice Chancellor's office with printouts and enthusiasm. She's done this before — the careful explanation, the patient demonstration, the hope that institutional weight might finally recognize what's already working in her classroom. The Dean listens politely, nods at appropriate intervals, asks about metrics and scalability.

Not funding. Not infrastructure. Not recognition of existing practice.

The conversation shifts to compliance frameworks and quality assurance. How do you measure peer learning outcomes? Where's the accountability structure? What happens when students teach each other incorrectly? Laura finds herself defending something that doesn't need defense — it works, her students engage, learning happens. But institutional language has no vocabulary for useful friction, for the productive struggle of figuring things out together.

The meeting ends with promises to "explore possibilities" and "circle back." Laura leaves with the same printouts she brought in.

### Perturbatore

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if the Dean's confusion isn't about peeragogy — but about recognizing learning that doesn't fit institutional measurement?"*

### Evidenze di Tenuta

**p2pu.org** — Peer learning models with defined facilitator roles and institutional partnerships are more sustainable than purely peer-driven learning. P2PU's learning circles work because someone designed the container.

**docs.fedoraproject.org** — Elected technical steering committees with defined scope and transparent records provide sustainable peer governance for large distributions. Fedora's FESCo demonstrates that formal structure enables peer production at scale.

### Evidenze di Faglia

**drupal.org** — Intentional governance transition from BDFL to community model requires years of structural work, not organic emergence. The theory's assumption that communities naturally evolve governance is contradicted by the effort Drupal required.

**p2pu.org** — P2PU's own governance challenges reveal that peer learning communities inevitably face credentialing and quality assurance crises that require either hierarchy or external validation frameworks — exactly what the theory claims they don't need.

### Euristiche Operative

**Regola Positiva:** Applica questo pattern quando la comunità di peer learning ha sviluppato strutture di governance esplicite e meccanismi di traduzione istituzionale. P2PU e Fedora dimostrano che ruoli definiti e partner istituzionali rendono la peer production sostenibile.

**Regola Negativa:** Non applicare questo pattern quando si cercano risorse istituzionali o partnership senza meccanismi di traduzione. Entrare in un ufficio istituzionale con la pretesa che il peer learning "si spieghi da solo" non funziona — il Dean non ha un vocabolario per la tensione produttiva.

### Sintesi & Tracciabilità

**Riferimento:** audit-v2 (delta tensione: 1.2 → 1.22, +0.02)
**Evidenze esaminate:** 4 (2 confermanti, 2 complicanti)
**Stato:** PROVVISORIO

Il pattern teorico assume che la validità del peer learning sia immediatamente visibile nella pratica. L'evidenza suggerisce il contrario: ogni modello di peer learning di successo nell'archivio richiedeva design istituzionale esplicito, ruoli definiti e quadri di validazione esterni. La tensione non è pedagogica ma amministrativa — e l'assunzione che "dovrebbe essere ovvio" non è una strategia di funding.
