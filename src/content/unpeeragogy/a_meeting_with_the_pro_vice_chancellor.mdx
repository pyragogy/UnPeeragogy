"Teacher, Peeragogy is a lifestyle for me." "Introducing it to others can be discouraging and confusing." "Show by doing."

The story of a meeting with academic authority. An attempt to explain peeragogy to someone who holds power.

**Why it matters:** Explaining peeragogy to authority is like translating a poem into a language with no rhymes. You lose everything. Horizontal structure, trust, emergence — concepts a dean has never experienced. They understand in theory. Theory isn't enough. The dean asks: "where are the results?" And you must translate "peer learning" into "credit hours." Translation is violence.

The problem isn't the dean. It's that you have to explain. Peeragogy should be so evident in its effectiveness that it needs no explanation. If you have to explain, you're already losing. Because explaining is an act of power. The explainer has more power than the listener. And you're explaining a method that denies hierarchy. It's a performative contradiction.

**What to do instead:** Don't explain peeragogy to the dean. Make it work. Results speak for themselves. If the dean doesn't see results, maybe there are no results. Peeragogy isn't explained. It's practiced. And when it works, it needs no defenders.

> **Perturbator:** *"Don't explain peeragogy to the dean. Make it work. If the dean doesn't see results, maybe there are no results. Maybe you're trying to sell something that doesn't work yet. Peeragogy doesn't get explained. It gets practiced. And when it works, it doesn't need defenders."*
