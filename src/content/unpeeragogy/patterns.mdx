### The Assumption

The Peeragogy Handbook describes patterns as hypotheses rather than guaranteed solutions, acknowledging they may fail when context or people change, yet users seek prescriptive guidance rather than descriptive frameworks.

### Operational Reality

Drupal spent years transitioning from BDFL to community governance. Not because the BDFL pattern was wrong. Not because the community governance pattern was right. Because no pattern survives first contact with organizational legacy without structural work.

The handbook describes patterns as hypotheses. This is intellectually honest — patterns work in some contexts, fail in others, and evolve with use. But groups seeking guidance do not want a hypothesis. They want to know what to do on Tuesday. The honest description fragments into advice that sounds like authority without actually having it.

GitLab's stewardship handbook does not describe governance as hypothesis. It documents boundaries: here is what the corporation controls, here is what the community controls, here is how conflicts are resolved. The patterns are embedded in operational reality, not offered as possibilities. Wikipedia's Good Article process demonstrates that pattern implementation requires structural scaffolding — not just pattern recognition. ArbCom demonstrates that the governance scaffolding around patterns is more predictive of success than the patterns themselves.

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if a hypothesis that never becomes a commitment is just procrastination with footnotes — and the difference between a pattern that works and one that does not is not the pattern, but the scaffolding around it?"*

### Empirical Evidence

**Resilience Cases (Confirming):**
- [GitLab Stewardship Handbook](https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/) — Explicit documentation of governance boundaries between corporate sponsor and community reduces friction in open-core projects. Patterns embedded in operational boundaries work better than patterns offered as hypotheses.
- [Wikipedia Good Article Process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Good_articles) — Peer assessment with transparent criteria and independent reviewers creates sustainable quality tiers. Pattern implementation requires structural scaffolding — not just pattern recognition.

**Fracture Cases (Complicating):**
- [Drupal Governance](https://www.drupal.org/governance) — Intentional governance transition from BDFL to community model required years of structural work. Even successful pattern changes require intentional governance work — patterns as hypotheses do not self-implement.
- [Wikipedia Arbitration Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arbitration_Committee_(Wikimedia)) — Permanent records of community conflicts that consensus failed to resolve. The governance scaffolding around patterns is more predictive of success than the patterns themselves.

### Operational Heuristics

**Positive Boundary:** Patterns work when governance boundaries are explicit and evaluation criteria are transparent — documented governance structures create sustainable pattern implementation across different contexts.

**Negative Boundary:** Do not apply patterns without governance scaffolding — even successful pattern changes require intentional structural work; organic emergence fails when coordination complexity exceeds informal relationships.

### Epistemic Synthesis

The difference between a pattern that works and one that does not is not the pattern — it is the scaffolding around it. GitLab does not propose stewardship as a hypothesis — it documents operational boundaries. Drupal did not change pattern by consensus — it worked for years on structural transitions. Patterns as hypotheses are intellectually honest but operationally useless. What groups seek are not possibilities — they are boundaries within which to operate.
