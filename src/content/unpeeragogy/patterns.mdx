### Reality

The handbook promises patterns as a practical guide — look them over, return as needed, let theory meet practice. I've watched teams clutch these patterns like recipes, expecting governance frameworks to self-implement and peer assessment to emerge from good intentions. It doesn't work that way.

Not templates. Not blueprints. Not step-by-step guides that guarantee results.

The honest part: patterns describe what worked somewhere else, under specific conditions, with particular people. They capture the shape of a solution, not its substance. When a corporate sponsor documents governance boundaries with their open-source community, they're not following a pattern — they're doing the human work of negotiating power, trust, and control. The documentation is just what remains visible afterward.

> What if treating patterns as hypotheses means accepting that most of our careful planning will need to be abandoned?

### Evidence That Is Ambiguous

**en.wikipedia.org** -- The Wikipedia Arbitration Committee (ArbCom) case in 2021 highlighted conflicts in community governance, where differing views on content moderation led to heated debates. The theoretical pattern posits that shared governance leads to consensus, yet this case illustrates how diverse opinions can complicate decision-making processes, resulting in protracted disputes.


### Synthesis

These cases suggest something the handbook doesn't quite say: patterns are diagnostic tools, not prescriptive ones. They help you recognize what's happening in your project, not dictate what should happen next. The governance transition from BDFL to community model takes years of structural work precisely because it's not about applying a pattern — it's about dismantling one person's accumulated decision-making power and redistributing it without breaking what already functions.

The useful friction lies in this gap between recognition and implementation. You can spot the pattern of peer assessment with transparent criteria, but creating those criteria requires negotiating what quality means to your specific community. The pattern shows you the shape; the work remains yours to do.
