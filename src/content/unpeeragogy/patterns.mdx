### Il Dogma

The Peeragogy Handbook describes patterns as hypotheses rather than guaranteed solutions, acknowledging they may fail when context or people change, yet users seek prescriptive guidance rather than descriptive frameworks.

### La Realtà

Drupal spent years transitioning from BDFL to community governance. Not because the BDFL pattern was wrong. Not because the community governance pattern was right. Because no pattern survives first contact with organizational legacy without structural work.

The handbook describes patterns as hypotheses. This is intellectually honest — patterns work in some contexts, fail in others, and evolve with use. But groups seeking guidance don't want a hypothesis. They want to know what to do on Tuesday. The honest description fragments into advice that sounds like authority without actually having it.

GitLab's stewardship handbook doesn't describe governance as hypothesis. It documents boundaries: here's what the corporation controls, here's what the community controls, here's how conflicts are resolved. The patterns are embedded in operational reality, not offered as possibilities.

### Perturbatore

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if a hypothesis that never becomes a commitment is just procrastination with footnotes — and the difference between a pattern that works and one that doesn't isn't the pattern, but the scaffolding around it?"*

### Evidenze di Tenuta

**about.gitlab.com** — Explicit documentation of governance boundaries between corporate sponsor and community reduces friction in open-core projects. GitLab shows that patterns embedded in operational boundaries work better than patterns offered as hypotheses.

**en.wikipedia.org** — Peer assessment with transparent criteria and independent reviewers creates sustainable quality tiers. Wikipedia's Good Article process demonstrates that pattern implementation requires structural scaffolding — not just pattern recognition.

### Evidenze di Faglia

**drupal.org** — Intentional governance transition from BDFL to community model required years of structural work. Drupal shows that even successful pattern changes require intentional governance work — patterns as hypotheses don't self-implement.

**en.wikipedia.org** — Wikipedia's Arbitration Committee publishes permanent records of community conflicts. ArbCom demonstrates that the governance scaffolding around patterns is more predictive of success than the patterns themselves.

### Euristiche Operative

**Regola Positiva:** I pattern funzionano quando i confini di governance sono espliciti e i criteri di valutazione sono trasparenti. GitLab e Wikipedia mostrano che strutture di governance documentate creano implementazione sostenibile dei pattern attraverso contesti diversi.

**Regola Negativa:** Non applicare pattern senza scaffolding di governance. Drupal mostra che anche cambiamenti di pattern riusciti richiedono lavoro strutturale intenzionale — l'emergenza organica fallisce quando la complessità di coordinamento supera le relazioni informali.

### Sintesi & Tracciabilità

**Riferimento:** audit-v2 (delta tensione: 1.4 → 1.3533, −0.0467)
**Evidenze esaminate:** 4 (2 confermanti, 2 complicanti)
**Stato:** PROVVISORIO

La differenza tra un pattern che funziona e uno che non funziona non è il pattern — è lo scaffolding intorno ad esso. GitLab non propone la stewardship come ipotesi — documenta confini operativi. Drupal non ha cambiato pattern per consenso — ha lavorato per anni a transizioni strutturali. I pattern come ipotesi sono onesti intellettualmente ma inutili operativamente. Quello che i gruppi cercano non sono possibilità — sono confini entro cui operare.
