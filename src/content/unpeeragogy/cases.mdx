### The Assumption

Case studies demonstrate the viability of peer learning. Concrete examples of how peer learning and cooperation manifest in practice prove that the model works and can be replicated.

### Operational Reality

The Peeragogy handbook lists several case studies. Some describe projects that no longer exist. Some present idealized versions of messy realities. One admits failure but frames it as learning. The meta-case — "Peeragogy in Action" — is about writing case studies.

The selection is not accidental. It reflects a structural problem: peer learning projects are fragile, and the ones that survive long enough to be documented are the ones that had institutional scaffolding — defined roles, formal governance, external partnerships — that the case study format tends to treat as background instead of as the main mechanism.

GitLab's stewardship documentation shows that explicit governance boundaries between sponsor and community reduce friction. P2PU's learning circles show that defined facilitator roles and institutional partnerships make peer learning sustainable. These cases are more useful than success narratives because they document the scaffolding, not just the outcome. Drupal's governance transition shows that peer governance does not emerge naturally — it requires years of structural work. P2PU's governance challenges show that credentialing and quality assurance crises are structural, not motivational.

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if case studies are where peer learning theories go to hide from reality?"*

### Empirical Evidence

**Resilience Cases (Confirming):**
- [GitLab Stewardship Handbook](https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/) — Explicit documentation of governance boundaries reduces friction. Honest case studies of governance design provide more useful patterns than idealized narratives.
- [P2PU Learning Circles](https://www.p2pu.org/en/learning-circles/) — Peer learning with defined facilitator roles and institutional partnerships is more sustainable. Cases documenting structural scaffolding are more actionable than success narratives.

**Fracture Cases (Complicating):**
- [Drupal Governance](https://www.drupal.org/governance) — Governance transition from BDFL to community model required years of structural work. Drupal contradicts the assumption that peer governance emerges naturally.
- [P2PU About](https://www.p2pu.org/en/about/) — Peer learning communities face credentialing and quality assurance crises that require hierarchy or external validation. Honest cases reveal structural tensions that success narratives hide.

### Operational Heuristics

**Positive Boundary:** Use case studies when they explicitly document governance boundaries and facilitator roles — cases showing institutional scaffolding are more useful than cases celebrating success.

**Negative Boundary:** Do not use case studies to prove that peer learning is naturally self-sustaining — cases that hide structural requirements are misinformation, not evidence.

### Epistemic Synthesis

The assumption that cases demonstrate model validity is contradicted by the evidence. Cases in the handbook tend to edit out structural tension. When cases are honest — like GitLab and Drupal — they reveal that peer learning requires explicitly designed structures, not organic emergence. Theory without cases is philosophy. Cases without theory is journalism.
