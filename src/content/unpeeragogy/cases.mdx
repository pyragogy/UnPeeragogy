The Case Studies page is a list with one link: SWATS.

One case study. One example. For an entire handbook of patterns.

**Why it matters:** A case study doesn't prove a pattern works. It proves it was applied once, by someone, in a specific context. Pattern science is built on "it always works" — but the evidence is "it worked once." Case studies are the weakest evidence that exists. And the Handbook has exactly one.

The Handbook is full of patterns. Nearly empty of concrete application examples. Why? Because documenting failure is easier than documenting success? Or because patterns are rarely applied in real life? The lack of case studies is evidence that peeragogy is more theory than practice. If it really worked, there would be dozens of examples. Instead there's SWATS — a technology framework, not a peer learning case.

**What to do instead:** Every pattern should have at least one associated case study. If it doesn't, it's a hypothesis, not a pattern. Hypotheses are beautiful. Facts are better. Put more facts in the Handbook.

> **Perturbator:** *"One case study. Think about it. A pattern handbook with a single application example. Doesn't that tell you something? That patterns are easier to write than to apply. That theory is more abundant than practice. That maybe patterns don't work as promised. Or maybe they work but no one wants to write about it. Either way, it's a problem."*

**Reference:** Peeragogy Handbook, "Case Studies" section.
