## Summary

Case studies document success but hide the scaffolding that made it possible. Theory says peer models self-organize — practice shows they need explicit boundaries and institutional backing. The gap between documented pattern and operational reality is where most implementations fail.

## Evidenze Grounded

### 🔹 Confirming (2)

**Caso 1 — GitLab stewardship handbook**
- **Fenomeno:** Explicit documentation of governance boundaries between corporate sponsor and community reduces friction in open-core projects.
- **Fonte:** [https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/](https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/)

**Caso 2 — P2PU learning circles sustainability**
- **Fenomeno:** Peer learning models with defined facilitator roles and institutional partnerships are more sustainable than purely peer-driven learning.
- **Fonte:** [https://www.p2pu.org/en/learning-circles/](https://www.p2pu.org/en/learning-circles/)

### 🔸 Complicating (2)

**Caso 1 — Drupal governance / community working groups**
- **Fenomeno:** Intentional governance transition from BDFL to community model requires years of structural work, not organic emergence.
- **Fonte:** [https://www.drupal.org/governance](https://www.drupal.org/governance)

**Caso 2 — P2P University governance challenges**
- **Fenomeno:** Peer learning communities inevitably face credentialing and quality assurance crises that require either hierarchy or external validation frameworks.
- **Fonte:** [https://www.p2pu.org/en/about/](https://www.p2pu.org/en/about/)

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern works when governance boundaries are explicitly documented and facilitator roles are clearly defined. GitLab's stewardship handbook and P2PU's learning circles show that peer models succeed when structural prerequisites are made visible and institutional partnerships provide external validation frameworks.

### Negative Rule

> Do not treat peer patterns as naturally self-sustaining without institutional scaffolding. Drupal's governance transition required years of structural work, and P2P University faced credentialing crises that demanded hierarchical intervention, showing that peer models inevitably encounter legitimacy and coordination challenges that cannot be resolved through peer mechanisms alone.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern claims peer learning naturally emerges from good intentions and shared purpose. Partial verification — when structural prerequisites exist. GitLab's stewardship handbook and P2PU's learning circles work because governance boundaries are explicit and facilitator roles are defined, while Drupal's governance transition required years of structural work and P2P University hit credentialing crises that demanded hierarchical intervention. The real mechanism isn't peer dynamics but the institutional scaffolding that remains invisible in most case documentation. Case studies become false stories when they edit out the boring infrastructure work.
