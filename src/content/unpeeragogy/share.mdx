## Summary

Sharing without structure stalls. Theory says peer networks self-organize — practice shows they need facilitators, governance, or institutional backing. The difference between resource pooling and collective creation is intentional design.

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern accurately predicts failure when sharing lacks governance structure or role definition. Drupal's governance transition shows that intentional structural work, not organic emergence, is required to move from resource sharing to collective creation.

### Negative Rule

> Do not apply this pattern when sharing includes defined facilitator roles or institutional partnerships. P2PU learning circles show that structured peer learning with facilitator roles and institutional support achieves sustainability, not stagnation.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern claims unstructured sharing leads to stagnation while structured approaches enable creation. Partial verification — it works when you define the distinction clearly. P2PU learning circles succeed with facilitator roles and institutional partnerships, while Drupal's governance transition required years of structural work, not organic emergence. WebAssembly's lighter governance trades authority for speed, complicating the simple sharing-versus-creating binary. What's missing is the mechanism that transforms sharing into creation — the specific organizational technologies that bridge resource pooling and collective work. The real question isn't whether to share or create. It's whether you're willing to design the transition.
