### The Assumption

Sharing is a first step but does not lead to creation. Groups that only share resources do not produce anything new, creating a library-like environment instead of a laboratory.

### Operational Reality

A teachers' group shares worksheets religiously. Google Drive full of lesson plans. Shared folders organized by grade, subject, standard. Two hundred teachers contributing. Zero new curricula produced.

The pattern correctly identifies the failure: sharing without structure is curation, not creation. But it misses the enabling conditions. P2PU's learning circles show that sharing with defined facilitator roles and institutional partnerships produces sustainable learning — not because sharing is replaced, but because sharing is structured. Drupal's governance transition required intentional structural work to move from sharing resources to creating them together. WebAssembly shows that lightweight governance creates the sharing-without-creation trap.

The problem is not sharing. It is sharing without a plan for what happens next.

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if the problem isn't sharing — it's sharing without a plan — and sharing with defined facilitator roles and institutional partnerships produces creation?"*

### Empirical Evidence

**Resilience Cases (Confirming):**
- [P2PU Learning Circles](https://www.p2pu.org/en/learning-circles/) — Peer learning models with defined facilitator roles and institutional partnerships are more sustainable than purely peer-driven sharing. Structured sharing produces creation.

**Fracture Cases (Complicating):**
- [Drupal Governance](https://www.drupal.org/governance) — Intentional governance transition from BDFL to community model required years of structural work. Moving from sharing to creation requires intentional governance work.
- [WebAssembly Community Group](https://www.w3.org/community/webassembly/) — Choosing lighter governance models for standards development trades authority for speed, creating different failure modes. Lightweight governance creates the sharing-without-creation trap.

### Operational Heuristics

**Positive Boundary:** The pattern accurately predicts failure when sharing lacks governance structure or role definition — intentional structural work, not organic emergence, is required to move from sharing to creation.

**Negative Boundary:** Do not apply this pattern when sharing includes defined facilitator roles or institutional partnerships — structured peer learning with facilitation roles and partnerships produces sustainable creation.

### Epistemic Synthesis

The pattern correctly identifies the failure: sharing without structure is curation, not creation. But it misses the enabling conditions. P2PU shows that sharing with defined facilitator roles produces sustainable learning. Drupal shows that moving from sharing to creation requires intentional structural work. The problem is not sharing. It is sharing without a plan for what happens next.
