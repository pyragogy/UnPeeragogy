## Summary

Lurkers aren't broken participants — your governance documentation is broken. Theory says co-learning tolerates messiness and partial participation. Practice shows lurkers become productive when boundaries are explicit, not when you fix the lurkers.

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern works when governance boundaries are explicitly documented and activity is summarized by third parties. GitLab's stewardship handbook and LWN.net's kernel summaries show that explicit governance documentation enables lurkers to participate productively through distributed monitoring and awareness.

### Negative Rule

> Do not treat lurking as inherently problematic when governance documentation is missing. Wikipedia's deletion debates show that pushing for participation without explicit governance boundaries creates de facto hierarchies where consistent participants gain disproportionate influence, excluding rather than including lurkers.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern claims lurking is normal and productive in peer learning environments. Partial verification — this works when governance boundaries exist in writing. GitLab's stewardship handbook and LWN's kernel summaries prove that documented boundaries let lurkers convert observation into distributed monitoring. But WebAssembly's lighter governance and Wikipedia's deletion wars show the mechanism breaks when authority remains implicit. The real engine isn't tolerance for messiness — it's making power visible. Lurkers need maps, not patience.
