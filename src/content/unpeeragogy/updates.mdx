## Summary

Updates concentrate power through curation. Theory says transparency — practice creates editorial gatekeepers. The weekly summary becomes the weekly agenda.

## Evidenze Grounded

### 🔹 Confirming (2)

**Caso 1 — GitLab stewardship handbook**
- **Fenomeno:** Explicit documentation of governance boundaries between corporate sponsor and community reduces friction in open-core projects.
- **Fonte:** [https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/](https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/)

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> Updates work as health indicators when governance boundaries are explicit and update processes are structured. GitLab's stewardship handbook and KDE's Community Council show that transparent governance frameworks with defined authority prevent updates from becoming covert power concentration mechanisms.

### Negative Rule

> Do not implement regular updates without distributing agenda-setting power. Debian's DPL newsletter shows that centralized update authorship creates hidden editorial control where what gets summarized becomes what gets community attention, undermining peer governance.

## Sintesi Obliqua

Structured updates claim to distribute information equally across peer networks. This works when governance boundaries are explicit — GitLab's stewardship handbook and KDE's Community Council prove that defined authority prevents update processes from becoming covert control mechanisms. But Debian's DPL newsletter shows how centralized authorship creates hidden editorial power where summary becomes agenda-setting. The missing piece isn't process design. It's recognizing that every update is an argument about what matters.
