### The Assumption

Regular updates in peer learning projects serve as health indicators and engagement mechanisms, with update frequency correlating to project vitality and community participation.

### Operational Reality

Your weekly standup is not transparency. It is editorial control disguised as process.

GitLab's stewardship handbook and KDE's Community Council show that transparent governance structures make updates trustworthy by distributing agenda-setting power. But Debian's DPL newsletter reveals the hidden cost: centralized update authorship creates editorial control where what gets summarized defines what gets done. WebAssembly's Community Group shows that updates without governance become performance — a ritual of transparency that obscures more than it reveals.

The pattern assumes update frequency correlates with project health. The evidence shows that update governance — who decides what gets reported — correlates with project health far more strongly.

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if your weekly standup isn't transparency — it's editorial control disguised as process — and update governance matters more than update frequency?"*

### Empirical Evidence

**Resilience Cases (Confirming):**
- [GitLab Stewardship Handbook](https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/) — Explicit documentation of governance boundaries reduces friction. Transparent governance structures make updates trustworthy.
- [KDE Community Council](https://community.kde.org/Community_Council) — Distributing agenda-setting power through transparent governance makes updates reliable health indicators.

**Fracture Cases (Complicating):**
- [Debian DPL Bits Newsletter](https://lists.debian.org/debian-devel-announce/) — Centralized update authorship creates hidden editorial control where what gets summarized defines what gets done.
- [WebAssembly Community Group](https://www.w3.org/community/webassembly/) — Updates without governance become performance — ritual transparency that obscures more than it reveals.

### Operational Heuristics

**Positive Boundary:** Updates work as health indicators when governance boundaries are explicit and update processes are structured — transparent governance and distributed agenda-setting power make updates reliable.

**Negative Boundary:** Do not implement regular updates without distributing agenda-setting power — centralized update authorship creates hidden editorial control where what gets summarized defines what gets done.

### Epistemic Synthesis

Update frequency does not correlate with project health. Update governance — who decides what gets reported — does. GitLab and KDE show that transparent governance structures make updates trustworthy by distributing agenda-setting power. Debian shows that centralized update authorship creates hidden editorial control. Your weekly standup is not transparency. It is editorial control disguised as process.
