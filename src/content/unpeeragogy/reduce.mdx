## Summary

Reduction requires authority before it requires motivation. Theory says eliminate waste first, build later. Practice does the opposite — establish who decides what counts as waste, then maybe you can throw things away.

## Evidenze Grounded

### 🔹 Confirming (2)

**Caso 1 — GitLab stewardship handbook**
- **Fenomeno:** Explicit documentation of governance boundaries between corporate sponsor and community reduces friction in open-core projects.
- **Fonte:** [https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/](https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/)

**Caso 2 — Wikipedia Good Article process**
- **Fenomeno:** Peer assessment with transparent criteria and independent reviewers creates sustainable quality tiers in open communities.
- **Fonte:** [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Good_articles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Good_articles)

### 🔸 Complicating (2)

**Caso 1 — Drupal governance / community working groups**
- **Fenomeno:** Intentional governance transition from BDFL to community model requires years of structural work, not organic emergence.
- **Fonte:** [https://www.drupal.org/governance](https://www.drupal.org/governance)

**Caso 2 — Apache Software Foundation governance model**
- **Fenomeno:** Meritocracy-based peer governance creates invisible barriers: those with more free time accumulate authority, creating a class system within the 'flat' organization.
- **Fonte:** [https://www.apache.org/foundation/how-it-works.html](https://www.apache.org/foundation/how-it-works.html)

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern works when transparent criteria and independent review processes are established before identifying waste. GitLab's stewardship handbook and Wikipedia's Good Article process demonstrate that explicit boundaries between roles and transparent quality criteria create sustainable simplification mechanisms.

### Negative Rule

> Do not apply this pattern without explicit governance frameworks for decision-making authority. Drupal's governance transition and Apache's meritocracy model show that reduction attempts without clear authority structures create invisible class systems where those with more time accumulate power over what gets eliminated.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern assumes reduction is a technical problem when it's a political one. Partial verification — but only when governance precedes optimization. GitLab's stewardship handbook and Wikipedia's Good Article process work because they define authority before defining waste, while Drupal's governance transition and Apache's meritocracy show how reduction without clear decision rights creates invisible hierarchies that resist simplification. The real mechanism isn't identifying redundancy. Authority to act on what you find.
