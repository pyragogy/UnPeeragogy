### The Assumption

Groups should eliminate waste by identifying and removing unnecessary complexity, redundant processes, and accumulated mistakes. Simplification is inherently beneficial and groups can recognize and act on opportunities to streamline their operations.

### Operational Reality

Telling people to "just simplify" is like telling them to "just be happy" — the advice assumes the problem is motivation, not structure.

Every group accumulates complexity. The question is not whether to reduce — it is who has the authority to decide what counts as waste. GitLab's stewardship handbook documents explicit boundaries that enable reduction without contestation. Wikipedia's Good Article process uses transparent criteria that make reduction decisions reviewable and legitimate. But Drupal's governance transition and Apache's meritocracy show that reduction without clear decision-making authority produces contestation, not simplification.

The pattern assumes waste is objectively identifiable. It is not. One person's waste is another person's essential infrastructure. Reduction is a political act disguised as housekeeping.

### Empirical Evidence

**Resilience Cases (Confirming):**
- [GitLab Stewardship Handbook](https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/) — Explicit documentation of governance boundaries between corporate sponsor and community reduces friction in open-core projects. Explicit boundaries enable reduction decisions without contestation.
- [Wikipedia Good Article Process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Good_articles) — Peer assessment with transparent criteria and independent reviewers creates sustainable quality tiers. Transparent criteria make reduction decisions reviewable and legitimate.

**Fracture Cases (Complicating):**
- [Drupal Governance](https://www.drupal.org/governance) — Intentional governance transition from BDFL to community model required years of structural work. Reduction without clear authority produces contestation, not simplification.
- [Apache Foundation How It Works](https://www.apache.org/foundation/how-it-works.html) — Meritocracy-based systems make reduction decisions without broad consensus, creating tension between efficiency and inclusion.

### Operational Heuristics

**Positive Boundary:** Reduction works when transparent criteria and independent review processes are established before waste identification begins — explicit boundaries and transparent criteria make reduction reviewable and legitimate.

**Negative Boundary:** Do not apply this pattern without explicit governance frameworks for decision-making authority — reduction attempts without clear authority produce contestation, not simplification.
