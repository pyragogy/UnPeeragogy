"A facilitator is not necessarily an authority. Rather, facilitation work is done in service of the group." "A facilitator can simply 'hold space' for the group."

Co-facilitation is one of the noblest patterns in the Handbook. Two people sharing the load of keeping the group alive.

**Why it matters:** Holding space is work. It's not simple. It's not automatic. It requires attention, preparation, energy. And when done well, no one notices. The group works, discussions flow, decisions get made. The facilitator worked. But the work is invisible.

The problem with co-facilitation is that it distributes invisible work between two people. It makes them less alone. But it doesn't make the work more visible. The group still doesn't see how much it costs to hold space. And when the facilitators get tired — and they will — the group doesn't know what to do. Because it never learned to facilitate. It always delegated.

**What to do instead:** Co-facilitation works if it's temporary and rotating. If the same two people facilitate for six months, you don't have co-facilitation. You have two people doing everything and ten people participating. Real co-facilitation is when everyone knows how to facilitate. Not when two people do it well.

> **Perturbator:** *"You have two facilitators. Good ones. They hold space, prepare the agenda, manage conflicts. And the rest of the group? Watching. Co-facilitation isn't a model of power-sharing. It's a model of work-sharing. The power stays with the facilitators. The work too. The only difference is now there are two people carrying the weight. The weight is still on the same shoulders."*
