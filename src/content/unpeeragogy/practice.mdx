## Summary

Peer groups dissolve into discussion circles without governance scaffolding. Theory promises emergent organization — practice demands explicit authority boundaries first. GitLab's stewardship handbook works because someone decided who decides what.

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern works when governance boundaries and responsibility assignment mechanisms are documented before practice begins. GitLab's stewardship handbook shows that explicit structural scaffolding enables sustainable community action rather than endless discussion.

### Negative Rule

> Do not apply this pattern expecting practice to emerge organically from enthusiasm and pattern awareness. The vault incidents show this produces knowledge-hoarding and decision-stagnation because no one has clear authority to move from discussion to action.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern assumes enthusiasm generates sustainable practice through pattern awareness alone. Partial verification: GitLab's explicit governance boundaries do reduce friction in open-core projects, proving structure enables action. But Drupal's years-long transition from BDFL to community governance and Wikipedia's consensus processes producing de facto hierarchies show that sustainable peer practice requires designed authority mechanisms, not organic emergence. The real mechanism missing: someone has to accept responsibility for moving from discussion to decision. Peers want equality — someone still has to close the meeting.
