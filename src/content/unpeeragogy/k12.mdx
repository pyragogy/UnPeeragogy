### Il Dogma

Peeragogy in K12 settings is nearly impossible due to compulsory attendance and fixed curriculum, with teachers maintaining control that hinders peer learning. The pattern assumes institutional authority is fundamentally incompatible with peer-to-peer learning dynamics, and that peer methods can transform the classroom if given space.

### La Realtà

Mrs. Chen locks her classroom door at 3:15 PM and drives home with a stack of papers to grade. She's been teaching fourth grade for twelve years. The district mandated a new reading curriculum this fall — the third change in five years. She attended the required training, nodded through the PowerPoint about "collaborative learning environments," then closed her door and taught the way that works for her kids. Not because she's stubborn. Because she's earned the right to know what holds.

The peeragogy handbook celebrates teachers who "generously share resources with one another." True enough. Teachers swap worksheets, trade classroom management tips, pass along which substitute actually shows up. But this isn't peeragogy — it's survival. The sharing happens despite the system, not because of it. When your evaluation depends on test scores and your principal changes every two years, you don't experiment with peer learning circles. You protect what works.

The friction isn't accidental.

### Perturbatore

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if you can't peer-learn your way out of a report card — and the isolation isn't the bug but the feature, the only space left where teaching can happen without measurement?"*

### Evidenze di Tenuta

**about.gitlab.com** — Explicit documentation of governance boundaries between corporate sponsor and community reduces friction in open-core projects. GitLab shows that clear boundaries between authority and community can create protected peer spaces even within hierarchical structures.

### Evidenze di Faglia

**drupal.org** — Intentional governance transition from BDFL to community model required years of structural work, not organic emergence. Drupal shows that moving from institutional authority to peer governance requires structural redesign, not just permission.

**arxiv.org** — Peer assessment systems in scholarly communication require endorsement hierarchies that contradict the egalitarian ideal. arXiv demonstrates that even the most peer-driven systems need validation mechanisms that look like authority.

### Euristiche Operative

**Regola Positiva:** Applica questo pattern quando i confini di governance sono documentati esplicitamente e gli spazi peer sono ritagliati dentro — non contro — i framework istituzionali. GitLab mostra che confini chiari tra autorità e comunità riducono l'attrito.

**Regola Negativa:** Non tentare il peeragogy in contesti K12 senza negoziare esplicitamente i confini con l'autorità istituzionale. L'assunzione del pattern che autorità e peer learning siano incompatibili porta a tentativi irrealistici che consumano energia senza produrre cambiamento sostenibile.

**Stato:** PROVVISORIO

L'insegnante che condivide risorse ma rifiuta l'osservazione tra pari non sta contraddicendo il peer learning — sta distinguendo strategicamente tra ciò che può essere condiviso (un foglio di lavoro) e ciò che deve essere protetto (il ritmo dell'insegnamento reale). La visione del manuale di educatori collaborativi assume un contesto che non esiste: tempo, fiducia e libertà dal giudizio esterno. L'attrito non è accidentale — è la risposta razionale a un sistema dove l'accountability individuale e l'innovazione collaborativa sono in competizione strutturale.
