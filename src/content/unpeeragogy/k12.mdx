## Summary

Teachers share resources but resist peer governance. The pattern assumes authority blocks learning — practice shows boundaries enable it. When institutions document what they control versus what peers decide, friction drops.

## Euristiche Operative

### Positive Rule

> This pattern works when governance boundaries are explicitly documented and peer spaces are carved out within, not against, institutional frameworks. GitLab's stewardship handbook shows that clear boundaries between authority and community reduce friction rather than eliminate hierarchy.

### Negative Rule

> Do not attempt peeragogy in K12 settings without explicitly negotiating boundaries with institutional authority. The pattern's assumption that authority and peer learning are incompatible leads to underground resistance or performative compliance when structural realities are ignored.

## Sintesi Obliqua

The pattern claims institutional authority kills peer learning in K12 settings. Partial verification — but conditional on how boundaries get drawn. GitLab's stewardship handbook proves explicit governance reduces friction rather than eliminates hierarchy, while Drupal's transition from BDFL shows peer structures need years of intentional design, not organic emergence. arXiv's endorsement system demonstrates that peer assessment requires institutional scaffolding to function. The mechanism isn't authority versus peers — it's undocumented versus explicit boundaries. You can't peer-learn around a system that won't name what it controls.
