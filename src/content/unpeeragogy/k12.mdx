"K12 is one place where peeragogy is very relevant." "An open question: can science be learned through peeragogy in a K12 context?"

The theory says K12 is relevant. The practice asks: can it actually work in schools? And the answer is: almost never.

**Why it matters:** K12 has features that make peeragogy nearly impossible: compulsory attendance, hierarchy of grades, fixed curriculum, external evaluation. Peeragogy in K12 is like gardening in a parking lot. It can be done, but the environment is fundamentally hostile.

The problem isn't the method. It's the container. Schools are built to produce specific outcomes (grades, skills, certification). Peeragogy produces emergent outcomes (knowledge, relationships, autonomy). The two systems conflict at every level.

**What to do instead:** If you want peeragogy in K12, work outside the school. In extracurricular groups, informal projects, summer experiences. Inside the school, peeragogy is decoration. It makes the day nicer. It doesn't change the system. Honest peeragogy in K12 is called "the club after school." Not "the class during school hours."

> **Perturbator:** *"Peeragogy in K12 works if the teacher is willing to give up control. How many K12 teachers are willing to give up control? Exactly. Peeragogy in K12 isn't a pedagogical proposition. It's a power negotiation. And the teacher always wins. The system designed it that way."*
