"After the Handbook, what's next for peeragogy?" "To be continued."

"What's Next" asks where the project is going. It's open. It's incomplete.

**Why it matters:** "What's Next" is always the most honest section. It reveals whether the project has momentum. If the section says "we plan to..." and the plans are specific, the project is alive. If the section says "to be continued" without details, the project is paused.

The Handbook's "What's Next" is vague. It mentions possible directions. None committed. The section reflects the project's state: possibilities without decisions. A future that's open because the present hasn't been closed.

**What to do instead:** "What's Next" should have at least one concrete commitment. "We will publish three case studies by June." Not "we might publish case studies." Commitment is what separates active projects from passive ones. The Handbook's "What's Next" is passive. It describes a future that might happen. Not a future that will happen.

> **Perturbator:** *"'What's Next' is a section full of possibilities. 'We might do this. We might do that.' Might. The future of peeragogy is a list of options. No decisions. A future without decisions is a future that never arrives. It stays a possibility. Forever."*
