### The Assumption

Future direction statements for the Peeragogy Handbook — outlining planned updates, community growth targets, and resource allocation — presented as transparent roadmap and collective ambition.

### Operational Reality

"Next steps" is what people write when they do not know what to do next.

GitLab's stewardship handbook shows that explicit governance boundaries produce sustainable direction — not "next steps" documents. Fedora FESCo demonstrates that elected roles with defined scope enable transparent roadmap decisions. But Debian's DPL newsletter shows that direction documents without governance produce statements too vague to follow or contest. Wikipedia's ArbCom shows that even transparent communities fracture when future direction is contested without binding authority.

The pattern is not wrong. It is premature. The direction is not the problem — the missing governance that would make direction meaningful is the problem.

> **Perturbatore:** *"What if 'next steps' is what people write when they don't know what to do next — and direction documents without governance produce statements too vague to follow or contest?"*

### Empirical Evidence

**Resilience Cases (Confirming):**
- [GitLab Stewardship Handbook](https://about.gitlab.com/handbook/company/stewardship/) — Explicit documentation of governance boundaries between corporate sponsor and community reduces friction. Governance boundaries produce sustainable direction more effectively than direction documents.

**Fracture Cases (Complicating):**
- [Debian DPL Bits Newsletter](https://lists.debian.org/debian-devel-announce/) — Direction documents without governance produce statements too vague to follow or contest.
- [Wikipedia Arbitration Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arbitration_Committee_(Wikimedia)) — Even transparent communities fracture when future direction is contested without binding authority.

### Operational Heuristics

**Positive Boundary:** Future direction works when governance boundaries are documented explicitly and authority roles are defined before strategic planning — elected technical roles with defined scope enable transparent roadmap decisions.

**Negative Boundary:** Do not rely on "next steps" documents as a governance mechanism — direction documents without governance produce statements too vague to follow or contest.

### Epistemic Synthesis

"Next steps" is what people write when they do not know what to do next. GitLab shows that governance boundaries produce sustainable direction. Fedora shows that elected roles with defined scope enable transparent roadmap decisions. The pattern is not wrong. It is premature. The direction is not the problem — the missing governance that would make direction meaningful is the problem.
